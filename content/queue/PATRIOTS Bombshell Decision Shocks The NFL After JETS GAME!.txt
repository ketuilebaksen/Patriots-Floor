Let’s break down the game itself, because it was a masterpiece from start to finish.

The Patriots scored touchdowns on their first two drives. Right out of the gate. Maye hit DeMario “Pop” Douglas for a beautiful 35-yard score. Then Rhamondre Stevenson punched one in from five yards out.

Kirk Cousins and the Raiders answered once, with a Brock Bowers touchdown. And honestly? That was basically their only real moment of the afternoon. Remember, all week everybody was worried about Bowers with the Patriots missing four corners. He got his one touchdown. That was it.

Before halftime, Maye found Hunter Henry for an 11-yard touchdown to make it 21 to 7.

And then the third quarter turned into an absolute party.

Kyle Williams took a 34-yard dime from Maye all the way to the house. Then Maye himself scrambled in from three yards out for the rushing touchdown. And Douglas added another receiving touchdown later in the game.

45 to 7. And it never once felt that close.

Maye spread the ball around beautifully, with completions to six different targets. Douglas hauled in two scores. Williams made highlight-reel catches that had the crowd losing its mind. And Henry kept being that reliable safety valve over the middle, the guy Maye can always count on.

And here’s what makes it so impressive. A.J. Brown was sidelined. Mack Hollins was on injured reserve. Two of the receivers this team was counting on weren’t even on the field. And the supporting cast didn’t just survive. It stepped up massively.

The offensive line, even after losing left tackle Will Campbell to a knee injury early in the game, kept Maye clean enough for him to cook all afternoon. That’s a credit to rookie Caleb Lomu, who came in for Campbell and allowed just one pressure on 29 pass-blocking snaps. And as a team, the Patriots held Maxx Crosby without a sack.

On defense, the unit adjusted without Gonzalez and Davis and still got the job done. Kevin Byard was mic’d up and locked in the entire game, you could see the leadership. And the edge group kept up its strong start to the season.

The Raiders, who had looked so promising coming in, were completely exposed. Rookie quarterback Fernando Mendoza even got some garbage-time snaps in his NFL debut once the game was well out of hand.

This wasn’t just a win. It was proof that this offense has found its rhythm, and that the next-man-up culture inside that building is very, very real.

Now here’s the thing about this team. Through five games, we’ve seen two completely different versions of it.

The first three weeks were a struggle. Low scoring. Turnovers. Questions flying in from every direction. Maye’s touchdown-to-interception ratio early on was rough.

The last two weeks? A league-best offensive output, averaging around 37 points per game. And Maye has seven touchdowns and just one interception in that stretch.

That’s not a small adjustment. That’s a different quarterback. And it’s a different offense around him.

And the depth is carrying them right now. Look at the wide receiver room.

DeMario Douglas is playing with real confidence and real production. Two touchdowns against the Raiders.

Kyle Williams is making explosive plays down the field. The kind of plays that change the shape of a defense.

Efton Chism III is earning more opportunities and looking like a real contributor.

And Romeo Doubs has been steady every single week. Not flashy, just reliable. The guy you need on third down.

Now imagine adding A.J. Brown to that group.

And that’s the bombshell decision. Vrabel said there’s a chance Brown could be activated as early as this week against the Jets, after rehabbing that high ankle sprain. If that happens, this receiver room goes from good to scary good.

Think about the problems that creates for a defense. You can’t double Brown without leaving Douglas or Williams one-on-one. You can’t sit back in deep coverage without giving Henry the middle. You can’t load up against the pass without Stevenson punishing you on the ground. There’s no right answer. And Maye, with the way he’s throwing the ball right now, is going to find whatever you leave open.

But Vrabel is being careful. And he should be. He’s said he wants to see where Brown’s conditioning is, and how he responds after practice. He even said Brown has looked okay, with some good plays and some bad plays, just like everybody. That’s an honest coach. He’s not going to throw Brown out there just because the fans want him. If Brown plays, it’ll be because he’s ready.

And it’s not just the receivers. Look at the edge rushers.

The trio of Draymond Jones, Elijah Ponder, and rookie Gabe Jacas has been exceeding expectations.

Ponder, the undrafted free agent, is making the year-two leap right in front of our eyes. He had two sacks against the Raiders, and he’s got five sacks on only 125 pass-rush snaps this season. That’s elite efficiency from a guy nobody drafted.

Jacas, the rookie, is stuffing the run and getting after the quarterback. A first-year player making plays on both fronts.

And Jones has been that steady veteran presence. Vrabel himself called him a real professional who knows his stuff, the kind of guy who’s good for a room full of young players.

That group ranks solidly in pressure rate, and it’s already delivered sacks, run stops, and takeaways. That’s not just a strong start. That’s a pass rush that can carry a defense.

And the roster moves keep showing how much depth this organization is building.

Safety Mike Brown and corner Kindle Vildor earned promotions from the practice squad. Brenden Schooler was activated just this week. And the team signed running back Hassan Haskins and corner DeShawn Jamison to the practice squad for more bodies.

The next-man-up mentality isn’t just talk around here. It’s operational. It’s how this team is built.

Now, there are concerns. Of course there are. And I’m not going to pretend otherwise.

TreVeyon Henderson is expected to miss multiple weeks with the ankle injury he suffered against the Raiders. That’s a real loss for this backfield.

Will Campbell’s knee needs monitoring after he went down early. It’s the same knee that cost him time last season, and the same knee he played through in the playoffs with a torn ligament. Nobody wants to see that repeated.

The cornerback depth is still thin. Carlton Davis and Karan Prunty are out against the Jets.

And fullback Reggie Gilliam and Mack Hollins are both on injured reserve.

That’s a lot of injuries. But the way this team responded on Sunday shows real resilience. And that’s what separates good teams from great ones.

Rhamondre Stevenson continues to be the lead back. And with Henderson out, he’ll shoulder even more of the load. Vrabel praised him this week as a great teammate who cares about the team, and especially for his pass protection and the way he plays without the football. With a rookie possibly starting at left tackle, that blocking matters more than ever.

And the offensive line has protected Maye well enough in these recent wins for him to play free and aggressive. That’s exactly what you want to see from a young quarterback finding his rhythm.
