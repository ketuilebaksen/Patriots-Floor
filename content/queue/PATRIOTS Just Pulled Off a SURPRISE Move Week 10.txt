PATRIOTS Just Pulled Off a SURPRISE Move Week 10!

Patriots Nation, Mike Vrabel stepped to the podium before the trip to Munich. And in just a few minutes, he made it very clear how this team is handling everything that’s happened over the last week.

The trade deadline passed with no additions. The Packers put up 45 points. The injury list keeps getting longer. And a former Patriot went on the radio and publicly disagreed with one of Vrabel’s comments.

And Vrabel’s response to all of it? He didn’t flinch. He didn’t make excuses. And he made a surprise move with his travel list that has a lot of fans talking. A.J. Brown is going to Germany.

So today on Patriots Floor, we’re breaking down everything Vrabel said before the Lions game. A.J. Brown’s status. The deadline. His scouting report on Detroit. The roster moves. The injury reserve crunch. And his sharp response to the criticism.

Let’s start with the surprise. A.J. Brown.

A reporter asked Vrabel if he expected Brown at practice, and whether the plan was still for him to go to Germany.

And Vrabel said yes. He said they’ll see what Brown can do. And then he said most everybody will be going to Germany, except for a few guys. And he included Brown in the group that would go.

Now, that’s a big deal. Brown missed the last two games with knee and ankle issues. He’s played in only three games all season, with nine catches for 101 yards. And earlier this month, Vrabel was even asked whether injured reserve was a possibility for him.

But he practiced on Wednesday, listed as limited. And now he’s on the plane to Munich.

But here’s the important part. A reporter followed up and asked if Brown going to Germany means he’ll definitely play.

And Vrabel was clear. He said there will be guys who go to Germany who won’t play. He said there will be a lot of guys who go but won’t be active. And he said he doesn’t know yet who those players will be.

So don’t get too far ahead. Brown traveling doesn’t guarantee he suits up. But it does mean the Patriots believe there’s a real chance. And for an offense that’s been missing its top receiver for weeks, even a chance is huge.

Then came the question everybody wanted to ask. The trade deadline came and went. The Patriots didn’t add anybody. Was Vrabel disappointed?

And his answer was pure Vrabel. He said he’s excited to get ready for the Lions. That’s all he can do right now. That’s it.

He said that was the focus before the deadline too. And he said it was good to get the team in on Tuesday afternoon to get a jump start, to understand the schedule, how they’re traveling, and what the plan is. And he said the Lions are another good football team, so that’s where the focus is.

No complaints about the front office. No talk about what could have been. Just the next game.

And honestly, that’s the right approach. The deadline is over. Jonathan Taylor, Derrick Henry, Josh Downs, none of them are coming. The only thing that matters now is winning with the players in the building.

Now let’s talk about the Lions. Because Vrabel had a lot of respect for this team.

He said Detroit knows how to win. He said they’ve built a program that he knows they’re proud of. And he called Dan Campbell a good football coach.

He said Jared Goff is playing at a high level, and has been for the last three years. He said the Lions have great skill players, speed, and they’re physical.

And on defense, Vrabel said the Lions have played really well over the last three weeks. He said they stay aggressive on fourth down, and they stay aggressive on special teams.

And then he said the stat that should scare every Patriots fan. The Lions don’t turn the ball over on offense. At all.

He said that’s because they just haven’t put the ball in harm’s way. And yet they still create explosive plays.

And he singled out Jahmyr Gibbs. Vrabel said Gibbs leads the league in touches.

Think about that matchup. The Patriots haven’t had a single turnover-free game this season, according to Drake Maye. And the Lions basically don’t turn it over at all. In a game like that, every possession matters. One turnover could decide it.

Vrabel was also asked about the Lions’ new offensive coordinator this season. And he said the run game and the scheme mostly look like what Detroit has always done. There are a few new wrinkles in the passing game. But a lot of the same concepts have carried over. And he said the coordinator has done a good job, giving Goff a lot of freedom at the line of scrimmage to get the offense into the right play.

That’s a tough combination. A veteran quarterback who’s been playing at a high level, with the freedom to change plays at the line, and weapons everywhere.

And Vrabel said it’ll be really hard to replicate Gibbs’s speed in practice. He said that’s always the concern when you face a player with that skill set. He said it’s hard to say they have anybody on the roster who can run the way Gibbs does, especially without pads on at this point of the season.

So practice this week was critical. Vrabel said that after the walkthrough on Tuesday, they needed to clean up the first and second down plan, and get some speed work in, against what the Lions do offensively and defensively.

He said the goal is to make sure everybody is communicating, they get their speed in, they understand the first, second, and third down game plan before they travel, and they get a real work day in. And he said he’s looking forward to getting back to work, and he thinks the players are too.

Now, here’s an interesting part of the press conference. A reporter asked about the defensive game planning. Is there enough cohesion among the coaches? And do players have any input on the game plans?

And Vrabel said cohesion is all they want as a staff. He said that’s what they’ve done since he became a head coach. Get everybody’s ideas, and figure out what works. And then they present the game plan to the players.

And then he said he knew where the question was going. He said every time the media questions a player and his ability to do his job, the answer becomes, well, it’s got to be the coaching. And Vrabel said, it’s all of us.

He said they talk to the players, and the players give feedback. But he said the professional mindset is simple. You have a job to do. Block your man. Throw the ball to the open guy. Run where they’re not. Cover. Rush. Tackle.

And he said the coaches have to teach the players the skills to do that, and give them the confidence to go out and do their job every week.

That’s a coach taking his share of the blame after a 45-point game. Not pointing fingers at players. Not pointing fingers at assistants. It’s all of us.

Now, the roster. A reporter asked about the roster spot that opened up.

And Vrabel said they’ll probably get to 53. He mentioned Greg Van Roten returning. And he said Reggie Gilliam will be back practicing too, which he called good news. But he said it was Wednesday, the first practice of the week, and it would be important to see how those guys did.

Van Roten matters a lot this week. With Ben Brown out with a knee injury, and Walter Rouse in the concussion protocol, the interior of the offensive line needs bodies. And Van Roten is a veteran who’s started a lot of football.

Gilliam, the fullback, could also help. He can shape the run game and help in protection. With Rhamondre Stevenson dealing with a knee injury and TreVeyon Henderson out, a fullback leading the way for Cory Ker could be a real asset.

Then came the question about Stevenson. Is there a chance he lands on injured reserve?

And Vrabel said there’s a chance anything could happen, but he wasn’t going to speculate. And then he got a little frustrated. He said, let’s get past the injuries, and focus on the Lions and what they need to do to win the game.

But he did add something important. He said if you pay attention, the Patriots don’t have many spots left to bring players back from injured reserve.

And that’s a real issue. Teams only get a limited number of IR returns each season. That’s why the Patriots released punter Bryce Baringer earlier this year. They’ve already used returns on players like A.J. Brown, Mack Hollins, and Greg Van Roten. So if Stevenson goes on IR, it could cost them one of their last remaining return slots. That makes every injury decision a lot more complicated.

Now, one of the most interesting parts of the press conference was about the Lions’ pass rush.

A reporter pointed out that Detroit has been one of the best defenses at creating unblocked pressure this season. And that’s an area where the Patriots’ offense has struggled in recent weeks.

So what’s the answer?

Vrabel kept it simple. He said the Patriots have to communicate. They have to identify who they’re blocking. And if they do that correctly, they should have everybody blocked. Then it comes down to whether they can actually win those blocks.

And if they choose not to redirect the protection, they have to know somebody’s going to be free. And then they’ll have to throw hot, or use whatever else is built into the play. He said you don’t have a lot of choices.

That’s going to be a huge test. Remember, the interior of this line is patched together. And the Packers had four sacks last week. Drake Maye has to be on the same page with his linemen, and get the ball out quickly when a blitzer comes free.

And Vrabel was also asked about offenses using quick snaps against his defense. He said when the center has his hand on the ball, the defense should probably be ready. And when the offense uses a hard count, the defense should probably wait until the ball is snapped. Pretty simple.

He said teams protect with seven blockers and quick snap, with three receivers in the route. And he said teams do that to defenses that pressure on third down or move players around. He said the Patriots have done okay with that, but there are times they could be a step quicker getting lined up. And he said third down overall hasn’t been a problem.

Then a reporter asked about building a program. Dan Campbell has established his program in Detroit. Vrabel is still building his. Has this season been a reminder of that?

And Vrabel said they’re not trying to reflect on anything. They’re just focused on this week. Who’s available. Who can improve. Who can practice. And finding a way to win a game.

And then he gave a really important answer about what’s been going wrong. He said they need to eliminate the things that get them beat, and keep doing the things that have been productive.

He pointed out that the offense hit explosive plays against Green Bay. And then he said, they just choose to turn the ball over too much.

Choose. That’s a strong word. Vrabel isn’t calling it bad luck. He’s saying turnovers are a choice, and they can be fixed.

And on defense, he said it comes down to a handful of plays. Whether the opponent makes a great play, or the Patriots hand it to them. And he said it’s okay if the other team earns it. But when the Patriots make it easy for them, those are the plays that get you beat.

He said the goal is to make every gain against this defense something the opponent has to earn. And then the Patriots have to come back, compete, stop the run, and pressure the quarterback. The things they do when they’re successful.

And now, the moment that really got people talking.

After the Packers loss, Vrabel and Mack Hollins both made comments about wanting players who want to be here. Hollins basically said that if anybody’s okay with not giving full effort, there are 31 other teams they could go play for.

And then, Robert Spillane went on the radio and said he didn’t agree with those comments.

So a reporter asked Vrabel if he had to explain what he meant. Were there any conversations about it?

And Vrabel’s answer was sharp. He said no, he didn’t have to explain himself. And he said Spillane didn’t say anything to him directly.

Then he explained what he meant. He said he wants people to be here. He wants the media to want to be here. If they’re going to come together as a team, he wants people who want to be here. He said that’s really nothing new. It’s not earth-shattering.

And he said he tried to explain that it wasn’t an accusation.

And then came the line that has everybody talking. He said if somebody took it like that, then it’s probably who they were talking to.

Patriots Nation, that’s Mike Vrabel. He’s not backing down. He’s not apologizing. He’s saying the message was clear, and if you felt targeted by it, maybe it was meant for you.

That’s a coach setting a standard. And it tells you he’s serious about effort.

And finally, Vrabel talked about the culture of the team. A reporter mentioned that Vrabel had praised the wide receiver group for being really unselfish, and had said he wished that was reflected in other parts of the team. Has he seen that spread since then?

And Vrabel said yes, that happens. And he said what they’ve actually talked about is spreading that attitude throughout the whole team. Not keeping it in one position group.

He said he wants the outside linebackers, the defensive line, the inside linebackers, every position group to have that same connection, urgency, and excitement. Not just the seven or eight receivers. He said nobody wants an environment where one group has it and the others don’t.

Remember what Vrabel said about the receivers earlier this season? Selfless. Happy when a teammate scores. On a pass, serve the quarterback. On a run, serve the running back. Now he wants that everywhere. On both sides of the ball.

And that’s exactly what this team needs right now. After a 45-point loss, the answer isn’t finger-pointing. It’s every player buying in for each other.

The surprise move: A.J. Brown is going to Germany, after practicing on a limited basis. But Vrabel was clear that some players who travel won’t be active.

The deadline: no additions, and Vrabel’s only response was that he’s excited to get ready for the Lions.

The opponent: a Lions team that knows how to win, with Jared Goff playing at a high level, Jahmyr Gibbs leading the league in touches, an aggressive defense, and an offense that doesn’t turn the ball over.

The roster: Greg Van Roten and Reggie Gilliam back at practice, the team expected to get to 53, and very few IR return slots left, which makes Rhamondre Stevenson’s situation tricky.

The message: turnovers are a choice. Make the opponent earn everything. And it’s all of us, coaches and players together.

And the controversy: Vrabel standing firm after Robert Spillane disagreed with his comments, saying if somebody took it personally, it’s probably who they were talking to.
