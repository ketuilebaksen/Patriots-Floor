Here’s what has my attention the most this week. Winning when everything is clicking is great. Anybody can look good when every starter is healthy and every play call works. But can this team keep playing at a high level when important pieces aren’t available?

Sunday could give us a real answer. Because it’s one thing to dominate a team when your offense is firing on all cylinders. It’s a completely different thing to find ways to win when players are missing and other guys have to step into bigger roles. That’s where depth becomes everything. And that’s exactly what the Patriots need to show against New York.

Let’s start with the defense, because we’ve got some great news.

Christian Gonzalez has been cleared to play. He’s made real progress in his recovery from the shoulder injury that kept him out the last two games, and Patriots Nation, getting him back could make a massive difference this week.

Why? Because of who’s on the other side. Garrett Wilson. The Jets’ top receiver has 31 catches for 374 yards and three touchdowns this season. That’s a guy who can make life miserable for a defense if he finds room to work. Give him space, and he’ll take over a game.

If Gonzalez is out there, he could be tasked with shadowing Wilson and making his day difficult from the first snap to the last. And I love that matchup. Having a cornerback of Gonzalez’s caliber gives this defense so much more flexibility in how it attacks the opposing offense.

Think about it this way. When you have a corner you trust to handle a tough matchup on his own, you don’t have to send safety help over the top every snap. That frees up the rest of the defense. More blitz options. More disguises. More bodies to help against the run or to take away the middle of the field. That’s what an elite corner does. He doesn’t just cover his guy. He unlocks the whole defense.

And Gonzalez’s return could also help New England avoid making as many adjustments in the secondary. That’s huge, because we saw how thin this group got. Heading into the Raiders game, Gonzalez, Carlton Davis III, and two more corners were all out at the same time. The staff had to piece together a secondary on the fly.

But there’s one problem. Gonzalez isn’t returning to a fully healthy secondary. Carlton Davis and Karan Prunty have both been ruled out for this matchup. So even with a key player coming back, the Patriots are still short on options in the back end.

And that matters. If Wilson starts winning his matchups, the Jets will keep going back to that well all game long. Offenses find the soft spot and attack it again and again. That’s why New England’s defense needs to be locked in from the opening snap, instead of relying on Gonzalez to fix everything by himself.

The key is making the Jets work for every single yard. Gonzalez can be a major part of that plan, but the rest of the defense has to do its job too. The pass rush has to get home. Remember Elijah Ponder? The undrafted guy with two sacks against the Raiders, and five sacks on only 125 pass-rush snaps this season. If he and the front can speed up the Jets’ quarterback, Wilson gets less time to work, and the backup corners get less time to get exposed.

If New England can limit Wilson’s opportunities and avoid giving up easy plays, it’ll have a much better chance of keeping the Jets’ offense under control.

Now, the injury concerns aren’t limited to the defense. On offense, TreVeyon Henderson’s situation deserves real attention.

Henderson is working his way back from the ankle injury he picked up against the Raiders, and he’s returned to limited practice. That’s encouraging. But it doesn’t guarantee he’ll be available against the Jets. Limited practice can mean a lot of things. It can mean he’s on track. It can mean he’s still a game-time decision.

Hopefully he gets back on the field soon, because there’s a lot to like about his potential. Before he got hurt on Sunday, he had four carries for 22 yards and a 16-yard catch. He gives this offense a different gear. But the Patriots have to be ready in case he can’t go.

Rhamondre Stevenson remains an important part of this offense. He played through a knee issue against the Raiders and still finished with 64 yards and a touchdown on 13 carries. That’s tough. That’s a veteran doing his job. But we all know getting through an NFL season takes more than one dependable running back. Bodies break down. Especially at that position.

And that’s exactly why Cory Ker’s performance against the Raiders caught my attention. He finished with 52 rushing yards, showing real improvement compared to his earlier outings. He ripped off runs of 10 and 32 yards in the fourth quarter.

But he also had a fumble on his very first carry that nearly made things difficult early in the game.

Now, I don’t think it’s fair to write off a young player because of one mistake. Every player needs opportunities to learn and improve. And honestly, the way he bounced back after that fumble says a lot about his mentality. But here’s the real question. If Henderson can’t play, is Ker ready to take on a bigger role in a division game? That’s a legitimate concern, and I don’t think anybody can answer it with confidence right now.

Here’s why it matters so much. The Patriots are trying to build on the momentum of this offense. And if the running game takes a step backward, that puts even more pressure on Drake Maye.

We know what happens when Maye feels like he has to carry everything. Through Week 3, his PFF passing grade was the lowest among 26 qualified quarterbacks. Over the last two weeks, it’s tied for the best in the NFL. A big part of that turnaround is the offense around him working. Take away the run game, and suddenly defenses can sit back and wait for him.

A reliable running game takes pressure off a quarterback by giving the offense another way to move the ball. It keeps the defense guessing, instead of letting opponents focus almost entirely on the passing game. That’s why the Patriots need to know exactly what they can realistically expect from their running backs if Henderson isn’t ready.

And that’s where a possibility worth discussing comes into play. Tony Pollard. The running back for the Tennessee Titans.

Should New England make a trade for him?

Look, I can see both sides of this debate. Let’s start with why it makes sense.

Pollard has experience. He’s shown he can produce over multiple seasons. And he can contribute as a pass catcher, which matters a lot in this offense. Through the first five games of 2026, he has 273 rushing yards on 68 carries and one touchdown.

He would give the Patriots another real option in the backfield. Especially if Henderson ends up missing more time. Think about the schedule. The Jets on Sunday. Then Chicago on Thursday night. Then a road trip to Miami. That’s a short week jammed between two division games. Running backs take a beating every week, and a short week makes it even worse. Adding a veteran like Pollard would give this team a lot more breathing room.

And Pollard’s pass-catching ability could be a weapon for Maye. When the primary receivers are covered, a reliable back out of the backfield is a lifesaver. That’s the kind of outlet that keeps drives alive and keeps a young quarterback from forcing throws into tight windows.

But here’s the other side. Would it really be worth giving up a draft pick to get him?

Pollard would be sharing carries with Stevenson. The Patriots would need to decide whether bringing him in actually makes this group better, or just makes it more crowded. That’s the big question.

Adding another running back might give the offense more depth, but the team would also have to find a role for him and figure out how to divide the carries. Stevenson on early downs? Pollard on passing downs? Henderson as the change of pace when he’s healthy? It can work. But it has to be planned, not thrown together.

If the current group can handle the workload, spending a draft pick might not be the best move. But if the coaching staff believes the running game needs more help, Pollard could be worth a serious look.

So what would it cost? A conditional sixth-round pick could be one possible starting point for trade discussions. But I want to be really clear here. That’s just an estimate. It’s not a confirmed asking price. Nobody should treat that as a done deal.

And the situation in Tennessee makes this more realistic than you might think. The Titans started the season with five straight losses. Pollard has just one more season under contract after 2026. When a team is 0 and 5, it starts thinking about the future. Veterans on short contracts become trade chips. Depending on Tennessee’s plans, a trade could make sense for both sides.

Still, I wouldn’t make a move just out of desperation. Panic trades are how franchises get burned. If the coaching staff believes the guys already on the roster can get the job done, maybe it’s smarter to hold on to those draft picks. But if there’s a chance to upgrade the offense at a reasonable cost, it’s at least worth considering.

And honestly, that’s how Mike Vrabel operates. He demands that every player is ready. He doesn’t care about names. He cares about who can do the job on Sunday. If the staff looks at this backfield and decides it needs another proven body to survive the stretch ahead, I trust them to make the call. And if they decide the answer is already in the building, I trust that too.

Now, speaking of players the Patriots could be missing on Sunday, there’s another situation we need to keep an eye on.

Will Campbell is dealing with a knee injury and is listed as questionable for the game. If he can’t suit up against the Jets, Caleb Lomu could step in at left tackle.

And here’s why you should keep your eye on him. Against the Raiders, Lomu played 29 pass-blocking snaps without allowing a sack. On top of that, the Patriots held Maxx Crosby, one of the best pass rushers in football, to zero sacks. That’s a small sample, of course. But it shows Lomu made the most of his opportunity.

Remember, Lomu was the 28th overall pick in April, and the Patriots traded up to get him. He played left tackle at Utah. He’s been working as a swing piece on the right side and in jumbo packages. And when his number got called in the middle of a game, he delivered.

That doesn’t mean replacing Campbell would be easy. Protecting Drake Maye has to remain the top priority, especially in a division game. The Jets will have had a full week of film on Lomu. They’ll know his tendencies. They’ll design pressures to test him. That’s what division rivals do.

But if Lomu puts together another solid performance, the Patriots might find out they have a promising option already on the roster. And that changes everything about how this team handles Campbell’s recovery. No rushing him back. No repeat of last January, when Campbell played through a torn ligament in the playoffs and Maye got sacked over and over again.

The challenge is that playing left tackle comes with a ton of responsibility. That player has to protect the quarterback’s blind side and give the offense a chance to run its plays without constant pressure. If Campbell can’t go, Lomu has to be ready for a bigger challenge than the one he faced against the Raiders. Last week, he came in mid-game against a team that was chasing the score. This week, he’d be starting from the first snap, against a defense that’s had all week to plan for him.

Of course, one good performance doesn’t guarantee the same results in the next game. But it does give the Patriots something positive to build on if Lomu steps up when his number is called. And for an offensive line dealing with this much uncertainty, that could make a real difference.

We also need to keep an eye on A.J. Brown. The receiver has returned to practice after dealing with that ankle issue. That’s big news. But whether he’ll be available against the Jets depends on the team’s final decision and his roster status. He’s been on injured reserve, so the Patriots have to make a move to activate him.

If he’s available, he gives Maye another dangerous option in the passing game. Think about adding a receiver of his caliber to an offense that just put up 536 yards. Douglas, Williams, Henry, and now Brown. That’s a lot for any defense to deal with.

If he’s not ready, the offense has to keep things moving with the players who are. And we’ve already seen this receiver room handle that. Vrabel called them selfless. When one guy eats, they all eat.

And that’s what makes this matchup so interesting. The Patriots have key players trying to get back on the field. Other players need to step up. And there are roster decisions on the table that could shape the rest of the season.

Now let’s talk about the bigger picture. New England comes into this game at 3 and 2. The Jets are sitting at 2 and 3. A division win would strengthen the Patriots’ position and build even more confidence after that dominant performance against Las Vegas.

But they can’t take the field thinking about the last game. Vrabel already made that clear to his locker room. None of Sunday’s success matters against the Jets. Every week starts at zero. And he’s right. The Jets have players who can cause problems. One mistake, one turnover, one blown coverage, and a matchup that looks favorable on paper can turn into a real fight.

I see plenty of reasons to be optimistic. Gonzalez is cleared, and that strengthens the defense in a big way. Lomu has already shown he can contribute on the offensive line. Henderson still has a chance to return. Brown is back at practice. And the conversation about adding a veteran back like Pollard shows this team is thinking seriously about depth.

At the same time, the absences in the secondary and the question marks on offense can’t be ignored. These are exactly the situations that show us whether the Patriots have enough depth to keep competing all season long.

For me, the biggest thing is seeing how New England responds when a game doesn’t go exactly as planned. Can the defense handle its responsibilities despite the injuries? Can the offense keep moving the ball if one of its running backs is unavailable? And can the offensive line give Maye enough protection to stay in rhythm?

Those are the questions that matter beyond one Sunday. A strong performance against the Jets wouldn’t just mean another win. It would give the Patriots real confidence that they can handle adversity as the season moves forward. And that’s what separates good teams from great ones.

And even if they come away with another big win, I’d still keep a close eye on that backfield situation. Because the decision this team makes about its running backs could matter a lot more in December than it does this Sunday.
