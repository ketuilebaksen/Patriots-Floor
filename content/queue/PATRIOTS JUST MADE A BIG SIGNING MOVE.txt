Let’s start with the win in Miami. Because it tells you everything about this team.

The Patriots went into halftime down by 10 points. On the road. In the rain. Against a division rival playing at home.

And look at who wasn’t on the field. A.J. Brown, ruled out with knee and ankle issues. Mack Hollins, sidelined with a calf injury. TreVeyon Henderson, out with an illness. Romeo Doubs, on injured reserve. And Hunter Henry was dealing with a foot injury.

And Drake Maye? He was spectacular. 16 of 20 passing, for 199 yards and two touchdowns, with zero interceptions. And he added a rushing touchdown on top of it.

Sixteen of twenty. That’s an 80 percent completion rate. With a patched-together receiving group. On the road. In the rain.

And the comeback itself? That’s character. A 10-point hole at halftime, with half your offense in street clothes, is the kind of situation where a lot of teams fold. This team came out in the second half and took the game over.

Now let’s get to the moves. Because this is where the real story is hiding.

The first move came the day before the Dolphins game. The Patriots signed running back Lan Larison from the practice squad to the 53-man roster.

And Larison’s story is a great one. He joined New England as an undrafted free agent out of UC Davis in 2025. And he spent his entire rookie season on injured reserve after a foot injury in the preseason.

Think about that for a second. A young player who did everything right. Worked in the shadows. Never got a chance to play. And now his name gets called when the team needs him.

And he has the background to back it up. At UC Davis in 2024, he ran for 1,465 yards and 17 touchdowns. He was the Big Sky Offensive Player of the Year in 2023. Over his college career, he had 3,634 rushing yards and 41 touchdowns.

And here’s the detail that stood out. The Patriots could have just elevated him from the practice squad. He still had two elevations left. Instead, they gave him a full roster spot. That tells you they believe in him. And it protects him from other teams who might try to sign him away.

That’s the kind of story that inspires a locker room. It sends a message to every player on the practice squad. Opportunity is real in this building.

At the same time, wide receiver Ryan Miller was elevated from the practice squad for the game.

And here’s where it gets fun. Miller started this season with, believe it or not, the Miami Dolphins. They waived him on October 13th. The Patriots signed him to the practice squad on October 19th. And then they put him on the active roster for the game against Miami.

Talk about a revenge game.

And Miller isn’t a stranger to the league. He’s appeared in 30 NFL games, with six starts. He’s caught 17 passes for 254 yards and four touchdowns in his career. And he has 421 career special teams snaps. For a team missing multiple receivers, that kind of experience at the bottom of the depth chart is gold.

Now, here’s this week’s big signing move. And it’s a story of how fast things change in the NFL.

The Patriots signed safety Mike Brown to the 53-man roster from the practice squad.

Brown had been elevated earlier in the season, so he’s been in and out of game-day roles. He was one of the practice squad players who earned a promotion, and now he gets a permanent spot on the roster. That’s a reward for hard work. And it gives this secondary, which has been hit by injuries all season, another reliable body.

Think about how much the secondary has been tested this year. Carlton Davis III missed multiple games with a neck injury. Christian Gonzalez missed time with a shoulder injury. Channing Canada, Karan Prunty, and Charles Woods have all missed time. Marcus Jones had to move outside, where he struggled. Every healthy defensive back matters on this team.

And to make room, the Patriots released safety Elliot Davidson. Davidson is only 24 years old, and he was promoted to the 53-man roster just two weeks earlier, on October 19th.

That tells you how fast things move in this league. One day you’re getting promoted. A couple of weeks later, your spot is needed somewhere else. It’s a cold reminder that the back end of an NFL roster is a revolving door. But it also shows the Patriots are constantly tinkering, looking for the right mix at every position.

The Patriots also signed running back Terrell Jennings to the practice squad. And if that name sounds familiar, it should. Jennings was in Foxborough for a workout back on October 16th, after Henderson’s ankle injury. He spent time with the organization in 2024 and 2025, joined this year’s offseason and preseason work, and landed on injured reserve in August before an injury settlement ended that stint.

So he knows the building. He knows the offense. He knows the terminology. And with Henderson out with an illness and Stevenson carrying a big load, adding a back who doesn’t need to learn the system from scratch makes a lot of sense.

And linebacker Jose Ramirez was signed to the practice squad too. Another familiar face. He’s been in and out of the organization, he has special teams experience, and he knows the Patriots’ system.

These are the kinds of moves that rarely trend on social media. But they build depth. And depth is exactly what wins you games in November and December, when injuries start taking over the league.

And this pattern didn’t just start this week. Let’s rewind a little further.

Back on September 30th, the Patriots signed veteran offensive lineman Matt Prior off the Arizona Cardinals’ practice squad. Prior is a nine-year veteran who’s started 40 games and played for six different teams.

At the time, the Patriots were down to their third-string right guard and desperately needed depth up front. And here’s why that signing was a big deal. When you sign a player off another team’s practice squad, you have to keep him on your active roster for at least three weeks. So the Patriots were fully committing to the move. That shows how seriously they took the offensive line situation.

And it paid off. Prior helped close out the Jets game, on that eight-run drive with Cory Ker that drained the clock. Then he started at right guard in Chicago. He even admitted he still didn’t know the entire playbook. But he went out there and competed. Mike Vrabel said it was fun to watch him take advantage of the opportunity.

And the offensive line concern still hasn’t gone away. During the win over Miami, the Patriots made a change up front in the second half. And the protection around Drake Maye got steadier as the game went along. That’s a big reason the comeback happened.

When your quarterback is playing at this level, the last thing you want is him running for his life. Every veteran lineman, every reliable backup, every extra body who can hold a block, gives Maye a better chance to keep this offense rolling.

Now, think about what Drake Maye has done with all this change around him.

At least three total touchdowns in five straight games. Coming into the Miami game, he had 1,784 passing yards, temporarily second in the NFL. After a rough start with six interceptions in his first three games, he’s become one of the most dangerous quarterbacks in the league.

But even great quarterbacks need help. You can’t ask Maye to win every single week with a patchwork receiving group and a shaky offensive line.

That’s why these moves matter. They’re not about finding the next superstar. They’re about making sure that when one player goes down, the whole machine doesn’t break down with him.

And that philosophy fits perfectly with what Mike Vrabel has been preaching all season. He’s talked about how everybody needs to prepare like a starter, because things like this can happen. Caleb Lomu said it himself earlier this season. Vrabel tells everyone to prepare like a starter all week.

It sounds like a coaching cliché, until you see it in action. Larison. Miller. Mike Brown. Matt Prior. Cory Ker. They’re the proof.

And then, on Monday, during his appearance on WEEI, Vrabel said the team will try to do what it can to make the roster better, with the trade deadline part of that conversation.

Now, that doesn’t mean a big trade is coming. Nobody outside the building can say that for sure. But it does tell us this coaching staff is never satisfied, and it’s always looking for an edge.

And the pieces are in place. The Patriots now have about 6.1 million dollars in cap space, after restructuring Hunter Henry’s contract. And Albert Breer has reported that the Patriots are looking to be buyers at the deadline. He even mentioned a possible reunion with K’Lavon Chaisson, the pass rusher who had seven and a half sacks for New England last season, plus three more in the playoffs, and is now with Washington on a one-year deal.

And there are other names out there too. Quenton Nelson on the offensive line. Andrew Wylie. Josh Downs, Maye’s college teammate. The front office has options.
