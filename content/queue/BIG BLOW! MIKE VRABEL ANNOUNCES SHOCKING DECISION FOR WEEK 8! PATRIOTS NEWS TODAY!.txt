Let’s start with the decision everybody’s talking about. TreVeyon Henderson in the return game.

A reporter pointed out that Vrabel has voiced confidence that Henderson will contribute going forward. And with that in mind, he asked if Vrabel has thought about putting Henderson back on returns.

And Vrabel didn’t shut the door. Not at all. He said that’s probably something they’ll always consider, and they’ll weigh the options.

Then he gave an honest look at where the return game stands. He said coming out of the Jets game, they were close on some returns. But against Chicago, they had a couple that really weren’t very effective, and one that was okay.

And then he said the focus is on Miami, and what they feel they need to do to create some positive momentum in the return game. Whether that’s with Cory Ker, or Kyle Williams. And then he added that they’ll certainly take a look at anybody else.

Patriots Nation, that’s the opening. “Anybody else.” And with Henderson’s speed, that’s a very real possibility.

Think about what Henderson brings. Bleacher Report ranked him second among all second-year running backs, behind only Ashton Jeanty. As a rookie, he topped 1,000 total yards and scored 10 touchdowns. And his acceleration is still there. He can change a defender’s angle in one step. That’s exactly the kind of skill that wins on kick returns.

And the Patriots need a spark in the return game. Remember, in the Jets win, it was special teams that sealed it, with DeMario Douglas recovering the onside kick. And in Chicago, it was a 58-yard field goal by the Bears that hurt. Special teams can swing a game either way.

And then came the question about Henderson’s role on offense. A reporter asked if there’s anything preventing him from being a little more impactful with the carries he does get.

And Vrabel’s answer was really interesting. He didn’t single Henderson out. He said they’ll see what happens this week. They have to go run the football. They have to execute. He said Henderson has had some runs where he’s helped them. And then he said this. We all have to be better in the run game. Not just one guy.

And he finished by saying they’ll have to go down to Miami and be able to run it. They’ll have to protect. And they’ll have to take care of the football.

That’s Vrabel protecting his player, while also setting the standard. And it lines up exactly with what we saw in Chicago. Take out Drake Maye’s scrambles, and the Patriots ran for 44 yards on 13 carries. Henderson had three carries for nine yards. Stevenson had six for 22. Cory Ker had four for 13. It wasn’t one guy. It was the whole system. And Vrabel knows it.

Vrabel was also asked about Eli Raridon’s growth. And his answer was short and very Vrabel. He said Raridon is just trying to get better each week. And then he said he wants to focus on Miami, not sit there and give a mid-season report after seven games. The team has to go on the road in the division.

Classic Vrabel. No looking back. One game at a time.

But we don’t mind giving the mid-season report on Raridon ourselves. He went from two catches for 32 yards in the first three weeks to 10 catches for 136 yards over the next four. He scored a 30-yard touchdown against the Bears, beating his defender through the middle. And according to SumerSports, his 2.13 yards per route ranked fourth among qualifying tight ends. That’s real growth.

And here’s what makes this week even more interesting. The Dolphins have been allowing the fifth-most catches and the fourth-most receiving yards per game to tight ends. So Raridon and Hunter Henry have a great matchup on Sunday.

There was also a quick update on a couple of players eligible to come back from injured reserve this week. Vrabel said he probably wouldn’t expect them to be at practice as the team prepares for Miami. So don’t expect reinforcements from IR just yet.

And remember why that matters. The Patriots only have five IR return slots left, with around 20 players on IR. That’s why they released punter Bryce Baringer earlier this week. Every return slot is precious.

Now let’s get to the scouting report on the Dolphins. Because Vrabel had a lot of respect for this team.

He said they play extremely hard. And he said he has a lot of respect for what their head coach, Jeff Hafley, has done. He mentioned they’ve dealt with a unique set of circumstances, which the Dolphins themselves have admitted.

And he talked about their quarterback, Malik Willis. He said Hafley has been around Willis and seen him grow, develop, and win football games.

On offense, Vrabel said the Dolphins run the ball, and they shift and use motion on every play. And they’ve run for 185 yards per game over the past two weeks. That’s a lot.

He said Willis can hurt you, whether on designed runs or by extending plays. He said they create explosive plays, and they’re tough to tackle.

And that’s the part that should worry Patriots fans the most. Because tackling was the biggest problem in Chicago. Vrabel himself said after the Bears game that the tackling wasn’t good enough. Marcus Jones alone was credited with six missed tackles. Against a team that’s tough to tackle and creates explosive plays, that has to be fixed.

Vrabel also mentioned a Dolphins player named Washington, who had a 74-yard return against the Patriots last year as a punt returner, and who can also make explosive plays on offense. He pointed to another explosive play last week, where the Dolphins threw a five-yard pass and the defense couldn’t make the tackle, and it went for a touchdown.

That’s exactly the kind of play the Patriots can’t allow. A short completion turning into a long touchdown because of one missed tackle.

And Vrabel talked about the Dolphins’ defense too. He said they’re doing a really good job continuing to improve defensively. They’ve got veterans and some core players.

He mentioned Chop Robinson, who keeps mixing speed and power as a pass rusher. That’s going to be a real test for this patched-up offensive line.

He mentioned Zach Sieler, a player Vrabel said the Patriots have always struggled with. Sieler even blocked a field goal against them last year.

He talked about Jordyn Brooks, who Vrabel said has led the league in tackles the last two years. And another linebacker he called an instinctive football player who hammers every single time.

And he mentioned a safety with two interceptions.

And then Vrabel said something important about the atmosphere. He said the Dolphins should be excited playing at home in front of their crowd. And the Patriots have to go on the road, inside the division, and keep improving.

Now here’s a fun part of the press conference. A reporter mentioned that Vrabel was around the Boston College program for a few years when his son, Tyler, was playing there. And Jeff Hafley, now the Dolphins head coach, was building that program at BC. So what did Vrabel like about it?

And Vrabel laughed. He said he had a job too. He was coaching the Titans. So it wasn’t like he was there every day watching practice and yelling, “Come on, Tyler, let’s go!” He said he didn’t tell Hafley what to do. He tried to talk football and just support his son like any parent would.

But then he gave Hafley a lot of credit. He said Hafley is a very good football coach. He’s coached at the highest level, as an assistant in the NFL and at Ohio State, and he did a really good job at BC.

And he described Hafley’s defensive style. An excellent coordinator. Sound, fundamental beliefs. He said Hafley’s defenses pressure, but it’s not unsound. It’s not a scheme you can just exploit, with guys left uncovered. They play split safety, post safety, multiple coverages. Hafley has a secondary background. And he mixes in the blitz, but he’s not blitzing every single play.

And he called Hafley a good teacher, and said he has a lot of respect for him.

So Drake Maye is going to face a defense that’s disciplined and multiple. Not the kind you can just beat with one trick. And remember, Maye has had to grow against pressure. Against the Bears, he said he had to have better answers, get the ball out quicker, and be the conductor at the line of scrimmage. This week is another test of that.

And then there was the running back the Dolphins have been riding. Ollie Gordon.

A reporter pointed out that Gordon has really come on over the last few weeks. And asked what Vrabel has seen, and how you defend a run game that likes to pull linemen as often as Miami does.

Vrabel said Gordon is a big back, and he’s taking advantage of his opportunities, just like they always talk about in the NFL. Over the last four games, he has four touchdowns, and he’s averaging 4.8 yards a carry. He said Gordon is doing exactly what you want. Getting opportunities, and making the most of them.

And on defending those pulling linemen, Vrabel broke it down. Whether it’s gap scheme, zone, or toss crack, you have to get players over there when the offense moves and adds gaps somewhere else. You either have to move your linemen over, or get your linebackers to fit, and get your secondary players to make tackles.

There it is again. Tackling. It all comes back to tackling.

And here’s the good news. The Patriots have been excellent against the run this season. Before the Bears game, they were one of only two teams that hadn’t allowed a single 20-yard run, and they’d given up just 91.8 rushing yards per game, the eighth-fewest in the league. So the foundation is there. They just have to tackle.

Now, one of the most interesting parts of the press conference was about self-evaluation.

A reporter asked if the extra time between the Thursday game and this one gave the team a chance to do more self-scouting.

Vrabel said they always try to do that. But with a little extra time, they could ask, where are we after seven weeks? What are some things we can improve? Where do we need to improve fundamentally?

And then he said something really honest. He said there are only certain ways to improve during the season. Maybe it’s a few details, or a few fundamentals. And he doesn’t think there’s going to be some whole big change based on seven weeks.

So instead, he said, they’re going to keep harping on the fundamentals, and give players a couple of things they can realistically improve on. Maybe that’s tackling. Maybe it’s a pass rush move that’s close, but just not finishing. Or reaching with the far arm. Those are the kinds of details they focused on over the weekend.

And Vrabel had explained a comment from Monday, about some things the team might just have to be okay with not improving. He clarified that he didn’t mean it in a negative way. He said, from his perspective, it’s what they’ve seen over seven weeks. And you can yell and scream about it until you’re blue in the face, or you can coach it, and maybe it’s not going to change. And the real question is simple. Is the guy getting his job done?

Patriots Nation, that’s a coach being real. Not promising miracles. Just focusing on what can actually get better.

And think about that pass rush detail. A move that’s close but not finishing. That sounds a lot like the young edge rushers. Gabe Jacas had five pressures against the Bears, his best game yet. Elijah Ponder has five and a half sacks. They’re getting close on a lot of plays. If they start finishing, this pass rush could take over games.

Vrabel was also asked about a young defensive player who’s been coming on more in recent games. And Vrabel said he continues to improve. He puts a lot of time in. He spends time with his position coaches so he can play multiple spots. And Vrabel said he’ll keep improving the more opportunities he gets and the more comfortable he becomes.

That’s the theme again with this staff. Develop the young players. Give them opportunities. Let them grow.

And then Vrabel got into the details of coaching young edge rushers. A reporter asked how you coach them to use their explosiveness to make plays, while still playing disciplined and not losing contain on the quarterback or the running backs.

And Vrabel’s answer was a masterclass. He said contain is a bit of a misnomer. Everybody thinks that every time the ball goes outside, it’s the outside linebacker’s job. But he said the outside linebacker might have that responsibility on about 70 to 75 percent of plays. Other times, it might be a safety or a linebacker. Somebody is responsible for setting the edge, but it’s not always the same guy.

And on the pass rush, he said it’s a balance based on who you’re playing and what the plan is that week. And he said it seems like every week there’s a mobile quarterback who can extend plays. So you have to put a plan together, and make great split-second decisions.

Do I come under? Do I try to stay level at the top of the pocket? Do I get back into the middle of the pocket? And those decisions affect the rest of the defense. What’s the coverage? When can they be aggressive? When there’s pressure called, do I have to keep my outside arm free and stay at the top of the pocket in case the quarterback takes off?

He said there are a lot of factors. Mainly the call they’re in, and who the quarterback is.

And this week, that quarterback is Malik Willis. A guy who can hurt you with designed runs and by extending plays. So Jacas, Ponder, and the whole front have to be disciplined. Aggressive, but smart.

And finally, the heat. A reporter asked how the Miami heat affects snap counts, and whether the Dolphins try to use it with tempo.

Vrabel said the Dolphins will go on the ball a little bit. But he said the Patriots have to be conditioned. And then he said the key. The better the offense plays, the more it possesses the ball, and the fresher the defense will stay.

He said they’ll rotate the receivers and the backs. He doesn’t think the offensive linemen will necessarily rotate, although they might play a couple of guys depending on who’s available. And they’ll rotate the edge rushers and the defensive line, because they want their best players available to help them win.

That’s a smart plan. Long drives on offense. Fresh legs on defense. And a rotation that keeps everybody ready for the fourth quarter in the Miami heat.

So let’s put it all together.

The surprise: Vrabel opening the door to TreVeyon Henderson returning kicks, saying they’ll take a look at anybody to spark the return game.

The run game: Vrabel saying it’s not just one guy, the whole team has to be better at running the football, protecting, and taking care of the ball.

The opponent: a Dolphins team that runs for 185 yards a game over the last two weeks, with Malik Willis making plays, Ollie Gordon scoring four touchdowns in four games, and a disciplined, multiple defense coached by Jeff Hafley.

The self-scout: no big overhaul after seven weeks, just a laser focus on fundamentals like tackling and finishing pass rushes.

And the plan for the heat: possess the ball, rotate the skill players and the defensive front, and keep the best players fresh.

The Patriots are 4 and 3, coming off a tough loss in Chicago. A division win on the road in Miami would be exactly the bounce-back this team needs, with the trade deadline coming on November 10th.
