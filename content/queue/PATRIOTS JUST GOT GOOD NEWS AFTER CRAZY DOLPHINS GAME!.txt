Reason number one. This team refuses to stay down.

Let’s go back to what this season has looked like. Because when you see the full journey, this win means even more.

Through the first three weeks, the Patriots were struggling. Low scoring. Turnovers everywhere. Drake Maye’s PFF passing grade through Week 3 was 36.6, the lowest among 26 qualified quarterbacks. People were already asking whether last season’s run to the Super Bowl was a one-year wonder.

The team sat at 2 and 2. And the doubters were loud.

Then came the response. A 29 to 26 win in Buffalo, one of the hardest places to play in the NFL. Then a 45 to 7 demolition of a Raiders team that had started the season 3 and 0. Then a 26 to 19 win over the Jets, closed out by Cory Ker carrying the ball eight straight times to drain the clock. Three straight wins.

Then Chicago. A 30 to 24 loss on a short week, on the road, with a guard making his first career start and another who’d been on the practice squad three weeks earlier. A tough one. The kind of loss that can send a season sideways.

And what did this team do? It went on the road, into a division rival’s stadium, without some of its best players, and won.

That’s the pattern. Get knocked down. Get back up. Every single time.

And that’s exactly what Mike Vrabel has been preaching. He told his locker room that the last game has no bearing on the next one. Every week starts at zero. After the big win over the Raiders, he told them none of it would matter against the Jets. And after the loss in Chicago, the same rule applied. Reset. Refocus. Go win the next one.

This team believes it. And it shows.

Reason number two. The depth on this roster is real.

Let’s talk about who wasn’t on the field in Miami. Because this list is incredible.

A.J. Brown, the star receiver, ruled out with a knee issue. TreVeyon Henderson, ruled out. Romeo Doubs, on injured reserve, after leading the team in receiving yards before he got hurt. Mack Hollins, on injured reserve. Alijah Vera-Tucker, the left guard, on injured reserve. Mike Onwenu, out for the season. Greg Van Roten, on injured reserve.

That’s a starting receiver, a backup receiver, a top running back, a star receiver, and three offensive linemen. And the team still won.

Do you know how rare that is?

Most teams would be in survival mode with that many players out. The Patriots went on the road and won a division game.

And that’s not luck. That’s the result of a front office and a coaching staff that built real depth. Remember what Vrabel said about undrafted players a couple of weeks ago? It doesn’t matter what you did before you got here. It just matters what you do when you get here. And then he said the team tries to find players who can help, and when they do, they get more opportunities.

That’s this roster. Guys like Efton Chism III, undrafted, stepping into bigger roles. Guys like Cory Ker, who cost the team a future seventh-round pick in a trade with Arizona, and became the closer in the Jets game. Guys like Walter Rouse, acquired in a late-round pick swap, who made his first NFL start in Chicago.

And in the days before Miami, the Patriots added Lan Larison to the 53-man roster, a running back who ran for over 3,600 yards and 41 touchdowns in college. And they elevated Ryan Miller, a receiver with 421 career special teams snaps, who had just been released by the very team they were playing.

Next man up isn’t a slogan in Foxborough. It’s how this team wins.

Reason number three. Drake Maye is turning into the quarterback we dreamed about.

Let’s talk about the franchise. Because everything starts with him.

Remember that rough start? In the first three games of the season, Maye completed 51 of 80 passes for 585 yards, with one touchdown and six interceptions. A 58.6 passer rating. That was hard to watch.

And then he flipped a switch.

Over the next four games, before Miami, he completed 90 of 134 passes for 1,199 yards. He threw at least two touchdown passes in each of those four games, with 12 total. Against the Raiders, he threw for 374 yards and five total touchdowns, with zero interceptions. PFF gave him a career-high 93.5 passing grade, and a 99.9 grade on deep throws, a mark only Jordan Love and Patrick Mahomes had reached this season.

Coming into this game, he had 1,784 passing yards, temporarily second in the entire NFL after the Bears game. His MVP odds had climbed back up. His turnover-worthy play rate had dropped from a league-high 8.6 percent to just 1.1 percent at one point.

And here’s what I love most. It’s not just the numbers. It’s the way he carries himself.

After the Bears loss, he stood at the podium and said, “I let my team down.” No excuses. No blaming anybody else. And he talked about needing to be the conductor at the line of scrimmage, having better answers, getting the ball out faster to help his linemen.

That’s a leader. And then he went out and helped his team win the very next game, on the road, without his top receiver.

And Mike Vrabel has his back every step of the way. After the Chicago game, with Maye having thrown an interception in the end zone, Vrabel said, “I’m glad he’s ours.”

That’s the relationship you want between a head coach and a franchise quarterback. Trust. Accountability. Belief.

Reason number four. The young players are growing up fast.

This might be the most exciting part of the whole season. Because the future of this team is showing up right now.

Look at Elijah Ponder. An undrafted pass rusher out of Cal Poly. Thirty-two teams passed on him. Coming into this game, he had five and a half sacks, with at least half a sack in five straight games. Seven tackles for loss. Ten quarterback hits. A forced fumble and two fumble recoveries. That’s a breakout season from a guy nobody drafted.

Look at Gabe Jacas. The rookie edge rusher had five quarterback pressures against the Bears, probably the best game of his young career. At one point, he drove the Bears’ left tackle straight back into the quarterback with his bull rush. Vrabel’s defense is getting a whole new wave of pass rushers.

Look at Caleb Lomu. The first-round rookie tackle stepped in twice for extended stretches when starters went down, on both the left and right side. Across 160 offensive snaps, he allowed just one pressure. Against the Raiders, he helped hold Maxx Crosby without a sack. Against the Jets, he played 59 snaps at right tackle without allowing a single pressure. Vrabel said he’s improved. And he’s only 21.

Look at Eli Raridon. The rookie tight end went from two catches for 32 yards in the first three weeks, to 10 catches for 136 yards over the next four. He scored a 30-yard touchdown in Chicago, beating his defender through the middle. According to SumerSports, his 2.13 yards per route ranked fourth among qualifying tight ends.

And look at the rookie class as a whole. They’ve played the second-most special teams snaps in the entire NFL. And Vrabel said something about them that every Patriots fan should remember. He said they’re very coachable. Much more coachable than some of the veterans. And that’s why he likes coaching them.

When your young players are this coachable and this productive, you’re not just building for one season. You’re building for a decade.

Reason number five. The veterans are leading the way.

Young talent is great. But you don’t win in this league without veterans who set the standard. And this team has them.

Look at Hunter Henry. Coming into this game, he had 26 catches for 296 yards this season. Over his last five games before Miami, he was averaging 54.3 receiving yards, up from 33.2 before that. In Chicago, he caught all seven passes thrown his way. And he has 28 touchdowns as a Patriot, tied with Russ Francis for third among the franchise’s tight ends.

And then, just days before this game, he agreed to restructure his contract, turning half his base salary into a signing bonus. That created about 3.3 million dollars in cap space for the team. He didn’t take a pay cut. But he made it easier for the front office to go get help before the trade deadline. That’s a team-first veteran.

Look at Rhamondre Stevenson. The lead back, with 77 carries for 285 yards coming in. Against the Bears, he had a career-high 93 receiving yards, including a 48-yard touchdown. And Vrabel has praised him for the things that don’t always show up in the box score. His pass protection. The way he plays without the football. His willingness to block for others. Vrabel said those are the guys who care about the team.

Look at Draymond Jones, the veteran edge rusher who battled through injury to get on the field. Vrabel called him a real professional who knows his stuff, and said he’s been great for a room full of young players.

And look at Kevin Byard, the veteran safety, bringing his experience and leadership to a secondary that’s been through so much this season.

That’s the mix you need. Young players who are hungry, and veterans who show them how to win.

Then, on November 15th, the Patriots head to Munich, Germany, to face the Detroit Lions at Allianz Arena. An international stage. The whole world watching.

And right in between, on November 10th, the trade deadline.

And think about where this team is heading into that stretch. 5 and 3. Riding the momentum of a division road win. With players starting to get healthier, and a front office that just created 5.2 million dollars in cap space.

The Patriots have been linked to some big names. Quenton Nelson, the highest-graded guard in the NFL. Andrew Wylie, the two-time Super Bowl champion lineman.
