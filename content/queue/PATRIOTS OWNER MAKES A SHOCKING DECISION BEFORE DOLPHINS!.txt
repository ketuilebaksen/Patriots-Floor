So let’s go in-depth on Ryan Miller.

He’s 26 years old, 6-foot-2, out of Furman. And here’s something interesting. He started his college career as a tight end, then moved to receiver. He went undrafted in 2023.

And he hasn’t stayed in one place very long. First, it was Tampa Bay. Then the Giants claimed him in December. After the Giants waived him in August, Miami claimed him the very next day. He was with the Dolphins until October 13th, when they waived him. And New England signed him to the practice squad just days later, on October 19th, after Romeo Doubs went on injured reserve.

And the cut is strange. Because back in Week 2, Miller caught a 77-yard touchdown pass from Malik Willis. Now, it came in garbage time of a 35 to 13 loss to San Francisco. But still. For a guy who just connected with your starting quarterback on a 77-yard touchdown, you’d think a team wouldn’t be in such a hurry to waive him.

For the season, he has three catches for 92 yards, pretty much all from that stretch with the Dolphins. He hasn’t caught a pass for the Patriots yet. This could be his first real chance.

And just because you haven’t heard much about him doesn’t mean he isn’t getting praised. Receivers coach Todd Downing talked about Miller on Friday, before any of these injuries happened. Downing said Miller is still learning the verbiage of the play calls, making sure he knows exactly what to do when a play is called.

But here’s the good news. He’s seen Miami’s defense from the inside. He practiced against it. He knows their tendencies. And he has a lot of special teams experience.

Tampa. The Giants. Miami. And now New England. Four teams in two years. And on Sunday, he walks into Hard Rock Stadium to face the team that cut him less than a month ago.

Now, Lan Larison is a different story. And honestly, it’s a strange one.

In 2024 at UC Davis, he ran for 1,465 yards and 17 touchdowns on 284 carries. He was the Big Sky Offensive Player of the Year in 2023. Over 50 college games, he had 3,634 rushing yards and 41 rushing touchdowns, plus 127 catches on top of that.

That’s real production.

The Patriots signed him as an undrafted rookie in 2025. But then he hurt his foot in the preseason, and that was his whole rookie year. No games.

This August, he was cut at final roster cuts on the 30th, and brought back to the practice squad the very next day, on the 31st. He’s been called up for just one game so far. Week 1 against Seattle, where he returned two kickoffs for 49 yards.

Now, he’s not going to be the lead back. Stevenson has 77 carries. Cory Ker has 35 carries for 151 yards. Larison will be behind both of them.

The likely read is that he gets a few carries, probably a couple of kick returns, and a chance to show he’s more than just a name on the practice squad. But that’s a read. It’s not a report. Nobody has said what his role will be on Sunday.

And again, that’s what makes the signing so interesting. He had two practice squad elevations left. So why sign him? It could just be that the Patriots want to protect him from other teams. Practice squad players can be signed away by anyone. Putting him on the 53 keeps him in Foxborough. Or it could mean the team sees something in him. Either way, it’s a decision worth watching.

And here’s why special teams might be where these two players matter the most.

Because the last time these teams met in Miami, special teams decided the game.

It was September 2025. The Patriots won 33 to 27. At one point, Miami’s Malik Washington returned a punt 74 yards for a touchdown to put the Dolphins up 27 to 23.

And if you watched the Patriots last year, you know what happened next. On the very next kickoff, Antonio Gibson took it back 90 yards for a Patriots touchdown. That gave New England the lead, and it put Gibson in the hearts of every Patriots fan.

Gibson is gone now. His season ended last year with a knee injury. But that play will always be remembered.

And this year, the Patriots needed a returner, and at least 10 players have auditioned for the job. Mike Vrabel said this week that the return game was close after the Jets game, but a couple of returns against Chicago weren’t very effective. He said they want to create some positive momentum in the return game, whether it’s with Cory Ker, Kyle Williams, or anybody else.

That’s exactly where Larison, with his kickoff returns from Week 1, and Miller, with his 421 special teams snaps, could come in.

And remember, Vrabel specifically warned about Malik Washington this week. He mentioned that 74-yard return against the Patriots last year. So the coverage teams have to be ready too.

Last season, the AP reported in November that Miami ranked last in the NFL in punt coverage, kickoff coverage, and kickoff returns. That was last year, of course. But it shows why a kick return can matter so much in this matchup.

Now, let’s look at who’s on the other side.

The Dolphins are 1 and 5. Their only win came October 25th, 28 to 25 over the Jets.

Their offense scores 16.3 points per game. That’s 30th in the NFL. They throw for just 162 yards per game, also 30th. Malik Willis has started all six games, the same quarterback who threw Miller that 77-yard touchdown.

But Vrabel warned this week that Miami has run for 185 yards per game over the last two weeks, and that Willis can hurt you on designed runs and by extending plays. And he said running back Ollie Gordon has four touchdowns in the last four games at 4.8 yards a carry. So this offense isn’t harmless, especially on the ground.

On defense, the Dolphins are giving up 26.2 points per game, 25th in the NFL. They have just six sacks all season. That’s dead last in the league. And they have only five takeaways, 29th in the league.

So in theory, the Patriots shouldn’t have to worry too much about giving the ball away. And that’s big. Because the Patriots still haven’t won the turnover battle in a single game this season. If there’s ever a game to finally win it, it’s this one.

And that matters even more because Drake Maye is playing some of his best football. He’s thrown at least two touchdown passes in each of his last four games, with 12 touchdowns over that stretch.

But the Patriots haven’t been perfect away from home. They’re 1 and 3 on the road this season, with losses to Seattle, Jacksonville, and Chicago. And the Jacksonville game was ugly, a 35 to 6 loss. So the last time the Patriots were in Florida, it left a bad taste. This time, Maye has a chance to change that.

And history is on New England’s side. Last season, the Patriots outscored Miami 71 to 37 in two games. In the second one, last January, it was a 38 to 10 win. Stevenson ran for 131 yards and two touchdowns, and Henry had five catches for 56 yards. And the last time Maye played in Miami, he went 19 of 23 for 230 yards and two touchdowns, with no interceptions.

So even with all these injuries, a Patriots win isn’t exactly a long shot.
