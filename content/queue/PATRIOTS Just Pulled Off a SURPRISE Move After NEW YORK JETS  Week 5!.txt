Let’s start with the surprise move itself. A.J. Brown.

When Vrabel was asked whether Brown would play, his answer was honest. The team wouldn’t make that decision until the next day. They had to see how Brown responded to that day’s practice, and go from there. And he said Brown wasn’t the only one in that spot.

When he was asked how Brown looked in practice that week, Vrabel kept it real. He said Brown looked okay. Like everybody, some good plays, some bad plays. And he said they were trying to figure out where his conditioning level was, and how he’d feel after practice.

Remember that word. Conditioning. Because it turned out to be the whole story.

The Patriots made the call. Brown played. And his first game back was rough. Three catches on 10 targets for 27 yards. A few passes he normally secures. And one of the reported mistakes came on the Jets’ onside kick attempt at the end.

So was activating him the wrong move? I don’t think so, and here’s why. The Patriots knew Brown needed game reps to get his timing back. You can’t practice your way into game shape. At some point, you have to play. Ten targets show the staff trusted him and tried to work him back in. The results weren’t there yet. But that’s what a first game back after weeks away often looks like.

And Brown handled it like a pro. He didn’t blame the ankle. He said, no excuses, ankle or not, he should make those plays. Vrabel didn’t hide from it either. He said Brown could have come down with more balls, acknowledged how long he’d been out, and kept his confidence in what Brown can do this season.

That’s exactly what Vrabel was hinting at before the game. Some good plays, some bad plays. Conditioning still coming. The coach knew. And now the question is how fast Brown gets back to being Brown, with a short week and Chicago coming on Thursday night.

Now let’s go back to the injury report Vrabel gave before the game. Because looking at it now, it explains a lot about how Sunday played out.

Vrabel said they had to see how Will responded. That’s Will Campbell, the left tackle who hurt his knee against the Raiders. He said there’d be some guys listed as questionable, and they’d see how TreVeyon responded in the morning. And he said for certain that Carlton Davis and Karan Prunty would be out.

So going into the Jets game, this team was already thin at cornerback, thin at running back, and uncertain at left tackle. And then, during the game, it got worse. Right tackle Morgan Moses hurt his right ankle in the second quarter. He went to the medical tent, then the locker room. He briefly came back to the sideline after halftime, but he was ruled out at the start of the fourth quarter. After the game, he was in a walking boot.

And still, the Patriots found a way to win. That’s what Vrabel has been building all season. Next man up isn’t a slogan in Foxborough. It’s how this team survives.

Now here’s a part of the press conference that looks really interesting in hindsight.

Vrabel was asked about Alijah Vera-Tucker. The left guard who’d played every single snap this season, and had been with the team through the offseason.

And think about who the Patriots were playing that week. The New York Jets. Vera-Tucker’s former team. The team that drafted him in the first round. Going up against your old team always carries a little extra fire.

Vrabel said playing every snap has really meant a lot to Vera-Tucker. He said he’s seen him improve. And then he said this was always the hope and the vision. That Vera-Tucker and Will Campbell could keep developing together and really solidify the left side of the offensive line. Two players the team believes are talented, and who still have room to grow.

That’s a big statement. That’s not a stopgap. That’s a plan. The left side of this offensive line, built around two players growing together.

Vrabel also said he can see how much it means to Vera-Tucker in the way he prepares and the way he responds. And his message was simple. Keep moving forward. Keep trying to progress.

And with Campbell out and Moses going down in the middle of the game, a guard who’s played every snap this season becomes even more valuable. He’s the steady piece in a line that keeps getting reshuffled. When a young tackle comes in next to you, the guard is the one calling out stunts and keeping everybody on the same page. That’s how a line survives a day like Sunday.

Now let’s talk about the rookie who keeps getting called into the fire. Caleb Lomu.

There was a fun moment in the press conference. A reporter brought up that Lomu didn’t do so well with his Friday update the week before. And Vrabel laughed. He said it was much better this time. He said Lomu had some good laughs, and that he came back strong that week.

Small moment. But it tells you something. Lomu is getting comfortable. He’s loosening up. And the timing couldn’t have been better. Because on Sunday, when Moses went down, it was Lomu who stepped in at right tackle.

Think about this kid’s last two weeks. Against the Raiders, he played 47 snaps at left tackle in relief of Campbell, allowed one pressure on 29 pass-blocking snaps, and the Patriots held Maxx Crosby without a sack. Then against the Jets, he stepped in on the right side in the middle of a division game.

Left side one week. Right side the next. Both times in the middle of a game, with no warning. That’s a first-round pick earning his keep.

And Vrabel’s comments on the rookie class fit perfectly here. He said the rookies have improved, and they’ve played in some good spots. He reminded everybody they’re 21- and 22-year-old kids, so it’s good to see them get better. And he said the team has to keep investing in them, and can’t lose sight of them, even if they’re not playing 80 percent of the snaps.

He mentioned Eli. He mentioned Caleb. He mentioned the whole group. And he talked about Karan gaining confidence as a gunner on special teams. Vrabel said it’s exciting. And then he said the rookies are very coachable. Much more coachable than some of the veterans. That’s why he likes coaching them.

Patriots Nation, that’s a coach who loves developing young players. And you’re seeing the results on the field every week.

Now let’s talk about the veterans, because Vrabel had high praise there too.

He was asked about Draymond Jones, who’s battled through an injury to get on the field this season. And Vrabel got a little personal. He said they’re from the same area. Growing up in Ohio, Jones went to the other Catholic high school, the Jesuit school in Cleveland, and he got to Ohio State after Vrabel did. So Vrabel has followed his career for years.

Vrabel said he’s always admired the way Jones plays. His versatility. He called him a real professional. Someone who knows his stuff, and has a good grasp of exactly what he needs to do in the game plan. And he said it’s been good to have Jones in a room full of young players, because of his experience.

That’s important on the edge, where Jones works alongside Elijah Ponder and rookie Gabe Jacas. A veteran who can teach the young guys what to look for, how to prepare, how to handle a game plan. That’s how a young pass rush grows up fast.

And then there was Harold Landry. Vrabel was asked whether the plan is for Landry to return at some point this season.

Vrabel said of course the plan would be for him to return, when he’s ready. He said Landry’s been around the team and he’s working extremely hard. He just isn’t ready yet.

And Vrabel described his daily routine. Landry comes in every single day. He does his part being around the team in the morning, and then goes and does his rehab. Vrabel said they just have to be patient. He’s not ready, so they’re not going to put him out there. But when he’s ready, that’s the plan.

Think about what Landry could add whenever he does return. Vrabel coached him in Tennessee. He knows exactly what Landry can do off the edge. Add a veteran like that to a pass rush that’s already producing with Ponder, Jacas, and Jones, and this defense could get a lot more dangerous down the stretch.

Now, there was another really interesting part of the press conference that most people skipped right over. Knee braces.

A reporter mentioned that, talking to some players, it seemed like Vrabel had mandated knee braces for offensive linemen in practice, at least during training camp. And he asked about Vrabel’s philosophy on braces in practice versus games.

Vrabel said it’s something they’ve done during padded practices in training camp. And then he explained his thinking. He said there are a lot of reports and studies suggesting the impact is minor. Potentially it helps with something like an MCL injury, but not as it relates to an ACL. And he said it’s not something he’s ever done for games, to mandate knee braces on game day.

Now, why does that matter? Because look at what’s happening to this offensive line. Will Campbell is dealing with a knee injury, the same knee where he had a grade 3 MCL sprain last season. Linemen are going down left and right. And here’s a head coach who’s clearly thought about how to protect his players’ knees. It shows a staff that pays attention to the little things. In practice, they take extra precautions. On game day, they let the players decide.

It’s a small detail. But small details are what Vrabel’s whole program is built on.

Now let’s get to the running backs, because Vrabel’s comments here look even better after Sunday.

First, Rhamondre Stevenson. Vrabel admitted he didn’t know Stevenson before he got the job. But he said Stevenson is a talented player who cares a lot about the team. A great teammate. A good skill set, catching and running.

And then he said what stood out to him on tape, before he ever met him, was what kind of pass protector Stevenson was. The way he played without the football. His willingness to make blocks for other people when the ball wasn’t in his hands. Vrabel said those are usually a good sign of a guy who cares about the team, who’ll do whatever he has to do.

Patriots Nation, think about how much that matters right now. With tackles going down and rookies stepping in, the running back’s pass protection becomes a lifeline for Drake Maye. A back who picks up the blitz is the difference between a completion and a sack. And Vrabel knew that about Stevenson from the very first tape he watched.

Then Vrabel talked about another back who breaks tackles. He said usually the first guy doesn’t bring him down. A lot of his explosive runs have come after contact, when somebody thinks they’ve got him, and they don’t. He called him a smooth runner who doesn’t look rigid, and said he’s liked him since college.

And then the big one. Cory Ker. After that fumble against the Raiders, Vrabel was asked about the level of trust in him.

His answer was excellent. He said everybody has to take care of the ball. But Ker did a lot of really good things, so there’s high confidence. And then he turned it around on the media. He said nobody ever talks about the defender who comes back into the gap and hammers the ball out. Everyone just puts it on the guy holding the ball.

Vrabel said they all share in that, and they’ll be better. He likes the way Ker runs. Yes, Ker has to hang on to the ball. But the team also has to do a better job protecting him and keeping defenders away from him.

He also said Ker works extremely hard. It’s a new system for him, and he didn’t have the luxury of being here since April like a lot of guys. So he puts in extra work with running backs coach Tony Dews. And Vrabel said he loves Ker’s spirit and energy. He’s willing to do whatever is asked of him. He said they’d try to get him more runs and maybe use him on special teams.

With Henderson’s ankle injury, that kind of depth isn’t a luxury. It’s a necessity.

Vrabel also gave an update on Marcus Bryant, the offensive lineman who went on injured reserve before Week 1 with a return designation. He said Bryant was another guy they’d look at in the morning, to see about his elevation, and see how he felt after a few days of work.

And after what happened to Morgan Moses against the Jets, Bryant’s status matters more than ever. This line needs bodies. Every healthy tackle counts right now.

And finally, special teams. Vrabel was asked about Brenden Schooler coming back, after the special teams coach had been full of praise for him. And Vrabel said you can hear how much special teams means to Schooler. You can hear him communicating. He understands the concepts and the scheme, and he can communicate that to everybody. There’s an energy there. And then there’s the physical skill set. He’s fast, and he can play multiple positions.

And think about how Sunday ended. The Jets went for an onside kick in the final minute, trying to steal one more possession. And DeMario Douglas recovered it to seal the win. Special teams decided the end of that game. That’s exactly why Vrabel values guys like Schooler who bring energy and communication to that unit.

So let’s put it all together.

Before the Jets game, Vrabel laid out the plan. Brown’s status would come down to his conditioning. Campbell and Henderson would be game-time evaluations. Davis and Prunty were out. And he praised the guys he trusts. Vera-Tucker on the left side. Stevenson for his blocking. Cory Ker despite the fumble. Draymond Jones for his professionalism. And a rookie class he called more coachable than some veterans.

Then came the surprise move. Brown activated and on the field. A rough day for him, but a necessary step. And when Moses went down, the depth Vrabel had been building got tested again, and the team still won.

That’s a coach with a plan. A coach who’s honest about his players. And a team that keeps finding ways to win, even when the injury report keeps growing.

Now comes the short week. Chicago on Thursday night. Moses in a walking boot. Lomu possibly starting at right tackle. Brown trying to find his rhythm. And a team riding a three-game winning streak.
