Let’s start with the news everybody’s talking about. Tyreek Hill.

Think about the history here. For years, Patriots fans had to watch this man torch New England. In 11 games against the Patriots, he put up 928 yards and seven touchdowns. Every time Miami came on the schedule, the first question was always the same. How do we stop Tyreek Hill? And most of the time, there was no good answer.

Now? He’s on our side.

When the Dolphins released him, the whole league started asking where the six-time All-Pro would land. And now Patriots fans finally get to stop worrying about covering him, and start dreaming about what he’s going to do in this offense.

And let me tell you why this fit is so scary. Look at what Drake Maye just did. On throws of 20 yards or more against the Raiders, he went 4 of 6 for 149 yards and two touchdowns. His deep passing grade from Pro Football Focus came out at 99.9. The only other quarterbacks to hit that mark in 2026? Jordan Love and Patrick Mahomes.

And Mahomes knows exactly what it looks like to throw deep to Tyreek Hill. That’s where Hill turned into a superstar. A cannon-armed quarterback launching it down the field, and the fastest man in the building running under it.

Now picture that in Foxborough. Maye’s arm. Hill’s speed. Defenses that already have to deal with A.J. Brown once he’s healthy, with DeMario Douglas, with Kyle Williams, with Hunter Henry. Where does the safety go? Who’s the double team on? You can’t take away everything. And Hill is the kind of player who doesn’t need you to make a mistake. He just needs you to be one step slow. And everybody is one step slow against Tyreek Hill.

Here’s what I think gets overlooked. Hill doesn’t just make plays himself. He changes the entire geometry of the field for everyone else. When a defense knows Hill can take the top off on any snap, the safeties back up. The corners give cushion. And everything underneath opens up. The intermediate throws. The crossers. The run game with lighter boxes. Hill makes the whole offense better just by lining up.

Now, I’m going to be honest with you, because that’s what we do on this channel. Hill is coming off a serious leg injury that ended his last season, and he’s not 25 anymore. So there will be people out there saying he’s not the same guy. That’s fair to watch. But I’ll say this. Even a version of Tyreek Hill that’s a half-step slower than his prime is still faster than almost every corner in the NFL. And in this offense, with this quarterback throwing the deep ball like this, I genuinely believe he’s going to do incredible things.

And here’s the best part. Circle the calendar. After the Jets, and after Chicago on Thursday night, the Patriots head down to Miami. Tyreek Hill, back in Miami, against the team that let him go. If you don’t think he’s going to have that one circled, you don’t know Tyreek Hill.

Now let’s talk about why this is even possible. Because Hill only matters if the quarterback can get him the ball. And on Sunday, Drake Maye showed us he’s back. Not kind of back. All the way back.

Maye went 26 of 34 for 374 yards, four touchdown passes, no interceptions, and he ran one in himself. He threw his first touchdown just minutes into the game, and the Raiders never really caught up.

PFF gave him a 93.5 passing grade in its initial review. That’s a new career high. He had five big-time throws, the third most by any quarterback in a single game this season. And zero turnover-worthy plays. Zero. A quarterback who had been giving the ball away all season didn’t put the ball in danger one single time.

And before anybody says “it was just the Raiders,” stop. This was not a soft opponent. Las Vegas walked into Foxborough ranked fourth in defensive EPA per play. Through four weeks, that defense had allowed an 80.1 passer rating and only 18 explosive plays total. Eighteen in four games. Maye produced 10 explosive plays in this one game alone. Ten. More than half of what that defense had allowed all season, in one afternoon.

And this isn’t a one-week fluke. The turnaround is real, and the numbers prove it. Through Week 3, his PFF passing grade was 36.6. That was the lowest among 26 qualified quarterbacks. Dead last. Over the last two weeks? His 92.6 passing grade is tied for the best in the NFL. From last to first. In two weeks.

His turnover-worthy play rate has dropped from a league-high 8.6 percent all the way down to 1.1 percent. That’s not small. That’s a completely different quarterback.

Against the blitz, he went from a 36.8 grade with zero big-time throws to a 90.4 grade with a league-high four. Defenses used to blitz Maye to rattle him. Now when they blitz him, he makes them pay.

And here’s one detail that jumped out at me. When teams blitz him now, he’s holding the ball about 3.05 seconds, up from 2.80 early in the season. Now normally, holding the ball longer against the blitz sounds like a bad thing. But PFF reads it as comfort in the pocket. He’s not panicking. He’s not bailing out early. He’s standing in, reading it out, and delivering.

Mike Vrabel saw the same thing. After the game he said, “I felt like he was in control. Gave him some time.”

Notice that last part. Gave him some time. That’s the key to everything. And that’s exactly why what happened next matters so much.

The play itself was ordinary. A second-quarter run, with Will Campbell blocking for Rhamondre Stevenson. According to the Boston Herald, Campbell got rolled up by multiple players. NESN’s angle showed Efton Chism III appearing to land on his leg. Either way, Campbell immediately grabbed his right knee and waved toward the sideline.

He went into the blue medical tent, then to the locker room. Later, he came back to the sideline, put his helmet on, rode the stationary bike, and even worked through some pass-blocking sets. Every Patriots fan watching was hoping. But he never came out after halftime, and he was officially ruled out midway through the third quarter.

Ian Rapoport of NFL Network reported that Campbell is believed to have suffered a knee sprain that will likely cause him to miss some time. An MRI was scheduled for Monday to confirm how serious it is.

And Patriots Nation, here’s why this one hits harder than a normal sprain. It’s the same right knee that ended his rookie regular season.

Let’s go back. Campbell was the fourth overall pick out of LSU in 2025. He started the first 12 games of his rookie year. Then against Cincinnati, he suffered a grade 3 MCL sprain in that same knee, was carted off, and placed on injured reserve. He came back after the four-game minimum and played most of the regular season finale.

Then came the postseason. And it was brutal. PFF gave him three of the four worst grades of his rookie season in the Divisional Round, the AFC Championship, and the Super Bowl. Maye was sacked five times in every playoff game leading up to the Super Bowl, and then six more times in the Super Bowl itself.

It was later revealed that Campbell played that entire run with a torn ligament in his knee. Think about that. A rookie, on the biggest stage in football, playing on a torn ligament for his team. And he took serious national criticism all offseason for it.

And then he answered it. Entering Week 5, PFF had him as the 14th best tackle in the league, and Musket Fire noted his pass blocking alone ranked 12th. Sports Illustrated counted only 11 pressures and one sack allowed in more than four games.

So this isn’t just a starter going down. This is a young player in the middle of a redemption season, hurting the knee he already gave so much for. Honestly, that’s the part that stings.

And Campbell wasn’t the only loss on that drive. TreVeyon Henderson came up hobbling after an 11-yard jet sweep when safety Jeremy Chinn appeared to land on his lower leg. Henderson had four carries for 22 yards and a 16-yard catch before he left with a right ankle injury. As of Sunday night, there was no clear diagnosis.

But the backfield still delivered. Stevenson, questionable before the game with a knee issue, finished with 64 yards and a touchdown on 13 carries. That’s almost five yards a carry from a guy people were worried about all week.

And third-stringer Cory Ker had a wild afternoon. He fumbled on his very first carry. Rough start. But he bounced back with runs of 10 and 32 yards in the fourth quarter. That’s resilience. That’s a young guy who didn’t let one mistake define his day.

But the real survival story, Patriots Nation, is at left tackle. And this is star number two.

Caleb Lomu was the 28th overall pick in April, and the Patriots traded up to get him. He played left tackle at Utah. But this staff had been using him as a swing piece on the right side and in jumbo packages, with an eye on eventually replacing 35-year-old Morgan Moses at right tackle.

Before Sunday, he’d taken only 50 offensive snaps all season. Fifty. That’s it.

On Sunday, he played 47 snaps. A career high. At the most important spot on the line, protecting the blind side of a quarterback who was in the middle of a career game.

On 29 pass-blocking snaps, he allowed one pressure and no quarterback hits.

And here’s the part almost nobody is talking about. As a team, the Patriots held Maxx Crosby to one tackle, two quarterback hits, and zero sacks. Maxx Crosby. One of the most relentless pass rushers in the NFL. Zero sacks. With a rookie who’d barely played stepping into the left tackle spot.

That’s not survival. That’s a statement.

Vrabel made a point of it afterward. He said, “Guys stepped up. Caleb had to go in there, and we had all hands on deck.”

And Lomu’s own explanation was simple. He said Vrabel tells everyone to prepare like a starter all week, because things like this can happen. That’s a culture thing. That’s a coach who makes sure the backup is ready before he’s ever needed. And on Sunday, it paid off big.

So here’s where we land on Patriots Floor. If Campbell misses time, we want Lomu at left tackle. Full stop. Don’t reshuffle three spots to cover one hole. He played the position in college, and he just held up against one of the best pass rushers in football. Why move three guys around and create three new problems, when you’ve got a guy who just proved he can handle it?

And we’d rather see Campbell sit an extra week than come back early. We already watched what playing hurt did to him last January. We watched Maye get buried in the playoffs. Nobody wants a repeat of that. Let the knee heal. Let the rookie hold the fort.

Now, to be fair, there are a few things we don’t know yet, and I’m not going to pretend otherwise.

First, the MRI result. As Musket Fire put it, a milder sprain could heal in a few weeks, while the most severe kind could cost Campbell a good chunk of the season. Until the team or a reliable reporter says otherwise, nobody knows which one this is. Anybody telling you they know for sure is guessing.

Second, 47 snaps is a small sample. The Raiders were chasing the game, and when a team is down big, the rush gets predictable. Next week, a division rival will have a full week of film to design pressures aimed straight at a rookie making his first start. That’s a very different test.

Third, the depth is getting thin. Mike Onwenu and Greg Van Roten are already on injured reserve. The team could open the 21-day practice window for Marcus Bryant, who went on IR before Week 1 with a return designation, but that hadn’t happened as of these reports. And Draymond Jones and Charles Woods also left Sunday’s game hurt. Injuries are stacking up, and at some point, depth gets tested everywhere.

So what comes next? Practice resumes Wednesday, and Sports Illustrated says Campbell almost assuredly won’t play against the Jets at Gillette on Sunday. That likely means Caleb Lomu’s first professional start.

And the schedule doesn’t let up. After the Jets, it’s Chicago on Thursday night. Then a road trip to Miami. That’s why Musket Fire warned this injury could cost Campbell key division games.

But think about that Miami game again. If things go right, that’s Tyreek Hill’s homecoming. In a Patriots uniform. With a rookie protecting Maye’s blind side, and Maye throwing deep like he’s never thrown before. That’s must-watch football.

Vrabel’s message to the locker room was blunt. None of Sunday’s success will matter against the Jets. And from a coach who preaches that the last play has no bearing on the next one, that sounds like a weekly rule. Celebrate Sunday, then forget it. Every week starts at zero.

So here’s where things stand, Patriots Nation.

The quarterback looks like himself again, and the numbers say it’s real. From the lowest graded quarterback in the league to tied for the best in two weeks. Ten explosive plays against a top-five defense. A 99.9 deep passing grade.

A superstar receiver in Tyreek Hill is coming to town, and if Maye keeps throwing it like this, the two of them could do things this fan base hasn’t seen in a long time.

And a rookie tackle in Caleb Lomu just held Maxx Crosby without a sack, and is about to get his first real start.

But Maye did it behind a clean pocket. And the man who protected his blind side is now waiting on an MRI for the same knee he played through on football’s biggest stage. Whether this season keeps climbing might come down to a rookie who wasn’t supposed to be a starter yet.

That’s the story. Two stars. One built the offense’s present on Sunday. The other might build its future. And both of them make this Patriots team a lot scarier than anybody expected.
