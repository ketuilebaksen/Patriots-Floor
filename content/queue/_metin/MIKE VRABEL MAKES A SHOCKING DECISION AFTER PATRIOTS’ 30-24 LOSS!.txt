MIKE VRABEL MAKES A SHOCKING DECISION AFTER PATRIOTS’ 30-24 LOSS!

Patriots Nation, it’s been a few days since the Patriots lost in Chicago, 30 to 24. And the more you look back at what Mike Vrabel and Drake Maye said after that game, the more it tells you about where this team is headed.

Because buried in that press conference was one of the most unusual admissions you’ll ever hear from an NFL head coach. Vrabel revealed that late in the game, on a short-yardage third down, he told one of his players to jump offside. On purpose. A deliberate penalty, as a strategic call.

It didn’t work out the way he wanted. The Bears’ kicker, Cairo Santos, went on to drill a huge 58-yard field goal. And Vrabel stood at the podium and said three words most coaches avoid at all costs.

That’s on me.

So today on Patriots Floor, we’re taking a deeper look. Not just at what Vrabel and Maye said, but at what this loss revealed, everything that’s come out since, and what it all means with Miami next and the trade deadline coming fast.

Let’s start with that decision, because it says so much about this coach.

Vrabel was asked about the challenge flag he threw on Drake Maye’s touchdown run, the one that was initially not ruled a touchdown. And he explained it simply. Someone on the staff had a good view of the play and told him to throw it. So he threw it. And it worked. The touchdown stood.

But then, without even being asked, Vrabel added something. He said, for the record, the team told Cory to jump offside on a third-and-inches situation. He said they felt it was the best way to get what they needed in that moment. And he said, that’s on me. The guys executed what they were told. And then Cairo Santos made a huge kick.

Think about that. Vrabel didn’t have to bring it up. Nobody asked him about it directly. He volunteered it. He wanted everyone to know that the penalty wasn’t a player’s mistake. It was his call.

Patriots Nation, that’s what accountability looks like. A head coach who stands in front of the cameras after a loss and protects his players by taking the hit himself.

And we’ve seen this pattern from Vrabel all season. When Cory Ker fumbled against the Raiders, Vrabel defended him. He said nobody ever talks about the defender who comes back into the gap and hammers the ball out. Everyone just blames the guy holding it. Then he handed Ker the ball eight straight times to close out the Jets.

When Drake Maye threw an interception in the end zone against the Bears, Vrabel said, “I’m glad he’s ours.”

And now, when a strategic penalty doesn’t work out, he says, “That’s on me.”

This is a coach who builds trust by carrying the weight himself.

And Vrabel had a moment of respect for the kicker who beat him. He coached Santos before, back in Tennessee. He joked that he thinks the last field goal Santos missed was when he kicked for the Titans. Then he corrected himself, since Santos actually missed one earlier in this game. But Vrabel said he’s really proud of what Santos has done. He just didn’t want him to make that one.

And when a reporter suggested the Bears out-executed the Patriots down the stretch, Vrabel pushed back a little. He said he doesn’t know how you call it getting out-executed when a guy makes a 58-yard field goal. He said the Patriots executed well at times. They just didn’t get enough drives stopped.

Now let’s look at what actually decided this game. And Vrabel was crystal clear about it.

He said it’s a thin margin when you play good teams. The Patriots did some good things. But they didn’t get enough stops in the red zone. And they didn’t convert their own red zone opportunity, the one time it mattered most. He said that’s probably the difference in the game.

The red zone. On both sides of the ball.

And then there was the tackling. Vrabel didn’t sugarcoat it. He said the tackling wasn’t good enough. Not against this team. Whether it was D’Andre Swift, Luther Burden, or Caleb Williams, missed tackles led to extra yards and extra conversions.

And the numbers since the game back that up. According to PFF, Marcus Jones alone was credited with six missed tackles in Chicago. And Jones has been forced to play outside cornerback far more than usual, with Carlton Davis III missing his third straight game with a neck injury. Jones has already played 93 snaps outside this season, compared to just 60 in all of last season. His coverage grades against the Raiders and Bears were 48.8 and 37.4.

That’s a great slot cornerback being asked to do a job that doesn’t fit his strengths, because the depth isn’t there. And Vrabel refused to use the injuries as an excuse. When he was asked if all the injuries in the secondary were catching up to the team, he said he’s not going to say that. They knew who was going to play.

But the injuries are real. And getting Davis back for Miami could change a lot.

And Vrabel called tackling and taking the ball away fundamental skills. Basic football. The stuff that wins close games.

Now, let’s talk about Drake Maye. Because this game showed both sides of him.

The good Maye threw for 321 yards and two touchdowns, behind a line with a guard making his first career start and another who was on the practice squad just three weeks ago. That brought him to 1,784 passing yards on the season, temporarily second in the entire NFL, just 10 yards behind Matthew Stafford.

And look at his turnaround. In his first three games, he completed 51 of 80 passes for 585 yards, with one touchdown and six interceptions. Over the next four games, he completed 90 of 134 passes for 1,199 yards. That’s a massive jump.

The bad the interception in the end zone, on a throw intended for Eli Raridon.

And Maye owned it completely. He said it was a huge point in the game. One of those plays that can swing everything. And when asked whether the decision or the throw was the problem, he gave an interesting answer. He said they actually hit the exact same play later in the game. So he liked his chances. But it was frustrating. And then he said it.

“I let my team down.”

And Vrabel had his back. He said Maye gave them a chance. He competed. Whether he was scrambling or finding good places to go with the football. Vrabel said Maye will learn from it like he always does. And then, “I’m glad he’s ours.”

Maye also talked about the end of the game. Down 27 to 21, the Patriots got the ball back twice with a chance to take the lead. And they couldn’t do it. Maye pointed to penalties, and to himself not operating at a high enough level. And especially one sequence. After the missed field goal, there was a three-and-out. A bad three-and-out, he called it.

He said that’s what he’ll be thinking about all weekend. Two chances to go score and trust the defense. He’s proud of the times the team has done that, like in Buffalo. This time, they fell short.

Now, here’s what I loved most about Maye’s press conference. The football IQ.

He explained the big play right before halftime, the 48-yard touchdown catch by Rhamondre Stevenson. Maye said he had a feeling the Bears were going to blitz. With five players out in the pattern and the Bears bringing five, that left four blockers and Stevenson with the ball against six defenders. And all it takes is one cut. He said that’s exactly how they drew it up, and they’ve been practicing that play since OTAs.

Stevenson finished that game with five catches, 93 yards, and a touchdown. A career high in receiving yards.

And Maye talked about how the Bears’ pressure gave them problems early. He said they showed a different look on third down, out of a package they’d shown a couple of times but mixed up. And he put it on himself to have the answers. Get the ball out faster, so his linemen aren’t holding blocks for so long. Be the conductor at the line of scrimmage.

And on his touchdown run, the one Vrabel challenged, Maye admitted he probably shouldn’t have reached the ball out. He knows the coaches will be on him about it. Because you can’t risk the ball that close to the goal line. Especially on a team that’s struggled with turnovers all season.

That’s a quarterback who’s thinking about the details. And that’s what turns a hot streak into a consistent season.

And remember Hunter Henry in this game. Seven catches on seven targets for 71 yards. Every single ball thrown his way, caught. Vrabel has called him a consistent presence and a veteran leader. He proved it again.

Now, let’s talk about the problem that’s been bothering everybody. The run game.

Vrabel was honest. He said they didn’t run the ball well enough. He thought they’d be able to run it better. But their best players were putting the ball in good spots and making good decisions. So they moved the ball however they needed to.

And Maye agreed. He said they creased the Bears once with Rhamondre. But when the defense loads the box and forces you to throw, you go throw for a bunch of yards. Still, he said they definitely have to get the run game going.

Take out Maye’s scrambles, and the Patriots ran for 44 yards on 13 carries. Stevenson had six carries for 22 yards. TreVeyon Henderson had three carries for nine yards. Cory Ker had four for 13.

Vrabel explained Henderson’s small role. He said they had certain packages and personnel groupings for him, and he’ll help them. But they also wanted to see what they had with Ker getting some carries.

And the offensive line was a big part of it. Walter Rouse made his first NFL start at left guard, with Matt Prior at right guard. Vrabel said the protection could probably have been better. But the bigger problem was critical penalties. Four of the Patriots’ 10 penalties were on offensive linemen. Vrabel called it beating ourselves. But he said he knows they competed.

So, what’s happened since this loss A lot.

On the injury front, it’s gotten worse before it gets better. Alijah Vera-Tucker went on injured reserve before the Bears game, and he’s out for at least four games. Mike Onwenu is out for the season. Greg Van Roten is on IR. Ben Brown is still dealing with a knee injury. Through six games, the Patriots had used 14 different offensive line combinations.

But there’s real hope in some of the young players.

Caleb Lomu, the first-round rookie tackle, has stepped in twice for extended stretches, on both the left and right side. Across 160 offensive snaps, he’s allowed just one pressure. Vrabel has been careful with his words, reminding everyone that Lomu is 21 and learning a hard position. But he also said Lomu has improved. And for now, he’s keeping him behind Will Campbell and Morgan Moses. Patience, by design.

And on defense, rookie edge rusher Gabe Jacas had five quarterback pressures against the Bears. Probably the best game of his young career. At one point, he drove the Bears’ left tackle straight back with a bull rush. Pair him with Elijah Ponder, who has five and a half sacks, and this pass rush is getting dangerous.

And then there’s the trade market. Because the Patriots’ front office has been linked to some big names.

Quenton Nelson, the highest-graded guard in the NFL, with no sacks allowed this season. Jonathan Taylor, the running back with seven rushing touchdowns. DeForest Buckner, the defensive tackle. All from a Colts team that just fired its GM and has started 2 and 4. And Albert Breer has named the Patriots as a possible fit for Buckner.

And ESPN’s Bill Barnwell suggested a cheaper option. Andrew Wylie, a two-time Super Bowl champion offensive lineman from Washington, for just a late-round pick swap.

None of these deals are done. No talks have been reported. But the deadline is November 10th. And the Patriots don’t play again until November 1st in Miami. So the phones should be ringing.

And finally, let’s end with something Maye said that really stuck with me.

He was asked what he enjoys about facing an elite young quarterback like Caleb Williams. And he said he loves competing. He loves being out there with the guys. He loves the challenge, the nerves, the pressure situations. He said there’s nothing else like that in life. You get a few hours a week to prepare for those moments. Sometimes you make them, and sometimes you wish you had them back. He called football such a special sport.

He said it was well fought. Hard fought. And they just came up short. A stinger for the weekend.

That’s a young quarterback who loves the game, who hates losing, and who’s going to come back hungrier.
