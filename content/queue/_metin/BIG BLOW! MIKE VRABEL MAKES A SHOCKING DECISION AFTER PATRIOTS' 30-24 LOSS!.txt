BIG BLOW! MIKE VRABEL MAKES A SHOCKING DECISION AFTER PATRIOTS’ 30-24 LOSS!

Pats Nation, it hurts. The Patriots lost to the Bears, 30 to 24. The three-game winning streak is over. And this one stings, because this was a game New England had every chance to win.

But what happened after the game might tell us even more than the game itself. Because Mike Vrabel stood at the podium and admitted to a decision that most coaches would never say out loud. A deliberate call late in the game, one that didn’t work out, and he put it squarely on himself. And Drake Maye stood up there and said four words no franchise quarterback ever wants to say. “I let my team down.”

So today on Patriots Floor, we’re going through everything Vrabel and Maye said after this loss. What went wrong. What went right. And what has to change before a division road game next week.

Let’s start with the shocking decision. Because this is the moment everybody’s talking about.

Late in the game, on a third down for the Bears, the Patriots defense jumped offside. And it wasn’t an accident. Vrabel revealed after the game that the coaching staff told one of their defenders to jump offside on purpose, on a third-and-six situation.

Vrabel explained that they felt it was the best way to get the stop they needed in that situation. It was a calculated move. A gamble. And then Vrabel said the words that tell you everything about him as a coach. He said, that’s on me.

And his players executed exactly what he asked for. But then the Bears’ kicker, Cairo Santos, stepped up and drilled a huge 58-yard field goal.

Patriots Nation, think about how rare that is. A head coach, after a loss, openly admitting that one of his own unusual decisions was part of the sequence that went wrong. No hiding. No blaming the players. No blaming the officials. That’s on me.

And Vrabel even had a little moment of respect for the kicker. He coached Santos before, back in Tennessee. And he joked that he thinks the last field goal Santos missed was when he kicked for the Titans. Well, he actually missed one earlier in this game too. But Vrabel said he’s really proud of what Santos has done. He just didn’t want him to make that one tonight. Former guy he coached, making the kick that hurt him.

When a reporter asked if this was a game where the Bears simply out-executed the Patriots down the stretch, Vrabel pushed back a little. He said he doesn’t know how you call it getting out-executed when a guy makes a 58-yard field goal. He said the Patriots executed well at times. They just didn’t get enough drives stopped.

Now, the biggest problem in this game, according to Vrabel, was simple. Tackling.

He was asked what he made of the defensive tackling, and he didn’t sugarcoat it. He said the tackling wasn’t good enough. Not against this team.

He said they knew coming in that tackling would be a challenge, especially on a Thursday night. But against this team, whether it’s D’Andre Swift, Luther Burden, or the quarterback, Caleb Williams, missed tackles just lead to extra yards and extra conversions.

Remember what we talked about before the game? The Bears lead the NFL in rushing, and in time of possession. They extend drives. And missed tackles are exactly how you let a team like that keep the ball.

And Vrabel said something else. He said the team needs to take the ball away, and it needs to tackle better. And he called those fundamental skills. Basic football. The stuff that wins games.

But he also said he liked the way his team competed. He said they gave themselves a chance. Not a great one, but enough of one.

And Luther Burden had a big day. A reporter asked how much of it was good play design, and how much was poor execution from the Patriots. And Vrabel said it’s not fair for him to answer that right after the game. He said it’s usually a combination. Good scheme, a player making a play, and the Patriots not doing a good enough job. He couldn’t give a percentage, but he said they’re going to have to tackle those guys better.

Then Vrabel was asked what decided the game. And his answer was clear. It’s a thin margin when you play good teams. The Patriots did some good things, but they didn’t do enough. They didn’t get enough stops in the red zone. And they didn’t convert their own red zone opportunity, the one time it mattered. He said that’s probably the difference in the game.

So it came down to the red zone, on both sides of the ball.

And here’s another thing I respected. A reporter asked Vrabel if all the injuries in the secondary were finally catching up to the team. Remember, Carlton Davis, Karan Prunty, and Channing Canada were all out.

And Vrabel refused to use it as an excuse. He said he’s not going to say that. They knew who was going to play, and they knew who they had coming in. And whenever the injured guys are available, they’ll see where they fit and how they can help.

No excuses. That’s Vrabel.

Now let’s talk about Drake Maye. Because this game will stick with him.

Vrabel was asked about Maye’s performance, and he defended his quarterback. He said Maye gave them a chance. He competed. Whether it was running around, scrambling, or finding good places to go with the football, he gave them a chance.

But Vrabel also mentioned the play everybody’s talking about. The interception in the end zone, on a throw intended for Eli Raridon. Vrabel said he knows Maye would like to have that one back. And he said Maye will learn from it like he always does.

And then Vrabel finished with this. “I’m glad he’s ours.”

Patriots Nation, after a loss, with his quarterback throwing a costly interception in the red zone, the head coach says, I’m glad he’s ours. That’s a coach who believes in his quarterback.

And Maye was brutally honest about that throw. He was asked about it, and he said it was a huge point in the game. He said those are the plays he always talks about, the plays that can swing a game. And that was definitely one of them.

When asked whether it was a bad decision or just a bad throw, Maye said something interesting. He said they actually hit the exact same play later in the game. So he liked his chances. But it was frustrating. He said he knows it was a big turning point. And then he said it. “I let my team down.”

That’s a leader. That’s a quarterback who owns his mistakes. And honestly, that’s exactly what Vrabel has been building all season. Accountability.

Patriots Floor family, tell me in the comments. After hearing Maye say “I let my team down,” are you more confident in him or less? Be honest.

Maye also talked about the end of the game. The Patriots got the ball back twice while trailing 27 to 21. Two chances to go down, score, and take the lead.

And they couldn’t do it. Maye said the team had to do its part, and they just couldn’t. Penalties. And he said he wasn’t operating at a high enough level to move the chains. And he pointed to one sequence in particular. After the missed field goal, there was a three-and-out. A bad three-and-out, he called it.

He said that’s what he’ll be thinking about most over the weekend. Having two chances with the ball, down 27 to 21. You have to go score, and trust your defense. He said he’s proud of the times the team has stepped up, like in Buffalo. And this time, they just fell short.

When asked if this was a game they should have won, Maye said it’s hard to say should’ve, would’ve, could’ve. He said he’s proud of the effort. They did some good things. But there’s a lot to clean up. And he gave credit to the Bears for a good plan on a short week.

Now let’s talk about what went right, because there were real positives.

Vrabel was asked about the offensive adjustments after the first couple of series. And he said the team just started playing more connected. He said they protected well when the Bears pressured, and that was a huge key. As Chicago kept loading up the line of scrimmage with pressure, the Patriots were able to pick it up and hit some chunk plays. And that’s the reason they were in the game in the second half.

Remember, before the game, we talked about how the Bears are elite on third down and with their pressure. And early on, the blitz package gave the Patriots real problems. But they adjusted.

And Maye talked about one big play before halftime. It was a play to Rhamondre Stevenson. He said he had a feeling the Bears were going to come after him with a blitz. So with five players out in the pattern and the Bears bringing five, it left four blockers and Stevenson with the ball against six defenders. And all it takes is one cut. He said that’s exactly how they drew it up, and they’ve been practicing that play since OTAs. And he said it definitely brought some life heading into halftime.

That’s a quarterback recognizing the blitz and beating it. That’s the growth we’ve been seeing all season.

Maye was also asked about the Bears’ pressures. He said they showed a different look on third down, out of a package they’d shown a couple of times before, but mixed up. He called them well coached, with good players. And he said the Patriots just didn’t execute well enough on third down.

And then Maye pointed the finger at himself again. He said he has to have answers. Get the ball out quicker, so his linemen aren’t holding up blocks for so long. Be, in his words, the conductor at the line of scrimmage.

That’s exactly what you want to hear. Not excuses. Answers.

Now let’s talk about the touchdown that almost wasn’t.

Maye scored a rushing touchdown, but it was initially not ruled a touchdown. And Vrabel threw the challenge flag. Vrabel explained it simply. Someone had a good view of the play and told him to throw it. So he threw it. And it worked. The touchdown stood.

But Maye admitted something about that play. He said using his legs is always a weapon, and when the defense rushes four, he can hang in the pocket or take off and score. But he said he probably shouldn’t have reached the ball out like that. He said the coaches would be on him about it. He had a feeling he got in, but you can’t reach and risk the ball that close to the goal line.

That’s a smart quarterback learning from a risky play, even one that worked. Because we know how much turnovers have hurt this team.

Now, the run game. This was a real issue.

Vrabel was asked about the offensive production in establishing the run. And he was honest. He said they didn’t run the ball well enough. But they moved it however they needed to. He said sometimes you’re going to have days like that. He thought they’d be able to run it better, and they didn’t. But he said their best players were putting the ball in good spots most of the time and making good decisions.

And Maye talked about the Bears loading the box. He said they creased them once with Rhamondre, which was nice. But the passing game was working. He said it’s hard for Josh McDaniels not to trust the passing game when it’s having success. When a defense loads the box and forces you to throw, you go out there and throw for a bunch of yards. But he said they definitely have to get the run game going.

Vrabel was also asked about TreVeyon Henderson, and why he wasn’t in much during the first stretch of the game. Vrabel said they had certain packages and personnel groupings for Henderson, and that he’ll help them. But they also wanted to see where they were with Cory Ker getting a couple of carries. And he said Henderson helped them plenty down the stretch.

So the running back rotation is still being figured out. Stevenson, Henderson, and Ker, all with different roles.

And then there was the offensive line. Remember, Walter Rouse made his first NFL start at left guard, and Matt Prior started at right guard.

Vrabel was asked how they did. And he said the protection could probably have been better. But the bigger problem was critical penalties. He said being able to handle the road environment matters, especially on third and short. And those penalties were unfortunate. He called it beating ourselves.

But he said he was sure they played okay, and he was sure they played hard. Without seeing the film, it’s hard to say exactly. But he knows they competed.

And there was one penalty call that a reporter asked about. Vrabel said it didn’t look like the player was moving, but maybe he didn’t get set. And then he said something with a little smile. He said he’s not in very good favor with the officiating department, so he probably shouldn’t say anything.

Classic Vrabel.

And finally, Maye was asked what he enjoys about facing an elite young quarterback like Caleb Williams. And his answer was beautiful.

He said he loves competing. He loves being out there with the guys. He loves a challenge. He loves the pressure situations. He said there’s nothing else like that in life. You get a few hours a week to prepare as much as you can for those moments. Sometimes you make them, and sometimes you wish you had them back. He called football such a special sport.

And he said it was well fought, hard fought, and they just came up short. He called it a stinger for the weekend.

Patriots Nation, that’s a young quarterback who loves the game. Who hates losing. And who’s going to come back hungrier.

So let’s put it all together.

The big blow: a 30 to 24 loss in Chicago, ending a three-game winning streak, and dropping the Patriots to 4 and 3.

The shocking decision: Vrabel admitting he told a defender to jump offside on purpose on a late third down, a gamble that ended with a 58-yard field goal. And he took full responsibility.

The turning point: Maye’s interception in the end zone on a throw to Eli Raridon, and two late possessions down 27 to 21 that didn’t produce points.

The problems: missed tackles against Swift, Burden, and Williams. Red zone failures on both sides. Critical penalties on offense. And a run game that couldn’t get going against loaded boxes.

And the positives: a team that fought, adjusted to the blitz, hit chunk plays, and stayed in the game against one of the NFC’s best teams on the road, on a short week.

Here’s my read. This loss hurts. But it’s not a disaster. This was a road game, on a short week, with two backup guards and three cornerbacks out. And the Patriots still had the ball twice at the end with a chance to win.

Now comes the real test. How does this team respond? Because next up is a road division game. And Vrabel’s rule has always been the same. The last game has no bearing on the next one. Every week starts at zero.

The Patriots have to tackle better. They have to win in the red zone. They have to stop beating themselves with penalties. And Drake Maye has to turn “I let my team down” into “I lifted my team up.”
