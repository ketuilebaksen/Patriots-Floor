Now, why a pass rusher? Because in today’s NFL, having a defense that can consistently pressure the quarterback is one of the biggest advantages a team can have.

Quarterbacks have become incredibly dangerous when they get time to scan the field. Give them three or four seconds, and they’ll find somebody open. That’s why a dominant edge defender can completely change the way an opposing offense approaches a game.

And here’s the thing. Defensive success isn’t just about stopping the run or covering receivers. A defense needs players who create pressure without needing constant blitzes. When a pass rusher can beat an offensive tackle one-on-one and force the quarterback to make faster decisions, the whole defense benefits.

Offenses have less time to run complicated passing concepts. Quarterbacks have to throw before their receivers finish their routes. And even when the pass rusher doesn’t get the sack, that pressure creates mistakes. Incomplete passes. Bad throws. Chances for the secondary to make a play.

We’ve already seen how much this matters for the Patriots. In Chicago, Caleb Williams had time, and he threw for over 300 yards. Reporter Evan Lazar noted Williams went 7 of 10 against zone coverage for 117 yards. The Patriots lost 30 to 24.

Now picture that same game with one more dangerous pass rusher forcing Williams to get the ball out faster. That’s the kind of difference we’re talking about.

And think about who’s coming up. Jordan Love and the Packers on Sunday. Jared Goff and the Lions in Munich on November 15th. Those are quarterbacks who will pick a defense apart if they get time. More pressure means fewer chances for them to do that.

Now, here’s what makes this interesting. The Patriots’ pass rush isn’t broken. Far from it.

The Patriots have 22 sacks this season. That’s seventh in the league. Elijah Ponder, the undrafted second-year player, has been a revelation, with at least half a sack in five straight games heading into Miami. Rookie Gabe Jacas had five pressures against the Bears. Milton Williams anchors the middle. Draymond Jones brings veteran experience.

So adding a pass rusher wouldn’t be about fixing a broken unit. It would be about turning a good pass rush into a great one. Making a strong defense even deeper for the playoff push.

And depth matters. Christian Barmore has missed two games this season, and his role has shrunk, with his snaps dropping to about 41 percent of the team’s defensive plays. Breer has even reported the Patriots are open to trading him. So there could be room in the rotation for a new face.

Now let’s talk about who the Patriots could actually target.

Candidate number one. K’Lavon Chaisson.

This is the reunion every Patriots fan is talking about. Albert Breer specifically floated the possibility of bringing Chaisson back from Washington.

Remember his story? A former first-round pick who was cut in training camp and ended up on a practice squad. Then the Patriots signed him in March of 2025, and he exploded. Seven and a half sacks in the regular season, a career high. Three more sacks in the playoffs, including two against the Chargers and one against the Texans. AFC Defensive Player of the Week after a two-sack game with a fumble return touchdown against the Titans.

Then he signed a one-year, 11-million-dollar deal with Washington.

And he’s still producing. Three and a half sacks and 10 quarterback hits in seven games. And against the Eagles, he had seven pressures, the second most in a single game of his career.

The best part? He already knows Vrabel’s system. No learning curve. He could walk into the building and contribute immediately.

And the price could be reasonable. Recent rental pass rushers have gone for late-round picks. Detroit paid a fifth and a sixth for Za’Darius Smith in 2024. Chicago sent a sixth for Joe Tryon-Shoyinka last year. Baltimore got Draymond Jones for a conditional fifth.

But the big question is whether Washington will sell. The Commanders are 2, 4, and 1, but they just beat the Eagles 27 to 10, and they’re two games back in the NFC East. Breer said Chaisson’s availability could depend on where Washington sits in that race.

Candidate number two. DeForest Buckner.

The Colts fired general manager Chris Ballard and started the season 2 and 4. Tom Pelissero reported strong interest from other teams, and said no Colts player is expected to be off-limits if Indianapolis sells. And Albert Breer named Buckner as a possible target for New England.

Buckner is a veteran interior defensive lineman. Through his first six games this season, he had 26 tackles, four sacks, and eight quarterback hits.

And here’s why he’s interesting. If the Patriots trade Barmore, Buckner could step right into that interior role. A veteran who’s still producing, on a contract nearing its end. Pressure up the middle helps the edge rushers too, because it takes away the quarterback’s escape route.

But the price would likely be higher than Chaisson. One story floated a mid-round pick as a possible starting point, but that wasn’t a reported offer. And the Patriots would have to think about his contract beyond this season.

Now, there’s a big decision here. Veteran or young player?

A proven veteran like Chaisson or Buckner gives you immediate production. Leadership. Experience in big moments. The coaching staff can put him in an important role right away, instead of waiting weeks for a young player to develop.

A younger pass rusher with upside could offer long-term value. He could grow alongside Drake Maye, Christian Gonzalez, Milton Williams, and the rest of this young core.

But potential doesn’t always turn into immediate production. And if the goal is to improve the defense right now, for the Packers, for the Lions, for the playoff push, a veteran makes more sense.

Personally? With this team at 5 and 3 and leading the division head-to-head, I want immediate help. That’s why Chaisson stands out so much. He’s a proven producer, he knows the system, and he wouldn’t cost much.

Now, let’s talk about the cost. Because blockbuster trades are exciting, but they come with tough decisions.

Draft picks are valuable, especially for a franchise building around a young quarterback. Giving up a high pick for one player could limit the team’s ability to address other needs in the future. And remember, the Patriots already sent their 2028 first-round pick to Philadelphia for A.J. Brown back in June.

The good news? The Patriots own their 2027 picks in rounds one through four and round six, plus a fifth from Chicago. So they have the ammunition for a rental, without touching the premium picks.

Then there’s the money. The Patriots now have about 6.1 million dollars in cap space, after restructuring Hunter Henry’s contract, turning half his base salary into a signing bonus.

And since a trade this late only costs what’s left of a player’s salary for the season, roughly half, a rental like Chaisson becomes very doable.

And if the Patriots trade Barmore, that could free up even more. His cap hit is 17 million dollars this season, and moving him could free about 20 million in future cap space. Money that could go toward extending Drake Maye and Christian Gonzalez down the road.

So you could see a scenario where the Patriots move Barmore for a pick, and bring in a cheaper rental pass rusher for the stretch run. Get younger, save money, and stay just as dangerous up front. That would be a smart move.

Now, let’s talk about how one more pass rusher could improve the entire defense.

First, the secondary. Defensive backs struggle when quarterbacks have time to wait for receivers to get open. Even great coverage breaks down if a play lasts too long. But when the defensive line consistently creates pressure, quarterbacks have fewer chances to find those openings.

And the Patriots’ secondary has been through a lot. Carlton Davis just returned from a neck injury and played 97 percent of the snaps in Miami until he was rested in the fourth quarter. The moment he left, backup Charles Woods gave up a touchdown on the very next drive. The depth behind Davis and Christian Gonzalez is still unproven.

More pass rush means less time those backups have to cover. That makes everybody in the secondary better.

Second, third downs. That’s where pressure matters the most. Offenses need to convert to keep drives alive. If the Patriots can force longer third downs and send a dangerous edge rusher after the quarterback, they create more punts, more turnovers, and more stops.

And those extra possessions are huge for an offense led by Drake Maye, who’s been playing at an elite level. Every time the defense gets off the field, Maye gets another chance to score.

And remember, the Patriots still hadn’t won the turnover battle in a single game this season heading into Miami. More pressure means more bad throws, and more chances for interceptions. That could finally swing that stat in New England’s favor.

And now, the final question. The one that might surprise you.

What if the biggest weapon the Patriots add isn’t a pass rusher at all?

Think about it. This team has been dominating on defense in a lot of ways, with 22 sacks, seventh in the league. But the offensive line has been the biggest problem all season. Mike Onwenu is out for the season. Alijah Vera-Tucker is on injured reserve. Through six games, the Patriots had used 14 different offensive line combinations.

Names like Quenton Nelson, the highest-graded guard in the NFL, and Andrew Wylie, the two-time Super Bowl champion, have been linked to New England. And protecting Drake Maye might matter even more than adding another pass rusher.

So the real question for this front office is, where does the next dollar do the most good? Rushing the other team’s quarterback? Or protecting your own?
