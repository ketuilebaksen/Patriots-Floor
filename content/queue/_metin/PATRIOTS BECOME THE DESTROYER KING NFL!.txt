Let’s start with the destroyer at the center of it all. Drake Maye.

Through Week 3, Maye’s PFF passing grade was 36.6. The lowest among 26 qualified quarterbacks. Dead last. His turnover-worthy play rate was a league-high 8.6 percent. He had seven turnovers through the first four weeks. People were asking if last season’s MVP runner-up run was a one-year wonder.

And then everything flipped.

Against the Raiders, he went 26 of 34 for 374 yards, four touchdown passes, a rushing touchdown, and zero interceptions. A 150.9 passer rating. A career-high 93.5 PFF passing grade. A 99.9 grade on deep throws, a mark only Jordan Love and Patrick Mahomes had hit this season. And 10 explosive plays against a Raiders defense that had allowed only 18 in its first four games combined.

Against the Jets, he threw three more touchdowns.

And here’s the stat that matters most. Maye has gone two straight games without a single turnover. The quarterback who couldn’t stop giving the ball away is now protecting it like it’s gold.

That’s not a hot streak. That’s a quarterback figuring it out. And when your quarterback figures it out, you start destroying people.

Now let’s talk about the destroyer on defense. Elijah Ponder.

An undrafted free agent. A kid from Cal Poly that 32 teams passed on. And right now, he’s one of the most consistent pass rushers on this roster.

Five and a half sacks this season. At least half a sack in five straight games. Seven tackles for loss. Ten quarterback hits. A forced fumble and two fumble recoveries. And against the Jets, he brought down Geno Smith.

And this defense, as a group, held Maxx Crosby, one of the best pass rushers in football, to zero sacks in the Raiders game. Then it kept a Jets offense under control while the Patriots offense kept handing the ball away.

That’s what a destroyer defense looks like. Missing cornerbacks. Missing pass rushers. And still finding a way to win the game.

So how is this happening? How is a team with this many injuries playing like this?

That’s where Mike Vrabel comes in. Because when you listen to how Vrabel talks about his players, you start to understand why this team keeps winning, no matter who’s on the field.

Let’s start with how he handles injuries. Because this is a team that’s been hit hard.

Before the Jets game, Vrabel was asked whether A.J. Brown would play. He didn’t pretend to know. He said they wouldn’t make that decision until the next day. They’d see how Brown responded to practice. And when he was asked how Brown looked, he was completely honest. He said Brown looked okay. Like everybody, some good plays and some bad plays. And he said they were trying to figure out where his conditioning level was.

No hype. No spin. Just the truth.

He said there’d be some guys listed as questionable, and they’d see how TreVeyon Henderson responded in the morning. And he said Carlton Davis and Karan Prunty would definitely be out.

That honesty matters. Because it tells you Vrabel isn’t going to rush players back to win one game. He’s thinking about the whole season.

And we saw that same patience with Harold Landry. Vrabel was asked whether the plan is for Landry to return this season. He said of course, when he’s ready. He said Landry is working extremely hard, but he just isn’t ready yet. He comes in every single day, he’s around the team in the morning, and then he goes and does his rehab. And Vrabel said they just have to be patient. He’s not ready, so they’re not putting him out there.

Think about what that means. A destroyer pass rush that’s already producing with Ponder, Gabe Jacas, and Draymond Jones, and there’s still a veteran like Landry waiting in the wings. Vrabel coached him in Tennessee. He knows what Landry can do. When Landry comes back healthy, this defense could get even scarier.

And the same patience shows up in the smallest details. A reporter asked Vrabel about knee braces for offensive linemen. Players said he’d mandated them in practice during training camp. And Vrabel explained his thinking. He said it’s something they do in padded practices in camp. He said a lot of reports and studies suggest the impact is minor, maybe helpful for an MCL injury but not an ACL. And he said it’s never something he’s mandated for games.

That’s a coach who’s thought about it. Who’s read the research. Who protects his players in practice, and lets them make their own call on game day. Little details like that are how a destroyer team stays on the field.

Now let’s talk about the offensive line, because this is where Vrabel’s vision really shows up.

He was asked about Alijah Vera-Tucker, the left guard who’s played every single snap this season. And Vrabel said it’s really meant a lot to him, and that he’s seen him improve.

And then he laid out the plan. He said it was always the hope and the vision that Vera-Tucker and Will Campbell could keep developing together, and really solidify the left side of the offensive line. Two players the team believes are talented, with room to grow.

That’s not a patch. That’s a foundation. And Vrabel said he can see how much it means to Vera-Tucker in the way he prepares and the way he responds.

And that foundation got tested big time. Campbell went down with a knee injury against the Raiders. Then right tackle Morgan Moses hurt his right ankle against the Jets and ended up in a walking boot. And who stepped in both times? Rookie Caleb Lomu.

Left tackle against the Raiders. Right tackle against the Jets. Both times in the middle of a game, with no warning. Against the Raiders, he allowed just one pressure on 29 pass-blocking snaps.

And there was a fun moment about Lomu in the press conference. A reporter brought up that Lomu hadn’t done so well with his Friday media update the week before. And Vrabel laughed. He said this one was much better. Lomu had some good laughs, and he came back strong that week.

A rookie getting comfortable. Getting confident. Right when this team needs him most.

And Vrabel had a lot to say about the whole rookie class. He said they’ve improved. They’ve played in some good spots. And he reminded everybody that these are 21- and 22-year-old kids, so it’s great to see them grow. He said the team has to keep investing in them, even if they’re not playing 80 percent of the snaps.

He mentioned Eli. He mentioned Caleb. He mentioned Karan gaining confidence as a gunner on special teams. And then he said the line that tells you everything about this coach. The rookies are very coachable. Much more coachable than some of the veterans. And that’s why he likes coaching them.

That’s how you build a destroyer. You develop young players, you trust them, and you throw them into the fire when it matters.

But this team isn’t just rookies. Vrabel values his veterans too. And he made that clear when he talked about Draymond Jones.

Jones has battled through an injury to get on the field this season. And Vrabel got personal. He said they’re from the same area in Ohio. Jones went to the other Catholic high school, the Jesuit school in Cleveland, and then got to Ohio State after Vrabel did. Vrabel has followed his career for years.

And he said he’s always admired the way Jones plays. His versatility. He called him a real professional. A guy who knows his stuff, and has a great grasp of what the game plan asks of him. And Vrabel said it’s been good for Jones to be in a room full of young players, because of his experience.

That’s the balance. Young talent that’s coachable, and veterans who teach. That’s what makes a team dangerous.

Now let’s talk about the backfield, because this is where Vrabel’s trust has been on full display.

First, Rhamondre Stevenson. Vrabel said he didn’t know Stevenson before he got the job. But he called him a talented player who cares a lot about the team, a great teammate, with a good skill set catching and running.

And what stood out to Vrabel on tape, before he ever met him, was Stevenson’s pass protection. The way he played without the football. His willingness to block for others when the ball wasn’t in his hands. Vrabel said those are the guys who care about the team, who’ll do whatever it takes.

Then Vrabel talked about another back who breaks tackles. He said usually the first guy can’t bring him down. A lot of his explosive runs come after contact, when the defense thinks they’ve got him and they don’t. A smooth runner who doesn’t look rigid. A guy Vrabel liked coming out of college.

And then the big one. Cory Ker.

After Ker fumbled against the Raiders, Vrabel was asked about the level of trust in him. And Vrabel said there’s high confidence. He said Ker did a lot of really good things. Then he pushed back on the media. He said nobody ever talks about the defender who comes back into the gap and hammers the ball out. Everyone just blames the guy holding it. Vrabel said they all share in that, and they’ll be better. He likes the way Ker runs. Ker has to hold on to the ball, but the team also has to protect him better.

He also said Ker works extremely hard. It’s a new system for him, and he didn’t have the luxury of being there since April. So he puts in extra work with running backs coach Tony Dews. And Vrabel said he loves Ker’s spirit and energy.

Now here’s why that matters so much. Because Vrabel didn’t just say it. He proved it against the Jets.

The Patriots got the ball with 7 minutes and 20 seconds left, leading 23 to 16. And Vrabel gave the ball to Cory Ker. Eight straight times. Ker ran for 42 of the drive’s 51 yards, burned more than 6 minutes off the clock, and set up a field goal. With the Jets desperately hunting for one play to get the ball back, Vrabel trusted the guy who had fumbled the week before. And Ker delivered.

Ker said the instruction before he went in was simple. Protect the team. Protect the ball. The ball is the team.

That’s the destroyer mentality. A coach who backs his players. And players who run through a wall for him.

And finally, special teams. Vrabel was asked about Brenden Schooler coming back, after the special teams coach had been praising him. And Vrabel said you can hear how much special teams means to Schooler. You can hear him communicating. He understands the scheme and can explain it to everybody. There’s an energy there. And he’s fast and can play multiple positions.

And special teams sealed the Jets game. The Jets went for an onside kick at the end, and DeMario Douglas recovered it. Game over. That’s the third phase of a destroyer team. Offense, defense, and special teams, all pulling their weight.

Now, let me be honest with you, because a real Patriots fan doesn’t just hype. There’s one thing standing between this team and being a true destroyer.

Turnovers.

The Patriots have 13 turnovers through six games. Seven interceptions and six lost fumbles. A minus 6 turnover differential. And after the Jets win, Vrabel went on WEEI and called it an embarrassment. He said the team is turning the ball over at a historic rate, and called it ridiculous.

Think about that. A head coach, right after a win, calling out his own team. That’s not a coach who’s satisfied. That’s a coach who knows this team can be even better. And if this team ever cleans up the turnovers? Look out, NFL.
