Let’s start with the big picture. Because to understand the great news, you have to understand the plan.

This roster is being shaped by Eliot Wolf’s front office and Mike Vrabel’s coaching staff. And the top priority is crystal clear. Build around young quarterback Drake Maye, and an elite core of players like Milton Williams and Christian Gonzalez.

And look at how that’s going. Maye just went 16 of 20 for 199 yards, two passing touchdowns, and a rushing touchdown in Miami, with zero interceptions. He’s put up at least three total touchdowns in five straight games. Gonzalez is one of the biggest talents on the entire defense. And Williams has become a leader on the defensive line.

But here’s the reality of building a contender. You have to think about this season and the seasons after it. Maye is on a rookie deal right now. Eventually, he’s going to get paid. Gonzalez is going to get paid. And the front office has to make sure there’s room in the budget when that day comes.

That’s why some tough decisions are on the table. And that’s exactly where the first piece of great news comes in.

Great news number one. The defensive line is so deep that Christian Barmore has become a trade chip.

Now, I know what you’re thinking. How is it great news that a good player might get traded? Hear me out.

Let’s look at the numbers. Last season, Barmore was on the field for 62 percent of snaps against the run, and 67 percent of snaps against the pass. He was a starter. A core piece.

This season? Those numbers have dropped way down. He’s playing just 36 percent of snaps against the run, and 56 percent against the pass. On first and second down, his presence has dropped from 60 percent to 40 percent. Overall, he’s playing about 41 percent of the team’s defensive snaps.

So why did his playing time drop? Because other players earned it. According to Albert Breer, Barmore has lost snaps to Milton Williams and Cory Durden.

And that’s the great news. The defensive line has gotten so good, with so much depth, that a player who once had 8.5 sacks in a season isn’t even a full-time starter anymore. That’s not a sign of a struggling unit. That’s a sign of a strong one.

And look at what this front has been doing. The Patriots have 22 sacks this season, seventh in the league. Elijah Ponder, the undrafted second-year player, has been a revelation. Rookie Gabe Jacas had five pressures against the Bears. Milton Williams is anchoring the middle. Draymond Jones brings veteran savvy.

And here’s the thing. Barmore is still really good when he’s on the field. He has 12 total pressures and three and a half sacks this season. His pressure rate per pass rush snap is 9.3 percent. And his pass rush grade is close to 80. So he’s still producing at a high level, in fewer snaps.

That’s exactly why other teams would be interested. And it’s why the Patriots could get real value if they decide to move him.

And here’s where the finances come in. Because this is the part that makes a trade so interesting for New England.

Barmore signed a four-year extension worth up to 92 million dollars. And his cap hit this season is 17 million dollars. In the coming years, his base salaries are set at 12 and 18 million dollars, with cap hits climbing above 23 and 24 million. And those future years aren’t fully guaranteed.

So think about it. A player who’s now playing about 41 percent of the snaps, with cap hits heading toward 24 million dollars. That’s a lot of money for a rotational role.

And according to the reporting, a trade or release would free up about 20 million dollars in future salary cap space.

Twenty million dollars. That’s money that could go toward extending Drake Maye. Toward extending Christian Gonzalez. Toward keeping this young core together for years.

According to the reports, the Patriots have been listening to offers, with a speculated value of around a fourth-round pick.

Now, let me be clear. Nothing has been confirmed. No deal has been reported. And when Barmore was asked about the rumors, he said he’s doing everything the coach asks, and that questions about his future should go to the coaching staff.

But if a trade happens, it wouldn’t be the Patriots giving up. It would be the Patriots being smart. Getting a pick for a player whose role has shrunk, freeing up big money for the future, and trusting the depth they’ve already built.

And if he stays? Then the Patriots keep an elite rotational pass rusher for the playoff push. That’s a win too.

Either way, the defensive line is in great shape. That’s great news number one.

Great news number two. Carlton Davis just proved exactly why he’s staying.

There’s been speculation that the Patriots could even consider moving veteran cornerback Carlton Davis III, who signed a three-year deal worth around 60 million dollars to start opposite Christian Gonzalez. The idea being that the team could get younger and create more financial flexibility.

But the Miami game gave us the clearest answer possible. Davis is way too important to trade right now.

Look at what happened. Davis came back from his neck injury and was on the field for 97 percent of the defensive plays until the fourth quarter. Then the coaching staff decided to rest him for the final stretch of the game.

And what happened the moment he left? Backup Charles Woods came in and was immediately beaten for speed, giving up a touchdown on the very first drive without Davis on the field.

That’s how much Davis matters. One drive without him, and the defense gave up points.

And we’ve seen what happens when he’s out for longer. Remember Chicago? With Davis missing that game, Caleb Williams threw for over 300 yards. Marcus Jones was forced to play outside, where he struggled with six missed tackles. The whole secondary was out of position.

The depth behind Davis is still unproven. Woods, Channing Canada, Karan Prunty, and Kindle Vildor are all still developing. If Davis were traded now, the defense would risk collapsing against good passing attacks. And with Jordan Love and the Packers coming to Foxborough on Sunday, and Jared Goff and the Lions waiting in Munich, that’s not a risk worth taking.

So here’s the great news. Davis is back. Davis is healthy. And Davis just proved, in front of everybody, that he’s a vital piece of this defense. Even the analysts who floated the idea admit that trading him in the middle of the season would destroy the stability of the secondary.

With Davis and Gonzalez together, this secondary can handle anybody. And Marcus Jones can move back into the slot, where he’s at his best.

And here’s something else to remember. Davis played in every game last season. When he’s healthy, he’s consistent and reliable. This season, in the games he’s played, he has 20 tackles, a pass defended, and an interception. Those are solid numbers for a cornerback who’s been dealing with a neck injury.

And with the Patriots fighting for a playoff spot, the last thing they need is to weaken a secondary that just got its starting cornerback back. That’s why the smart money says Davis stays. And that’s great news number two.

Great news number three. The backfield has more options than ever.

Let’s talk about the running back room. Because it’s been one of the most talked-about position groups all season.

TreVeyon Henderson’s usage has dropped this season. He’s gone from about 28 snaps per game to around 14. And if you take away his biggest run of the year, a 39-yard touchdown against Pittsburgh, he’s averaging just 3.2 yards per carry. There have been questions about his pass protection and blocking. And he missed the Miami game because of an illness, and didn’t travel with the team.

Now, I want to be fair to Henderson. He was a 1,000-yard player as a rookie, with 10 touchdowns. Bleacher Report ranked him second among all second-year running backs. The talent is real. And Mike Vrabel has said he believes Henderson will help this team.

But here’s the great news. The Patriots aren’t relying on just one guy anymore.

Rhamondre Stevenson has become the clear lead back. He had 77 carries for 285 yards coming into Miami. Against the Bears, he had a career-high 93 receiving yards, including a 48-yard touchdown. And Vrabel has praised him for his pass protection and the way he plays without the football.

Cory Ker has proven he can close out games. Remember that drive against the Jets? Eight straight touches, more than 6 minutes off the clock, sealing the win. He has 35 carries for 151 yards. And Vrabel said there’s high confidence in him.

And now there’s Lan Larison, signed to the 53-man roster before the Miami game. A guy who ran for over 3,600 yards and 41 touchdowns in college, and was the Big Sky Offensive Player of the Year in 2023.

Plus, the team just brought back Terrell Jennings to the practice squad, a back who already knows the system.

That’s four running backs on the active roster, and another on the practice squad. That’s real depth. And it gives the Patriots flexibility, no matter what happens with Henderson. If he gets healthy and finds his rhythm, great, the backfield gets even better. And if the front office decides to explore the market for him, the team has options to keep the run game going.

Either way, this team won’t be stuck. That’s great news number three.

So now let’s look at the three paths in front of the Patriots before the deadline.

Path number one. Be a smart seller. Trade Barmore for a mid-round pick, free up that future cap space, and keep Carlton Davis to protect the secondary. This path gets value for a player in a reduced role, without weakening the team for the playoff push.

Path number two. A bigger restructuring. Move multiple veterans, including Davis, and focus fully on the long term. But that would mean giving up on this season, at 5 and 3 and leading the division head-to-head. I don’t see that happening. This team is too good right now.

Path number three. Hold everybody until the offseason. Keep the roster together, make a run, and handle the big contract decisions in March.

And here’s what makes this so exciting. Every single one of those paths comes from a position of strength. The Patriots aren’t desperate. They’re not panicking. They’re 5 and 3 with a franchise quarterback, a deep defensive line, a healthy starting cornerback back on the field, and a backfield full of options.

And don’t forget, being a seller in one area doesn’t stop them from being a buyer in another. Albert Breer has reported the Patriots are looking to buy at the deadline, and even mentioned a possible reunion with K’Lavon Chaisson, the pass rusher who had seven and a half sacks for New England last season. The team has about 6.1 million dollars in cap space after restructuring Hunter Henry’s contract. And Mike Vrabel said this week the team will try to do what it can to make the roster better.

So the most likely scenario? A smart, balanced approach. Maybe move Barmore for draft value and future cap space. Keep Carlton Davis to protect the secondary. Trust the depth in the backfield. And possibly add a piece somewhere else, like a pass rusher or offensive lineman, with the extra flexibility.

That’s how a front office builds a team that wins now and later.
