So who is Andrew Wylie?

He’s 32 years old. And he can play right tackle or either guard spot. That’s exactly where the Patriots are short.

And his story? It’s the story of a grinder.

He went undrafted out of Eastern Michigan in 2017. The Colts signed him as an undrafted free agent, and then cut him. The Browns cut him from their practice squad. The Chargers had him on their practice squad for just eight days.

Then the Chiefs called him up in January of 2018. And he stuck.

He went on to win two Super Bowls with Kansas City. And he started in Super Bowl 57.

In March of 2023, Washington signed him to a three-year, 24-million-dollar deal. And this year, they brought him back on a two-year deal worth 7.5 million dollars.

Through the 2025 season, he’d played in 116 NFL games and started 93 of them.

Patriots Nation, that’s not a rookie. That’s not a backup you’re hoping can figure it out. That’s a guy who’s been through everything. Cut, released, practice squad, and then starting in the Super Bowl.

And you know what that story sounds like? It sounds a lot like what Mike Vrabel said this week about undrafted players. It doesn’t matter what you did before you got here. It just matters what you do when you get here. Wylie is the perfect example. Undrafted, cut multiple times, and then a two-time champion.

So why has he barely played for Washington this year?

According to Pro Football Reference, Wylie has played just 15 offensive snaps all season. And that’s even with Washington’s left tackle, Laremy Tunsil, on injured reserve after tearing his triceps in August.

Here’s why. Brandon Coleman moved over to left tackle, and he’s played 392 of Washington’s 403 offensive snaps. Josh Conerly Jr. has the right side. So Wylie is the backup, and he’s pretty much buried on the depth chart.

So the question becomes, why would Washington trade him if he’s such a good backup?

Because their season is slipping. The Commanders have just one win, four losses, and a tie. They’re two games behind the Giants in the NFC East. And Terry McLaurin is now out for the season.

And their offensive line problems aren’t about the backups. In their last game, a 23 to 7 loss to the 49ers, Jayden Daniels was sacked four times, and the line drew five penalties.

Head coach Dan Quinn said this week that he wasn’t pleased with the standard the group set, and he doesn’t think they’ve met it yet. So his problem is the starters on the field, not the guy on the bench.

And trading Wylie doesn’t change a single starter for Washington. Barnwell says they could use one of their practice squad tackles as the backup.

And here’s another big reason Washington would do this. It saves them money next year. According to Spotrac, Wylie has a 3.875-million-dollar cap hit in 2027. And only 425,000 dollars of that is dead money.

So Washington clears most of that money, and they move up a round in the draft, all for a backup with only 15 snaps this season.

Athlon Sports summed it up perfectly. They called a sixth-round pick for Wylie found money.

This is a team with much bigger decisions to make. Washington isn’t losing sleep over trading a backup lineman to move up one round and clear some cap space.

So what does it actually cost the Patriots?

Almost nothing.

The Patriots have three picks in the sixth round of the 2027 draft. Their own, Houston’s, and New Orleans’s. Barnwell himself said that with multiple sixth-round picks, the Patriots can afford to ship one off to add a veteran.

And they get a seventh-rounder back. So they’d still have two sixth-round picks, plus an extra seventh. That’s barely a dent.

But here’s the best part. The Patriots have already made this exact kind of trade.

On August 30th, they sent a sixth-round pick to Minnesota for Walter Rouse and a seventh. And Walter Rouse is the guy who just started at left guard on Thursday night.

That’s how fast a sixth-round pick swap can turn into a starter. Eliot Wolf has already proven this works.

And the money works too. Wylie’s cap hit this season is 3.2 million dollars. And a team that trades for him now only takes on what’s left of his salary for the rest of the season. According to Spotrac, the Patriots have about 4.5 million dollars in cap space. So they wouldn’t even need another move to make his money fit.

Remember with Quenton Nelson, the money was the problem. The Patriots would need to send salary back or restructure a contract. With Wylie? It just fits. Clean and simple.

This is what good teams do at the deadline. Eliot Wolf knows you don’t wait until you’re down to five linemen again before you break the glass. You do it now. The deadline is November 10th.
