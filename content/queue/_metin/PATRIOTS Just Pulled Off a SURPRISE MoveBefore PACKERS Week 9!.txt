Let’s start with the trade report. Because the clock on that one is very real.

The deadline is Tuesday, November 10th, at 4 p.m. Eastern. That’s two days after the Packers come to Foxborough.

Josh Downs has asked out of Indianapolis. ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler reported back on October 28th that Downs has trade interest from multiple teams. But no deal has happened yet.

Now, think about how unusual this is. A receiver asking for a trade, on a team that’s 2 and 5, and he’s still suiting up this Sunday against the Cowboys.

And Downs isn’t a role player. He leads the Colts with 330 receiving yards and 51 targets this season. The next name on that list is Keenan Allen, with 325 yards. Downs is Indianapolis’s top target.

And there’s a Patriots connection that doesn’t need a source to understand. Drake Maye and Josh Downs played together at North Carolina. In 2022, Downs caught 94 passes for 1,029 yards and 11 touchdowns, with Maye throwing to him. They already have the timing. They already have the trust.

And the price? Jordan Schultz reported the Colts want a third-round pick, and he believes they could even settle for a fourth. Schultz also named the Commanders as a team to watch, and another report had the Steelers checking in.

A third- or fourth-round pick is the kind of deal a contending team makes at the deadline without blinking.

So why would New England want him? Look at the receiving numbers.

A.J. Brown has just nine catches on 20 targets this season. The Patriots’ leaders in receiving yards are DeMario Douglas, with 332, and tight end Hunter Henry, with 326.

That tells you how this offense is scoring right now. Through a slot receiver and a tight end. Not through a true number one.

Brown has the name recognition. But with 20 targets and nine catches, he’s actually fifth on the team in targets. Behind Douglas with 37. Henry with 34. Rhamondre Stevenson with 27. And Romeo Doubs with 24. And Brown keeps showing up on the injury report.

That’s the hole a trade would fill. And it’s a hole the Patriots can see on their own depth chart, not just in a report.

And here’s the thing. The Patriots are 5 and 3, and they’ve won four of their last five games. This isn’t a rebuild. It’s a team deciding whether to push forward.

And there’s some help coming from inside the building. Mack Hollins and Greg Van Roten came back to practice from injured reserve this week. That helps. But it doesn’t automatically make the receiver room click. After Douglas and Henry, there’s Romeo Doubs with 261 yards, who’s on injured reserve, and Hollins with 209, who could be back soon.

So a receiver is the obvious need. And the injuries are the reason this can’t wait.

There’s also a money side. The Patriots don’t have a ton of cap space, even after restructuring Hunter Henry’s contract. So any trade has to be for a pick, with a contract that fits.

Now let’s go through the injury report. Because every name on it is out for a different reason.

A.J. Brown missed practice again. That’s his third straight day, with knee and ankle issues. And when Mike Vrabel was asked earlier this week whether injured reserve is a possibility for Brown, he said he could only speak about where Brown is today.

TreVeyon Henderson is out too. But with an illness, not an injury. And from the reports, it sounds like this illness might be more involved than just a simple cough. He also missed the Miami game for the same reason, and didn’t travel with the team.

Christian Barmore was away with an illness too. But here’s the good news. He returned to practice today. Remember, he just got a sack in Miami, and Vrabel was praising him this week for clearing his feet on his pass rush. So getting him back on the field is a big plus.

Henderson and Barmore are the ones to watch for a quick return, because an illness isn’t a knee injury.

Then there’s Hunter Henry. He sat out for a second straight day with a foot injury, after playing only eight snaps on Sunday against Miami. That’s a big concern, because Henry has been one of the most reliable players on this offense all season. He’s second on the team in receiving yards, with 326.

And Marcus Jones. He was a full participant in practice on Wednesday. But today, he didn’t practice, with a foot injury designation.

And Jones matters a lot. He leads the Patriots with 39 tackles. He’s the first-unit punt returner. And he’s on the verge of franchise history. His punt return yards have passed Dave Meggett for fourth place in Patriots history.

So when Jones’s foot shows up on the injury report, it’s not just a cornerback spot you’re worried about. It’s the return job too.

None of this is new for the Patriots. They’ve been playing with starters missing all season. That’s exactly why the depth moves get noticed in a week like this.

So the question isn’t really who’s out. It’s who’s next. And Vrabel makes that call on Friday.

And that brings us to the surprise move. The not-so-quiet part.

The Patriots released cornerback Kindle Vildor on Tuesday, November 3rd, when Marcus Bryant came off injured reserve. Now, Vildor had interest from other teams’ practice squads. He had options.

But he decided to stay in New England. And the Patriots re-signed him to their practice squad today.

And that’s a really sneaky good signing.

Think about the choice he made. Other teams offered him a practice squad spot. And he took the one where he already knows the playbook, the defense, and the coaches. And maybe he had a little inside feeling about why he might be needed this Sunday.

And remember what Vildor did the last time the Patriots needed him.

He was signed to the 53-man roster on October 10th. The next day, against the Raiders, he played 44 defensive snaps. And he earned a game ball.

He punched the ball out of Brock Bowers’s hands on a fourth down, and Craig Woodson recovered it. He also had a pass breakup on third down. The Patriots won that game 45 to 7.

Now, sure, that was a blowout. But 44 snaps for a guy who was signed the day before is still a test, and he passed it.

And the coaches loved him. Mike Vrabel called him a veteran who has played a lot of football. He said Vildor was thrust into the lineup with Christian Gonzalez and Carlton Davis both out, and he handled it well.

And defensive coordinator Zak Kuhr called him the ultimate pro. He said Vildor didn’t bat an eye. He got in there and competed.

So here’s why this matters for Sunday. If Marcus Jones can’t play, Vildor is the cornerback to watch.

The Patriots can elevate a practice squad player for a game, without using a roster spot. They did exactly that with Vildor on October 3rd, before the Buffalo game.

And he’s also been listed on three special teams units in Week 8. So even if Jones is out, Vildor already has a potential job on Sunday.

Now, we don’t know yet if he’ll officially be elevated. That’s our read, not a report. But with Jones dealing with a foot injury, and Henry and Brown off the practice field, it seems likely we’ll see some elevations on Saturday.

And here’s why that matters so much. The Packers aren’t an easy team to cover with a thin secondary.

Jordan Love has 2,246 passing yards this season, and a 46.24 passing EPA. That’s a measure of how many points his throws add compared to an average quarterback. And Vrabel said this week that nobody has had more 20-yard pass plays than Green Bay. He called Love wildly efficient.

That’s the kind of quarterback who tests a backup cornerback on every third down. And that’s exactly what Vildor did well against the Raiders, with that pass breakup on third down.

And the Packers’ receivers are dangerous. Vrabel mentioned Matthew Golden, Christian Watson, and tight end Tucker Kraft. He said when you give Love time, he finds them. So the secondary will be tested from start to finish.

And remember, Carlton Davis is back. In Miami, he played 97 percent of the snaps until he was rested in the fourth quarter. With Davis and Gonzalez on the outside, and Vildor ready if Jones can’t go, the Patriots have a plan. And it was smart of the front office to make sure that plan was in place before the weekend.

Now, let’s talk about the Packers matchup as a whole. Because this is a big test.

Vrabel said this week that the Packers are very physical on defense. They’ve created the second-most negative plays in the league. And 17 percent of their opponents’ drives end in a turnover. That leads the league.

So the Patriots have to protect the football. And the good news is Drake Maye has been doing exactly that. In Miami, he went 16 of 20 for 199 yards, two touchdowns, a rushing touchdown, and zero interceptions. He’s put up at least three total touchdowns in five straight games.

And remember, the Patriots came back from a 10-point halftime deficit in that game, in the rain. Vrabel said the second half looked more like the team he expects to see. That’s the version of this team that needs to show up on Sunday.

And after the Packers? The Patriots head to Munich, Germany, to face the Detroit Lions on November 15th. A long trip. So whoever stays healthy this Sunday has to be ready for the next one too. That’s why the Friday injury report is the one that really tells you what the Patriots are planning.
