Let’s start with some good news. And honestly, I think it’s kind of funny news too. Because it’s all about accountability.

Milton Williams came out this week and said he’s putting in a 1,000-dollar fine for any Patriots defender who jumps offsides or gets penalized for encroachment.

And that’s because the Patriots committed seven of those penalties over the last two games, against Miami and Green Bay. ESPN’s Mike Reiss pointed that out. Seven times the defense just handed the other team free yards.

And here’s the best part. If anybody gets fined, that money goes to the training staff and other team personnel. I love that. The guys who work the hardest behind the scenes, keeping these players healthy, get rewarded when someone makes a mental mistake.

And this is exactly the kind of leadership this defense needs after the Packers game. That game was rough, especially on the defensive side of the ball. 45 points allowed. The most the Patriots have ever given up in a game at Gillette Stadium. There were problems everywhere. But a lot of them can be cleaned up. And penalties before the snap are the easiest mistakes to fix.

Lord knows, if I were playing on the defensive line, I could not afford to jump offsides. Literally could not afford it.

But think about what this says about the locker room. A veteran like Milton Williams is holding his teammates accountable. He’s not waiting for the coaches to fix it. He’s taking it on himself. That’s what good teams do.

And it lines up with what Vrabel said this week. He said it’s okay if the other team earns a play. But when you hand it to them, those are the ones that get you beat. An offsides penalty is handing it to them.

Now let’s get to the injury report. Because there’s a lot to go through.

First, the players we already know are out. Ben Brown, the center, is out. Carlton Davis III is out. And TreVeyon Henderson is out.

And with Henderson, we always want to say this. He’s dealing with a serious health situation, recovering from unexplained neurological symptoms that put him in the hospital for several days. His health is what matters most. We all hope he gets the answers he needs and makes a full recovery.

So with those players out, who gets called up from the practice squad?

My read, and this is my guess, not a report, is that one of the elevations will be cornerback Kindle Vildor. With Carlton Davis out, the secondary needs a body. And Vildor has done this before. Remember the Raiders game? He was signed to the roster the day before, played 44 snaps, forced a fumble from Brock Bowers on fourth down, broke up a pass on third down, and earned a game ball. Defensive coordinator Zak Kuhr called him the ultimate pro.

And with Henderson out, I could see the other elevation being running back Terrell Jennings. He was just brought back to the practice squad, and he already knows the system from his time with the team in 2024 and 2025.

Now, here’s where it gets interesting. The big names who are questionable. A.J. Brown. Rhamondre Stevenson. Walter Rouse. And Greg Van Roten.

Let’s start with Brown and Stevenson. Because this is the shocking part.

At the beginning of the week, a lot of people thought neither one of them would play. People were saying Rhamondre Stevenson might miss the next couple of weeks with his knee injury. People were saying A.J. Brown might be going back on injured reserve. Vrabel was even asked earlier this month whether IR was a possibility for Brown.

And now? Both of them are listed as questionable. Both of them could play today in Germany.

And the way Vrabel talked to the media this week made it sound a lot more optimistic than people expected. He said Brown and Stevenson looked pretty good based on their practices, even though he still wanted to see how the next session went.

Brown traveled to Germany, and he practiced throughout the week. Stevenson was limited on Thursday, and on Friday he took part in warm-ups, but he wasn’t in uniform for practice. And the good news is Stevenson didn’t need surgery on that knee.

So here’s my take. If A.J. Brown is truly good to go, I think that would be huge. Especially against this Lions defense, which has been struggling against the pass. The Lions came into the week with cornerback D.J. Reed not practicing, and safeties Kerby Joseph and Brian Branch limited. That’s a banged-up secondary. A healthy A.J. Brown could feast.

Same with Stevenson. If he’s good, and the Patriots feel confident, pull the trigger. Make the move.

But if not? Let them rest. Because here’s the thing. After this game, the Patriots have their bye week. If Brown and Stevenson sit today, that’s about three weeks of rest before the Chargers game. That could make a huge difference for the rest of the season. You need these guys in December and January, not just for one Sunday in November.

Now, there’s another big decision. The open spot on the 53-man roster.

The Patriots have one available spot. And the question is, who gets it?

On the offensive line, Walter Rouse is in the concussion protocol, and he’s already on the 53-man roster. Then there’s Greg Van Roten.

Van Roten came in when Mike Onwenu went down for the season. And then, not long after, about 60 snaps later, he got hurt too, and ended up on injured reserve.

But Van Roten is a veteran who started for two years in a row for the Giants, and he played well there. He was a great depth pickup for the Patriots. And I think a lot of people would agree, if you had to pick between Van Roten and Rouse right now, you’d probably take the experienced veteran.

So my guess, and again, this is my guess, is that if the Patriots are going with Van Roten, you’ll see him activated to the 53-man roster sometime today. That would fill the open spot, and give Drake Maye a veteran presence at guard.

And that matters so much this week. Because the Lions’ pass rush is dangerous. They came into this game with 27 sacks, the third-most in the NFL. And Vrabel said Detroit is one of the best teams in the league at creating unblocked pressure. With Ben Brown out and Rouse in the protocol, the interior of this line needs every experienced body it can get.

And there’s one more name to watch. Charles Woods, the cornerback, is questionable too. And that’s another big reason I think Vildor gets elevated. With Davis out and Woods questionable, the secondary is really thin.

Now, let’s talk about the keys to the game. Because this one is going to come down to a few big things.

Key number one. Protect the football.

This is the big one. The Patriots haven’t had a single clean, turnover-free game this season, according to Drake Maye himself. And against Green Bay, they turned it over four times, three interceptions from Maye and a fumble from Stevenson.

And the Lions? Vrabel said they don’t turn the ball over on offense at all. So if the Patriots give it away, they’re going to be in big trouble. Vrabel said this week that the Patriots choose to turn the ball over too much. Choose. That means it can be fixed.

Maye has to play smart. He said after the Packers game that he has to see the deep safeties better, and see the defenders jumping the crossing routes. If he does that today, the Patriots have a real chance.

Key number two. Protect Drake Maye.

With 27 sacks coming into this game, the Lions’ pass rush is a real threat. The Patriots have to communicate up front. They have to identify the blitzers. And when somebody comes free, Maye has to get the ball out quickly. Vrabel said it simply. If a blitzer is unblocked, you have to throw hot.

And getting A.J. Brown back would help with that too. A receiver who wins quickly protects the quarterback.

Key number three. Stop Jahmyr Gibbs.

Vrabel said Gibbs leads the league in touches. He said it’s really hard to replicate Gibbs’s speed in practice. If Gibbs gets loose, the Lions will control this game. The Patriots’ defense has been strong against the run for most of the season. Before the Bears game, they hadn’t allowed a single 20-yard run all year. They have to get back to that.

Key number four. No free yards.

That’s where Milton Williams’s fine comes in. Seven offsides and encroachment penalties in two games. That can’t happen against a team like Detroit. Make the Lions earn every yard.

And key number five. Christian Gonzalez has to bounce back.

Against the Packers, Gonzalez gave up 10 catches for 187 yards. That was the third-most yards allowed by any defender in a single game this season. But through his first six games, he had allowed just 196 yards total. He’s one of the best cornerbacks in the league. And with Carlton Davis out, the Patriots need him to be that guy today against Amon-Ra St. Brown and Jameson Williams.
