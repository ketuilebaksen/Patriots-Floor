BIG BLOW! PATRIOTS JUST DID SOMETHING NO ONE SAW COMING!

Patriots Nation, an ESPN report just dropped, and it paints a picture of a team in real trouble.

The Patriots are 5 and 5. On paper, they’re still on the edge of a wild-card spot. But that’s about the only good news. Drake Maye is hurt, and we don’t know for how long. And according to ESPN, there are cracks showing in the foundation. In the culture. In the effort. In the roster.

And on top of all that, the Patriots made a move with TreVeyon Henderson that a lot of fans didn’t see coming. He’s now on injured reserve.

Let’s start with the report itself.

Mike Vrabel is known for two things. Building culture, and building a tough-minded defense. But ESPN’s report says cracks are showing on both of those fronts.

Look at the numbers. Over the last two games, the defense gave up 80 points and 972 yards. That’s a 45 to 27 loss to Green Bay, and then a 35 to 10 loss to the Lions in Munich. In just two games.

And ESPN described the team as looking rudderless and uninspired over these past two weeks. The report says Vrabel is seemingly struggling to motivate the team.

Think about how strange that sounds. A coach whose whole identity is culture. A team that, according to ESPN, nobody seems to be steering. And a starting quarterback who was left behind in Germany for tests.

Now, here’s what makes this different from just one writer’s opinion. The players are saying it too.

After the game in Munich, safety Kevin Byard said the Lions played harder than the Patriots. And he called it a slap in the face that the defense let them play harder.

Then Rhamondre Stevenson said something similar. He said there are some things that don’t take talent. You go out there and you play 100 percent on every play.

When a veteran safety and a veteran running back are both saying the same thing, it’s not just a columnist’s opinion anymore. It’s two players telling you that effort is the issue. And you can’t fix effort with a game plan.

And then came the line you almost never hear about a Vrabel-coached team. According to ESPN, Vrabel himself admitted on Monday that the Lions played harder.

And it wasn’t just that. On WEEI, Vrabel said the Lions beat the Patriots in all four phases. Offense, defense, special teams, and coaching. All four. He didn’t give himself a single excuse.

Smash that like button if you respect Vrabel for being honest, even when it hurts.

And the criticism didn’t start in Munich. Three days before that game, the Boston Globe’s Christopher Gasper wrote that year two of the Vrabel era looked like a sophomore slump. He said the “pardon our appearance” sign was still up.

That’s a sharp line. And it reflects what a lot of fans are feeling. Last season, this team went to the Super Bowl. This season, it’s 5 and 5, and it’s lost three games by 30 points or more.

But here’s the thing. Most of the Patriots’ problems are pretty clear. And a big part of it is the roster.

ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler pointed to the receiver room. He said the Patriots swapped a productive Stefon Diggs for a disappointing A.J. Brown. And ESPN’s Dan Graziano went even further. He called the Brown trade an injury-riddled bust so far.

That’s tough to hear. The Patriots gave up a 2028 first-round pick for Brown. And so far, injuries have kept him off the field for most of the season.

Now let’s talk about the move that surprised a lot of people. TreVeyon Henderson.

Henderson is now on injured reserve. The Patriots placed him there on November 14. That means he’s out for at least three more games. So, as long as nothing changes, that’s the Chargers, the Bills, and the Vikings without him.

Henderson said he had to be hospitalized and tested for his neurological symptoms. And let’s be clear, his health is the only thing that matters here. We all hope he gets the answers he needs and comes back healthy, whenever that is.

But from a football standpoint, this is a big blow. Henderson is one of the most explosive players on this roster. And with Maye out, the Patriots needed every playmaker they could get.

Now let’s go through the Munich game. Because the numbers tell the story.

The game was basically over by halftime. Detroit led 7 to 3 after the first quarter. Then the Lions outscored the Patriots 14 to nothing in the second quarter. And it was 28 to 3 after three quarters.

So the Patriots trailed by four after one quarter, and by 25 after three. And their only touchdown came late, when the game was already decided.

Jahmyr Gibbs ran for 153 yards and two touchdowns on 22 carries. And the Patriots’ leading receiver had just 48 yards on five catches, with a lot of those yards coming while Maye was still in the game.

Look at the Patriots’ scoring by quarter. Three points. Then nothing. Then nothing. That’s what a team without its quarterback and without A.J. Brown looks like. And the culture questions get louder when the roster is this thin.

And there was a strange twist. The sack that ended Maye’s day also cost Detroit. Aidan Hutchinson hurt his knee celebrating after that sack. And there’s been a report that Hutchinson tore his ACL, which would end his season. That’s brutal for the Lions too.

Now let’s go through the full injury board. Because it’s long.

First, Drake Maye. The Patriots left their starting quarterback in Germany for a day to get tests on a possibly broken ankle. It happened in the first quarter, on the sack by Aidan Hutchinson. Maye never came back. We still don’t have an official diagnosis. A fractured ankle has been reported, but nothing has been confirmed. And Vrabel said on Monday that he doesn’t anticipate Maye being available coming out of the bye.

Second, A.J. Brown. And Brown actually has two separate injury problems, not one. He suffered a high ankle sprain in Week 1 at Seattle that kept him out from Week 2 through Week 5. Then knee and ankle problems kept him out of more games before Munich.

He came back for the trip to Germany. But in the third quarter, he left with a hamstring issue. ESPN says that hamstring will most likely keep him out for a little while. There’s no diagnosis and no timeline yet. Brown left the locker room limping, and declined to speak to the media. And Vrabel’s answer was that they’d see how severe it is once they got back.

And this is where the criticism gets loud. NBC Sports Boston’s Michael Hurley called it clear mismanagement of a player’s health. He pointed out that Brown was activated the first moment he was eligible, then played twice in about 100 hours, and then flew to Germany without being a full participant in practice.

Now, that’s one columnist’s opinion, not a report. But it’s a question the Patriots are going to keep getting asked until Brown plays at a high level again.

Third, Romeo Doubs. He’s now eligible to come back from injured reserve. He landed on his shoulder on a touchdown catch against the Jets, and he’s been on IR since October 19. But eligible doesn’t mean activated. The team hasn’t said yet whether he’ll play.

Then there’s Henderson on IR, and Rhamondre Stevenson, who’s still banged up with his knee. Carlton Davis III was ruled out with a knee injury before Munich.

And on the offensive line, Greg Van Roten came off injured reserve on November 14 after a hamstring injury. Walter Rouse is in the concussion protocol. And Alijah Vera-Tucker is still on IR.

Add it all up, and ESPN says the Patriots have been missing six offensive starters overall. Quarterback. Receivers. Running back. Offensive line. Graziano says they’re banged up at all four spots.

So this isn’t a team with a culture problem on top of a few injuries. This is a team where every fix comes with a question mark.

Now let’s talk about why Maye is the injury that matters most.

Maye has started 43 straight games. And ESPN’s Mike Reiss says that streak is now in jeopardy because of the ankle injury, although there’s still no official confirmation.

And look at his numbers this season. His EPA is at 43.3. And over his last five games before the injury, his passer rating was 111.8. That’s up from 85.5 before that stretch. He was getting better and better.

And it’s not just the passing game. Rhamondre Stevenson leads the team with 403 rushing yards. And Maye is third on the team with 230 rushing yards. So when you take Maye out, you lose the passing game, and you also lose a big chunk of the running game. And that’s before you even count the injured receivers and linemen.

So now Tommy DeVito is the next man up. Graziano says DeVito could end up being the team’s best option, since the trade deadline has passed. Behind DeVito is rookie Behren Morton. And if Maye is out for the rest of the season, that’s the whole quarterback room.

DeVito played poorly in Munich. And Fowler writes that the solutions aren’t readily available.

Smash that like button if you’re still behind Tommy DeVito.

So what’s the answer? With the trade deadline gone, there are no instant fixes. And Fowler says scoring points has to be a collective effort, starting with the running game.

But think about what that running game looks like right now. Stevenson with a banged-up body, and Henderson on injured reserve. That’s a lot to ask.

The bye week does give Vrabel time to work on solutions, both on the culture side and on the personnel side. Graziano calls this a real inflection point. He says the staff and the roster will show what they’re made of by how the Patriots finish the season.

And ESPN does say the Patriots should get healthier on offense in the coming weeks. But it also says it’s hard to see them getting healthy enough for any kind of serious run.

Now, here’s the good news. The playoff race is still alive.

At 5 and 5, the Patriots are eighth in the AFC. That’s just one game behind Cleveland for the last playoff spot. Pittsburgh is 6 and 3, and Cleveland is 6 and 4. Neither one is out of reach.

And the schedule helps, at least a little. Three of the Patriots’ last seven games are against the Chargers at 1 and 8, the Jets at 2 and 8, and the Dolphins at 1 and 8. Those three teams are 4 and 24 combined.

But the other games are much tougher. Buffalo at 8 and 1. Kansas City at 7 and 2. Denver at 7 and 2. And Minnesota on a Thursday night. And all of those games will most likely be played without Drake Maye.

A healthy, motivated team would feel good about that split. But a team that just got called rudderless has to win the games it should win. And those are exactly the kinds of games a team with an effort problem loses.

So here’s the math. To get to seven wins, the Patriots need to win just two of their last seven games. To really push for the playoffs, they’ll need more than that. And it starts with the Chargers on November 29.

Maye is now eligible for a contract extension. And Fowler says the Patriots are most likely going to pay him. But he also says neither side is heading into the offseason with momentum.

So the Patriots have a big decision coming. And whatever they decide, their offseason is going to start with a franchise quarterback who just got hurt, and a coach whose team has been questioned about its effort.
