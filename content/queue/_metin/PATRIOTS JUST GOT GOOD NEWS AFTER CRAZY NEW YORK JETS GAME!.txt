Let’s start with the injury that changed the game. Morgan Moses.

The veteran right tackle couldn’t finish the win over the Jets. He hurt his right ankle in the second quarter. He got attention on the field, went into the medical tent, and then headed to the locker room. He did briefly come back to the sideline after halftime, and for a moment, Patriots fans were hoping. But at the start of the fourth quarter, the Patriots ruled him out.

And after the game, Moses was seen in the locker room wearing a walking boot on his right foot.

Now, I want to be careful here. Those details deserve attention. But they don’t tell us how long he’s going to be out. A walking boot can be precautionary. It doesn’t reveal how serious the injury is. Anybody telling you right now that Moses is out for weeks, or that he’ll be fine for Thursday, is guessing.

What we do know is what happened on the field. And that’s where the good news starts.

Caleb Lomu stepped in at right tackle.

Think about this kid’s last two weeks. Against the Raiders, he played 47 snaps on the left side in relief of Will Campbell. He allowed just one pressure on 29 pass-blocking snaps, and the Patriots held Maxx Crosby without a sack. And now, one week later, he’s stepping in on the right side in the middle of a division game.

Most of his work with New England has actually been on the right, so this is closer to his home. But stepping into a division game midway through still demands instant communication. A quick read on where the pressure is coming from. And Maye needs enough time to run the whole play, not just throw to his first read.

And here’s a fair point an informed fan will make. The team spent a first-round pick to have talent ready for exactly this situation. And I agree with that expectation. If Lomu is ready, now is the time to show why New England traded up to draft him. This is his moment.

But treating one draft pick as complete injury insurance lets the roster off way too easily.

Look at the reality. There was no other primary tackle on the game-day roster. Matt Prior and Walter Rouse have played tackle before, so there are emergency options. But moving people around has a cost. The interior line could lose depth. The blocking plan might need regular help from a tight end or a running back. And the same five linemen working together can read protection switches a lot faster than a reshuffled group.

And the injuries didn’t stop with Moses. Two more linemen, including Ben Brown, also left the game hurt. So this offensive line is getting tested from every direction. We have to see how many linemen can even practice this week.

So what should we watch? Who practices on each side of the line. The staff needs to learn how much Lomu can handle on his own.

Here’s why that matters so much. If Lomu holds down the right edge by himself, Maye can keep more receivers out in routes. More options. More big plays. But if pressure arrives too quickly, Josh McDaniels has to shorten the plays, give the tackle help, or accept fewer downfield options.

And this is the part people miss. The cost of this injury won’t just show up in the sack total. It’ll show up in the plays the coordinator feels he can no longer call. The deep shots. The slow-developing routes. The plays that made this offense so dangerous against the Raiders.

If Moses comes back quickly, Lomu will have been an important bridge. If he doesn’t, New England has to decide. Is the rookie the starter? Or does this team need another layer of protection?

Now let’s talk about the player everybody was waiting for. A.J. Brown.

Brown returned against the Jets as soon as he was eligible, after the ankle injury he suffered in the opener. And this was the addition fans had been dreaming about. A physical target who could change matchups for Maye. The missing piece.

But his first game back was rough. There’s no other way to say it.

Three catches on 10 targets. 27 yards. And some passes that a player of his ability normally secures. One of the reported mistakes came on an onside kick recovery attempt late in the game.

When a player of Brown’s caliber comes back, fans expect an immediate impact. And Sunday was an uncomfortable reminder that being cleared to play and being sharp are two very different things.

Now here’s what I respect. Mike Vrabel didn’t cover for the performance. He said Brown could have come down with more balls. He acknowledged how long Brown had been out. And he kept his confidence in what Brown can bring this season.

And Brown didn’t blame his ankle. His response was direct. No excuses. He said, ankle or not, he should make those plays.

I like that answer. A lot. Because it shows a player demanding more from himself. That’s the kind of accountability you want from a veteran star. But let’s be real. The right words don’t finish a catch. His next opportunity is what’s going to change the evaluation.

So before we pass judgment on Brown, let’s separate two things.

Ten targets show the offense tried to work him back in after weeks without game reps. That’s the staff showing faith. Three catches show that effort didn’t produce much. Neither number explains every route or every throw.

The drops on catchable balls are the most troubling part. Because they take away exactly what Brown is supposed to give this offense. The ability to turn tough chances into positive plays.

So the criticism should be firm. But calling one bad return proof that he can’t help anymore? That’s replacing analysis with impatience. One game after weeks out. Let’s give the man a chance to find his rhythm.

And there’s a serious counterpoint to consider. The week is short. Thursday’s opponent is Chicago. And that ankle could still be affecting how Brown accelerates and stops. So the best answer might not be throwing 10 more balls his way just to force a stat-line rebound.

It might be finding the routes he can run well right now, and getting him the ball when he has a real advantage. That doesn’t excuse him. But it also means continuing to use the players who produced while he was out.

And there’s a direct connection to the offensive line here too. If Maye has less time in the pocket after Moses’s exit, Brown has to be in sync on routes where the ball comes out quickly. One secure catch on third down might do more for this offense than a forced attempt to prove the chemistry is already back. And if the protection holds, New England can use Brown’s range farther downfield.

His return and the health of the line meet at the exact same point. The timing of the play. A mistake on either side leaves less room for the other.

We don’t need one spectacular play to call Brown’s return a success. We need good decisions, secure hands, and a role that gives Maye confidence when a drive gets tight. Brown owned the performance. Vrabel kept faith in him. And a win gives the team room to fix it.

So here’s what I want to see against Chicago. How he practices this week. What routes he runs. Whether catchable balls become catches again. And whether Maye still looks for him when a defense tightens up.

And that brings us to the name everybody’s talking about. Jack Conklin.

The veteran is a two-time first-team All-Pro. He was released by the Browns in March. He plays right tackle, exactly where the Patriots need help right now. And here’s the connection. He worked with Mike Vrabel in Tennessee in 2018 and 2019.

But Patriots Nation, this is where things get really interesting. Knowing the coach doesn’t settle the main question. If Moses misses time, what would Conklin give the Patriots that Lomu can’t give right now? And what would that mean for the rookie’s development?

Let’s start with the case for him, because it’s pretty straightforward.

A young quarterback is leading a team that just moved to 4 and 2, with three straight wins. Maye threw for 235 yards and three touchdowns against the Jets. When an offense is producing like that, an injury in pass protection can’t just be brushed off as a footnote. You protect your quarterback. Period.

And Conklin spent years taking on NFL pass rushers at right tackle. In 2019, with Vrabel as his coach, he started all 16 regular-season games for a Tennessee team that reached the AFC Championship game. Vrabel has seen him at his best, up close, in the biggest games.

Now, that history doesn’t guarantee the same player today. But it does explain why his name belongs in a serious evaluation.

And there are other advantages. Signing a free agent wouldn’t cost a draft pick. Nothing to give up but a contract. And his history with Vrabel could help the staff evaluate him quickly and honestly.

Still, knowing the head coach doesn’t mean Conklin knows every Josh McDaniels call, or every lineman he’d be playing next to. And signing a veteran during a short week, and actually getting him ready to play, are two separate decisions.

Now for the objection his resume can’t answer.

Conklin played only eight games in 2025. He’s dealt with injuries in recent years. And he might not be the pass protector he was at his All-Pro peak. If the whole goal is to reduce risk for Maye, signing him without checking his health and readiness could just swap one uncertainty for another.

Any real conversation would have to cover a medical evaluation, his playing condition, the price, and the role. Without those answers, a reunion with Vrabel makes a great headline. But it doesn’t make a complete roster decision yet.

And what about Lomu? He can’t become a background character in a debate about veterans. The rookie came in against the Jets, he’s worked on both sides of the line, and he’s a major investment. More snaps could reveal a reliable starter and prevent a rushed signing.

But if injuries keep piling up, relying only on him leaves very little margin for error.

So an outside option could be insurance. It could be competition. Or it could be an immediate need. And each of those scenarios calls for a different price.

So here’s my position today. Evaluate Conklin. Don’t crown him the solution.

And let me be clear about something. There’s no report of the Patriots contacting him or scheduling a workout. This is a possibility, not a deal. Moving forward would depend on Moses’s availability, how the internal options perform, and the health of the backups.

If Moses improves and Lomu responds, New England can make this decision without rushing. But if protection starts limiting the play calls, waiting out of pride would be an expensive gamble with Drake Maye. And nobody in Foxborough should be willing to make that gamble.

So watch the injury report, the practice lineup, and any concrete sign that the team is evaluating the veteran.

Now, here’s the best news of the whole day. Because while everybody’s worried about the line and Brown’s rough return, the Jets game showed us another way this offense stays standing. One player took every opportunity he got, and then he secured the final possession.

DeMario Douglas.

Douglas finished with seven catches, 86 yards, and a touchdown. He led New England in receiving yards. But what stands out to me is how far apart his two biggest plays came.

Before halftime, he caught a 9-yard touchdown from Maye that helped build a 17 to 3 lead.

And in the final minute, with the Jets still trying to steal a possession, Douglas recovered the onside kick.

One play built the cushion. The other kept the win from slipping away. That’s a complete game.

And I want to be fair here. That onside kick isn’t only about Brown’s mistake. The ball was live, and Douglas secured it. Brown has to improve. But Douglas deserves the credit, without someone else’s error becoming the whole story of his day. His touchdown helped build the lead, and seven catches show he was involved from start to finish.

Because make no mistake. That 26 to 19 final was not comfortable. The Jets scored 10 points in the third quarter and cut into the lead. Maye found Hunter Henry for another touchdown. Andy Borregales hit a 27-yard field goal. New York answered and went for the onside kick. And Douglas had to finish the job, because the Jets were still alive.

Now, recovering an onside kick doesn’t make Douglas the new centerpiece of this offense. One strong afternoon doesn’t settle the target order. But while Brown finds his rhythm, Douglas delivered volume, yards, and a touchdown. That gives McDaniels another reliable option.

If the line forces Maye to throw early, a sharp route from Douglas can keep a drive alive. If Brown gets back to his form, the offense gains combinations, instead of leaning on one name. That’s the dream. Not one star. Multiple threats.

And sharing the ball also means sharing responsibility. Douglas showed that Maye can throw to whoever gets open, rather than treating Brown’s return as an order to force it to him even when the play points somewhere else. That’s how great quarterbacks play. Read the defense, take what’s there.

The Bears will test that approach with a different defensive plan. Douglas deserves routes with a real chance to get the ball. If the first option is covered, Maye needs to trust that the second can finish the play. Douglas’s production should matter when the next game plan is built.

And remember what Vrabel said about this receiver room a couple weeks ago. Selfless. When one guy eats, they all eat. Douglas just lived that out.

We’ll get our answer on the field, from how both are used against Chicago, how securely they catch the ball, and who Maye looks for when he needs a conversion.

The ending against the Jets showed us that trust is built by finishing the most uncomfortable play of the day. Douglas finished it.

Now this offense has to keep that kind of option alive while it solves the protection question.

So let’s put it all together.

The good news. Three straight wins. 4 and 2. Maye with three more touchdown passes. Douglas stepping up big when it mattered most. Lomu showing he can play both sides of the line. And a veteran like Conklin out there as a possible option if things get worse.

The concerns. Moses in a walking boot with no diagnosis yet. More linemen banged up. Brown’s rough first game back. And a short week with Chicago on Thursday night.

The Jets win bought New England some time. But a short week leaves very little room for a bad evaluation.

My read on Foxborough is simple. Maye has players who can win. As long as the line lets him use them, and every receiver does his job.

We still need a diagnosis on Moses. A response from Lomu as a possible starter. And a cleaner game from Brown. The injury report and the opening drives against Chicago will show whether this staff found the right balance, without shrinking the offense.
