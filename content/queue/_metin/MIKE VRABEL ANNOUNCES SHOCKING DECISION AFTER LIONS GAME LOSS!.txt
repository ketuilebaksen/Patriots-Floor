MIKE VRABEL ANNOUNCES SHOCKING DECISION AFTER LIONS GAME LOSS!

Patriots Nation, it hurts. The Patriots went to Munich, played the Detroit Lions in front of an international crowd, and came home with a loss. That’s two losses in a row, after the Packers game, and now the record sits at 5 and 5 heading into the bye week.

And right now, a lot of fans are asking the same questions. What’s going on with this team? Who’s coming back? And what’s Mike Vrabel going to do about it?

Well, before this game ever kicked off, Vrabel stood in front of the media in Germany and made some decisions that, looking back now, tell us a lot about where this team is headed. One of them was about TreVeyon Henderson and the roster spot. One was about Rhamondre Stevenson. And one was a very clear message about Drake Maye and the turnovers that keep hurting this team.

So today on Patriots Floor, we’re going through everything Vrabel said in Germany, and what it all means now, after the loss, as this team heads into the bye week.

Let’s start with the biggest decision. The roster spot, and TreVeyon Henderson.

Henderson didn’t travel to Germany. And when a reporter asked Vrabel if there was anything new on Henderson’s status, Vrabel said that not traveling was his status.

But then Vrabel said something that every Patriots fan should hear. He said he talks to TreVeyon every day. He said that just like everybody else who isn’t available, Henderson is working hard to get back. And when that will be, Vrabel said he doesn’t know. He doesn’t have a timeline.

And then he said he loves his interactions with Henderson. He loves talking with him and seeing how he’s doing.

Patriots Nation, that’s a head coach who cares about his player as a person first. And that’s how it should be. Henderson spent several days in the hospital with unexplained neurological symptoms, and he’s still looking for answers. His health matters more than any football game.

But here’s the decision. A reporter asked about the roster and the injured reserve situation. And Vrabel explained that the team is very conscious of the roster. But he said they’ll hold off until they absolutely need the spot. And he said they haven’t really felt that pinch yet.

He said they’re also still trying to figure out when Henderson may be available. And at the same time, they have players coming back from injured reserve, or players who have been designated to return. So he said there are a lot of things to weigh with the roster. They know they have to be at 53. And they have to get 48 players to the game, whether that’s by elevating practice squad players or using guys from the 53-man roster.

And he finished by saying they talk it through and try to figure out what’s best for the team.

So what does that mean? The Patriots are choosing not to rush any roster move with Henderson. They’re keeping the spot. They’re not putting him on injured reserve until they’re forced to.

And there’s a big reason for that. The Patriots are running low on return designations from IR. The NFL only gives each team eight per season. And according to Evan Lazar, the Patriots had already used five: A.J. Brown, Marcus Bryant, Mack Hollins, Greg Van Roten, and Reggie Gilliam. Vrabel himself said earlier this week that the team doesn’t have many spots left to bring guys back from injured reserve.

So every IR decision from here on out matters. And the Patriots are being very careful.

Now, with the bye week coming, this decision is going to come up again. Do they keep holding the spot? Or do they eventually need it? We’ll find out over the next couple of weeks.

Now let’s talk about who didn’t travel at all.

Vrabel said the only game status he could really talk about was for the players who didn’t make the trip. And there were three of them.

Carlton Davis III didn’t travel. TreVeyon Henderson didn’t travel. And Ben Brown didn’t travel.

That’s a starting cornerback, a running back, and the starting center. All of them stayed home. And for everybody else, Vrabel said they’d have to go through practice and see where guys were that afternoon.

He said this was a little unique, talking to the media before the team’s Friday practice. He said based on Thursday, things looked pretty good, but they still had to go through Friday, see where that went, and then meet with the medical staff and meet with the players.

And then Vrabel said something that I think sums up this whole season right now. He said the more the team can get everybody back, the more it contributes to what they want to be as a team. He said one guy doesn’t make the difference. But the more players they get back, the more exciting it is, because the pieces can start to build and come together.

And he said he hoped some of the guys who haven’t been with the team, or who got hurt the week before, could be available.

Patriots Nation, that’s exactly the story of this loss. This team went into Germany banged up. Missing its center. Missing its starting cornerback across from Christian Gonzalez. Missing a running back. With A.J. Brown, Rhamondre Stevenson, Walter Rouse, and Greg Van Roten all listed as questionable.

And now the bye week comes at exactly the right time. Vrabel wants the pieces to come together. The next two weeks are the chance for that to happen.

Now let’s talk about the offensive line.

Vrabel was asked about Greg Van Roten and Walter Rouse. And he said they’d see how Greg came out of practice on Friday, noting that Van Roten had been returned to play. And he said they’d see where Walter was in the concussion protocol, and then go from there.

This matters because the interior of the line has been hit hard. Ben Brown out. Mike Onwenu out for the season. Alijah Vera-Tucker on injured reserve. And against a Lions defense that came into this game with 27 sacks, the third-most in the league, protecting Drake Maye was always going to be a huge challenge.

Van Roten started for two straight seasons with the Giants before coming to New England. Rouse started his first career game against the Bears. Both of them were fighting to get healthy at the same time.

Getting this offensive line healthy over the bye might be the single most important thing for the rest of the season. Because when Maye is protected, this offense can be one of the best in the league. We saw it in October, when he won AFC Offensive Player of the Month.

Now, let’s get to Drake Maye. Because this is the part a lot of fans want to talk about.

A reporter brought up interceptions. And Vrabel’s answer was really interesting.

He said the same thing they always coach Maye to do is to be great with the football, and to respect the football.

But he also said Maye knows the impact he makes on this football team. Whether that’s explosive plays, running with the ball, or delivering accurate passes. And Vrabel said Maye will keep doing that.

And then he said the team has a lot of confidence in Drake. He said he looks forward to Maye continuing to have success. And he said it’s just unfortunate when you put the ball in harm’s way, because it’s tough to win that way.

Patriots Nation, that’s the message. Vrabel is fully behind his quarterback. But he’s also being honest. Turnovers are what’s killing this team.

Against the Packers, the Patriots turned it over four times, three interceptions from Maye and a fumble from Stevenson. And Maye himself admitted that the team still hadn’t played a single clean, turnover-free game this season.

And Vrabel pointed out before this game that the Lions are a team that doesn’t turn the ball over on offense. At all. So when you’re playing a team that doesn’t give you anything, and you’re the one giving the ball away, it’s almost impossible to win.

But here’s the thing. Maye is 24 years old. Last year, he led this team to the Super Bowl. He had five straight games with at least three total touchdowns this season. This is still one of the best young quarterbacks in the league. The turnovers can be fixed. And Vrabel clearly believes he’ll fix them.

Now let’s talk about the defense, because Vrabel had a very clear message there too.

He was asked what the defense needed to do. And he said it starts with getting off the field on third down, limiting explosive plays, and creating turnovers.

He said it’s tough to play defense in this league without creating some turnovers. So you have to find guys who can go take the ball away from the other team.

And then he broke down all the ways you can do it. Tip a pass. Hit a guy hard enough to knock the ball loose. Read the quarterback in zone coverage. Don’t let your man catch the ball in man coverage. And hope that disruption leads to somebody else picking it off.

He said there are a lot of ways to do it, and it starts by affecting the quarterback.

And that’s the big picture for this defense. Early in the season, this unit was elite. Before the Bears game, they hadn’t allowed a single run of 20 yards or more. They were one of the best defenses in the league against the run. But over the last few weeks, against Green Bay and now Detroit, the explosive plays have shown up and the turnovers haven’t.

So what’s the fix? Pressure on the quarterback. That’s where it all starts. Milton Williams, Christian Barmore, Harold Landry, K’Lavon Chaisson. These guys have to get home. Because when the quarterback is under pressure, the turnovers follow.

Vrabel also talked about what he expects from every player on defense. He was asked about responsibilities, and he said everybody’s job is to understand the call and the details of the play. Understand the concept.

He gave examples. It could be asking a defensive lineman to move from a five technique to a three technique. It could be containing the quarterback. It could be running a stunt. There are always things you have to do within your job.

And when it comes to coverage, he said nobody, especially the Patriots, can just play one coverage. That’s not possible. So every player has to understand the intent of the play, and what they need to do in each coverage, because responsibilities change.

That ties right into the message Vrabel gave earlier in the week. It’s all of us. Coaches have to teach it. Players have to execute it. And everybody has to know their job on every snap.

Now let’s talk about Rhamondre Stevenson. Because Vrabel had high praise for him.

A reporter asked about Stevenson being out there and at least giving it a shot, even with his knee injury.

And Vrabel said the team is always looking for players who are looking for ways in, as opposed to ways out.

And then he said something every fan needs to understand. At this point in the season, after 10 straight weeks without a bye, pretty much nobody out there is 100 percent. He said they have to recognize that. But they also have to find ways to do their job. And he said Stevenson is one of those players looking for a way in.

Patriots Nation, that’s a huge compliment. Stevenson didn’t need surgery on that knee. And instead of shutting it down, he fought to get back on the field for this game in Germany. That’s toughness. That’s the kind of player Vrabel wants.

And Vrabel tied it to the culture he’s trying to build. When he was asked what one quality makes someone fit this team, he said they talk about a lot of things that require no talent or skill. Competitive spirit. Communication. Toughness. Attitude. He said there are a lot of things you can do that require no skill, that can still help you win. And those are the qualities they’re trying to build.

So even after this loss, that’s the foundation. Effort. Toughness. Attitude. Those don’t change based on a result.

And Vrabel actually addressed that directly. Somebody asked if there’s any difference in how the team prepares after a win or after a loss.

And Vrabel said no. Not in this league. He said you have to stay consistent and understand that there’s another challenge ahead.

He said after a loss, you probably have some things to address that kept you from being successful. But in every game, there are going to be some good plays and some bad ones. And then there are the ones that get you beat. And he said those are probably more frequent in the games you end up losing.

Patriots Nation, that’s exactly the mindset this team needs right now. Two straight losses. A 5 and 5 record. But Vrabel isn’t going to panic. He’s going to stay consistent, fix the plays that got them beat, and go after the next challenge.

And the next challenge is a big one. After the bye, the Patriots face the Chargers on November 29. Then the Bills on December 6. Those are the games that will decide this season.

Now, a couple of lighter moments from Germany.

Vrabel gave a huge shout-out to the operations staff. He said the whole staff was phenomenal. Video, equipment, the training room, nutrition, security. He said everybody made the trip seamless. He said the team felt great about where they were and getting ready to practice, and that was all because of the staff.

But when it came to the players’ free time in Germany, Vrabel had a classic answer. He said that’s their time. He said he’s not going to babysit them and make them sit in a circle and hold hands. He said they’re grown men, and if they want to talk to somebody, they’ll talk to them. And he said their job is to go out there and play good football.

That’s Vrabel. Straight to the point.

He also explained why the team flew in a day early. He said, having done this a few times, he wanted to do a little more than what had been done in the past on a Friday. So they got there early, walked through, came over Thursday night, and walked through again, to help the players get moving and get the flight out of their bodies. Then Friday was a regular Friday.

And then there was a fun question. A reporter mentioned a world record set in the Swiss Alps, and asked Vrabel if he’d like to play a game in Switzerland. Vrabel said the team will play wherever the league wants them to play. And he said he’d love the chance to go over there and ski. He even asked the reporter for recommendations.
