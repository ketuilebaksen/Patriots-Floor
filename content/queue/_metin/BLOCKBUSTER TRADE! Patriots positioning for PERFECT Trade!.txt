Now let’s talk about the future, because that’s what makes this trade even smarter.

When you make a receiver trade, you have to look further than just this season. And this receiver room has some big contract questions coming.

Mack Hollins is set to become a free agent. Pop Douglas is set to become a free agent too. And Kyle Williams heads into his third year next season, which opens up options for the team down the road.

So the front office has to figure out who’s part of the long-term core. And Downs could be a key piece of that answer.

Yes, Downs has an expiring contract too. He’s set to be an unrestricted free agent after this season as well. But here’s the thinking. If you trade for him, you get his rights, you get time with him in your building, and you get a head start on an extension. The extension possibility with Downs could be very real, especially if you’re the team that goes out and gets him.

And you could argue his long-term value is higher than re-signing an aging veteran or paying big money to keep everybody. Instead of trying to bring back every free agent at once, you could build the future around Brown, Doubs, and Downs.

So why is Downs even available Why would the Colts consider trading a young receiver who’s playing well

First, the Colts have been playing poorly. When a team is losing, it starts looking at the future. Veterans on expiring contracts become trade chips.

Second, they just made a change at general manager. They fired Chris Ballard. And when a new front office takes over, it often wants to reshape the roster its own way. A new GM doesn’t have the same attachment to the players the old regime brought in. That makes them more willing to make moves.

And with Downs on an expiring contract, the Colts have to ask themselves a question. Do they want to pay him a big extension, or get value for him now A struggling team with a new front office might choose to cash in.

That’s why the timing for New England could be perfect.

Now, let me be clear about something, because I’m not going to sell you a fake trade on this channel. There’s no confirmed deal here. No reported contact between the teams. This is a target that makes sense, not a trade that’s done. And there are real hurdles.

First, Indianapolis has to be willing to listen. A new GM might want to keep Downs and build around him. We don’t know yet.

Second, you have to figure out the asking price. Downs is playing well, but he’s on an expiring contract. That usually lowers the price. But the Colts know he’s young and productive, and they’ll want real value. What’s the right number A mid-round pick More That’s going to be the big debate.

Third, if the trade happens, the Patriots have to plan for that contract extension. Because trading for a guy and then losing him in free agency would be a waste.

And fourth, it all has to work with the salary cap. Every dollar counts, especially with other contracts coming up.

So this isn’t a no-brainer. It’s a move that needs the right price, the right plan, and the right timing.

And there’s another thing to consider. The Patriots have to protect Drake Maye up front too. With Campbell’s knee and Moses’s ankle, the offensive line might need help as well. A name like Jack Conklin has come up as a possible option there. So the front office has to decide where the biggest need really is. Receiver depth, offensive line depth, or both.
