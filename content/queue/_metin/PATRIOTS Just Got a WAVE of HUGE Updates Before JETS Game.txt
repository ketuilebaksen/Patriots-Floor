Let’s start with the question everybody wanted answered. Is A.J. Brown playing on Sunday?

Vrabel’s answer? We don’t know yet. He said the team wouldn’t make that decision until the next day. They had to see how Brown responded to that day’s practice, and then go from there. And he made it clear Brown wasn’t the only one in that situation.

So who else is up in the air? Vrabel said they had to see how Will responds. That’s Will Campbell, the left tackle who hurt his knee against the Raiders. He said there would be some guys listed as questionable, and they’d see how TreVeyon responds in the morning. Then they’d evaluate and go from there.

Patriots Nation, that’s a lot of uncertainty heading into a division game. Your top receiver, your starting left tackle, and one of your running backs, all in wait-and-see mode.

But one thing was definite. Vrabel said Carlton Davis and Karan Prunty will be out. So the secondary is still short-handed, even with Christian Gonzalez cleared to come back. Gonzalez is going to have his hands full with Garrett Wilson, and the depth behind him is thin. That’s the reality.

Now let’s go back to Brown, because Vrabel gave us a really honest look at where he is.

When he was asked how Brown looked in practice this week, Vrabel said he looks okay. And then he said something I really appreciated. Like everybody, some good plays and some bad plays. No hype. No “he looks great, he’s ready to go.” Just honesty.

And Vrabel said they’re trying to see where Brown’s conditioning level is, and how he feels after that day’s practice.

That’s the key word. Conditioning. Brown has been out since the high ankle sprain he suffered in the opener. He’s been on injured reserve for the required four weeks. And getting back into football shape is a completely different thing from just being healthy. You can have a healed ankle and still not be ready to run route after route for four quarters against a division rival.

So here’s how I read it. Vrabel isn’t going to rush him. If Brown’s conditioning isn’t where it needs to be, he could sit, or he could play a limited role. And honestly? I’m good with that. Because the last thing this team needs is Brown going out there at 70 percent and getting hurt again.

But if he does play? This offense gets a lot scarier. Remember what Josh McDaniels said this week. Last year, the Jets doubled Stefon Diggs at times. If they try that with Brown, somebody else is left one-on-one. And a quarterback who just threw for 374 yards and four touchdowns is going to find that matchup.

Now, here’s one that flew under the radar, and I think it’s one of the most important parts of this whole press conference.

Vrabel was asked about the young left guard who’s played every single snap this season and has been with the team since the offseason. And his answer told us a lot about the long-term plan for this offensive line.

Vrabel said playing every snap has really meant a lot to the young guard. And he said he’s seen him improve. And then he said this was always the hope and the vision. That this guard and Will Campbell could keep developing together and really solidify the left side of the offensive line. Two players the team feels are talented, and still have room to grow.

Think about that. The left side of the line, built around two young players growing up together. That’s not a short-term fix. That’s a foundation.

Vrabel also said he can see how much it means to the young guard in the way he prepares, and the way he responds. And his message was simple. Keep moving forward, keep trying to progress.

And here’s why this matters so much right now. With Campbell questionable, the guard next to him becomes even more important. If Caleb Lomu has to make his first start at left tackle, he’s going to need help from the guy lined up right next to him. Communication. Calling out stunts. Passing off twists. A guard who’s played every snap this season brings stability to a rookie tackle who’s just getting his feet wet. That pairing could decide whether Drake Maye stays clean against the Jets.

And remember what Lomu did against the Raiders. He played 47 snaps, a career high, after coming in for Campbell. On 29 pass-blocking snaps, he allowed one pressure and no quarterback hits. And as a team, the Patriots held Maxx Crosby to zero sacks. If Lomu starts Sunday, the Jets will have had a full week to study him. That’s why having a steady, improving guard next to him is so valuable.

Next, Vrabel was asked about Draymond Jones, who has battled through an injury to get on the field this season. And this answer had a personal side to it.

Vrabel said the two of them are kind of from the same area. Growing up in Ohio, Jones went to the other Catholic high school, and he got to Ohio State after Vrabel did. So Vrabel has followed his career for a long time.

And Vrabel said he’s always admired the way Jones plays. His versatility. He called him a real professional. And he said Jones knows his stuff. When you’re asking him questions or going through the game plan, he’s got a good grasp of exactly what he needs to do based on what the coaches are asking.

Then Vrabel added something important. He said it’s been good for Jones to be in that room with some young guys, because of his experience.

That’s a big deal. On a team this young, veterans who know the playbook and can teach it are worth their weight in gold. Especially with injuries piling up. When young players get thrown into bigger roles, the veteran next to them who knows every assignment is the one who keeps the whole unit from falling apart.

Now let’s talk about the running backs. Because this is the position that’s been getting tested the hardest, and Vrabel had a lot to say about it.

First, Rhamondre Stevenson. Vrabel was asked what he’s learned about Stevenson and what he means to this team. And Patriots Nation, this answer was one of my favorites of the whole press conference.

Vrabel admitted he didn’t know Stevenson before he got the job. But he said Stevenson is a talented player who cares a lot about the team. A great teammate. With a good skill set, catching and running.

And then he went deeper. Vrabel said that even before he met Stevenson, he was watching tape, and what stood out was what kind of pass protector Stevenson was. The way he played without the football. His willingness to try to make blocks for other people when the ball wasn’t in his hands.

And Vrabel said those are usually a good indication of a guy who cares about the team. Somebody who’ll do whatever he has to do. The way a player plays without the football says everything about him.

Patriots Nation, that’s high praise from a coach who values toughness and selflessness above everything. And it lines up perfectly with what we’ve seen. Stevenson played through a questionable knee against the Raiders and still put up 64 yards and a touchdown on 13 carries. That’s a guy putting the team first.

And it connects to something bigger. Remember how Vrabel described the receiver room? Selfless. Happy when a teammate scores. On a pass, serve the quarterback. On a run, serve the running back. Now he’s praising Stevenson for blocking when he doesn’t have the ball. That’s the culture Vrabel is building. Everybody serving everybody.

And that pass protection Vrabel loves? It matters even more this week. If Lomu is starting at left tackle, Maye needs a running back who can pick up a blitz. Stevenson is that guy.

Then Vrabel was asked about another back, and this answer was fun. He said when this back gets the ball, usually the first guy doesn’t tackle him. Doesn’t bring him down. He said a lot of his explosive runs have come after contact, where somebody thinks they’ve got him, and no, they don’t.

Vrabel said he breaks a lot of tackles. He runs hard. And he called him a smooth runner who doesn’t look rigid. And he said he’s always liked him, even coming out of college.

That’s exactly the kind of runner this team needs right now, with Henderson banged up. A guy who turns contact into extra yards. A guy who makes the first defender miss.

Then came the question everybody was waiting for. What’s the level of trust in Cory Ker after last week’s fumble?

Remember, Ker fumbled on his very first carry against the Raiders. Then he bounced back with runs of 10 and 32 yards in the fourth quarter.

And Vrabel’s answer was outstanding. He said everybody has to take care of the ball. But he said Cory did a lot of really good things, so there’s high confidence.

And then he turned it around on the media. He said nobody notices or talks about the guy who comes back into the gap and hammers the ball out. He said you always just put it on the guy who has the ball.

Patriots Nation, that’s a coach protecting his player. And he’s right. Sometimes a fumble isn’t just the runner’s fault. Sometimes a defender comes free because a block was missed, and he gets a clean shot at the ball.

Vrabel said they all share in that, and they’ll be better. He said he likes the way Cory runs. Yes, he has to hang on to the ball. But the team also has to do a better job of protecting him and keeping people away from him.

That’s leadership. Hold the player accountable, but don’t hang him out to dry. Share the responsibility. And believe me, Ker is going to run even harder on Sunday after hearing his head coach say that.

Vrabel also talked about Ker’s preparation. He said Ker works extremely hard. This is a new system for him, and he didn’t have the luxury of being here since April like a lot of guys. So he works really, really hard with running backs coach Tony Dews.

And Vrabel said he loves Ker’s spirit and his energy. He’s willing to do whatever is asked of him to help this football team. He said Ker had some good runs last week, and they’ll see if they can get him more runs this week, keep developing him, and maybe get him on special teams too. And Vrabel finished by calling him a great person, and said again how much he loves his spirit.

So what does that tell us? Ker is going to get more opportunities. With Henderson’s status up in the air and Stevenson managing a knee, Ker could have a real role against the Jets. And his coach is fully behind him.

Now let’s get to the roster updates, because a couple of these could matter a lot in the coming weeks.

Vrabel was asked about Marcus Bryant. Remember, Bryant went on injured reserve before Week 1 with a return designation. And Vrabel said Bryant is another guy they’ll look at in the morning, to see about his elevation, and to see how he feels after a few days of work.

That’s interesting. Because the offensive line depth is getting tested. Mike Onwenu and Greg Van Roten are already on injured reserve. Campbell is questionable. If Bryant is ready, that’s another body for a group that badly needs one.

Then Vrabel was asked about Harold, who has been around the team but hasn’t been ready to play, and whether the plan is for him to return this season.

Vrabel said of course the plan would be for him to return at some point, when he’s ready to go. He said Harold has been around and he’s working extremely hard. He just isn’t ready yet.

And Vrabel described his routine. He comes in every single day, does his part being around the team in the morning, and then goes and does his rehab. Vrabel said they just have to be patient. He’s not ready, so they’re not going to put him out there. But when he’s ready, that’s the plan.

And that’s a theme we keep seeing from this coach. Patience. No rushing guys back. We saw what happened last January when Campbell played through a torn ligament in the playoffs. Vrabel isn’t going to repeat that mistake.

Finally, let’s talk about the rookie class, because Vrabel’s answer here was genuinely exciting.

Vrabel was asked what stands out about the rookie group as a whole, going back to the spring. And he said they’ve improved. They’ve played for the team in some good spots. And then he reminded everybody of something important. They’re 21- and 22-year-old kids. So it’s good to see them improve.

And then he said this. You have to invest in those guys, and you can’t lose sight of them. Maybe they’re not playing 80 percent of the snaps, but you have to keep investing in developing them, to see what they can do and how they can help.

He mentioned Eli. He mentioned Caleb, and the whole group. And he mentioned Karan, who was coming on and really gaining confidence as a gunner on special teams. Vrabel said they have to keep doing that, and that it’s exciting.

And then came my favorite line of the entire press conference. Vrabel said the rookies are very coachable. Much more coachable than some of the veterans. And that’s why he likes coaching those guys.

Patriots Nation, that’s a coach who loves teaching. And when you have a coach who’s that invested in young players, those young players get better. That’s exactly what we’ve seen with Lomu. Thrown into the fire, and he held up.

Vrabel was also asked about a player returning to special teams, someone the special teams coordinator had been praising. And Vrabel said you can hear how much special teams means to him. You can hear him communicating. He understands the concepts, the scheme, and what’s going on, and he can communicate that to everybody. Vrabel said there’s an energy there. And then there’s the physical skill set. Being fast, and able to play multiple positions.

Special teams doesn’t get the headlines. But in a division game, one big special teams play can swing everything.

So let’s put this whole wave of updates together.

A.J. Brown’s status won’t be decided until the last minute, and Vrabel is watching his conditioning closely. Will Campbell is questionable. TreVeyon Henderson is being evaluated in the morning. Carlton Davis and Karan Prunty are out. Marcus Bryant might be elevated. And Harold is still working his way back.

But beyond the injury report, we learned a lot about what Vrabel values. He’s building the left side of the line around two young players he believes in. He loves Stevenson for what he does without the ball. He’s standing behind Cory Ker after a fumble, and sharing the blame. He trusts veterans like Draymond Jones to lead the young guys. And he’s fully invested in a rookie class he calls more coachable than some veterans.

That’s a coach with a clear vision. And for a team coming off a 45 to 7 win, that’s exactly what you want. Because as Vrabel told his locker room, none of last Sunday’s success matters against the Jets. Every week starts at zero.

The Patriots are 3 and 2. The Jets are 2 and 3. A division win at Gillette would put New England in a strong spot, with Chicago coming up on Thursday night and a road trip to Miami after that.

So here’s what I’m watching on Sunday. Is Brown active, and how much does he play? Who starts at left tackle? Who carries the ball, and does Cory Ker get more work? And can a thin secondary hold up against Garrett Wilson?
