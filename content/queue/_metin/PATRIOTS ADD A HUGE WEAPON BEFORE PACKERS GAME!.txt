Derrick Henry. Thirty-two years old. On Thursday night, he passed LaDainian Tomlinson for seventh place on the NFL’s all-time rushing list. And on that same night, his team lost 33 to 10, starting a backup quarterback.

Let’s start with the big question. Why would Baltimore sell Derrick Henry?

Because things in Baltimore have fallen apart.

The Ravens are now 4 and 5. They’ve lost four straight games. To Cleveland. To Cincinnati. To Buffalo. And now to Jacksonville. And their quarterback situation is a mess.

And look at Henry’s rushing totals in those four losses. 58 yards. 45 yards. 53 yards. 57 yards. Not one game over 60.

So the Ravens are in a tough spot. And when a season starts slipping away, veterans become trade chips.

And here’s the part that makes this deal so realistic. The contract.

According to AtoZ, Baltimore only owes Henry about 600,000 dollars for the rest of this season, because his bonuses have already been paid. And next year’s 12-million-dollar salary has no guaranteed money.

So if the Ravens want to get a draft pick for him, this is the cheapest week to do it.

And for the team that trades for him? You’re taking on almost nothing this season. Remember, the Patriots don’t have a ton of cap space, even after restructuring Hunter Henry’s contract. Most trade targets have been tough to fit. Derrick Henry fits easily.

And AtoZ’s own trade simulator put his price somewhere between a late second-round pick and a late third-round pick.

That’s it. Cheap price. Cheap contract. A future Hall of Famer.

Now, before we talk about New England, we have to ask the obvious question. Is Derrick Henry washed?

Because the Ravens’ record isn’t the only thing that’s changed this year.

In 2024, Henry ran for almost 2,000 yards. Last year, he ran for more than 1,500. This year, he’s at 689 yards through nine games.

And look at how this season has split. In his first four games, he ran for 374 yards and seven touchdowns. In his last five, he has 315 yards and just one touchdown. That’s a drop from 93.5 yards per game, down to 63.

But some of that is the scoreboard. In the 36 to 10 loss in Buffalo, Henry had just 11 carries. In the 33 to 10 loss in Jacksonville, he had 17. Both games got away from the Ravens early. And when you’re getting blown out, the running back stops getting the ball. The King just wasn’t being fed.

And he’s not on the decline list yet. Henry has 13,650 career rushing yards. That’s fourth among the 12 running backs with 10,000 or more since 1999.

AtoZ’s Easton Freeze put it perfectly. He said this isn’t about the dreaded running back cliff, where a guy wakes up one day and he’s washed. The cliff he’s talking about is the cliff of perceived value. In other words, Henry is still a good player. It’s just that teams see him as older, so his trade value has dropped.

And after Thursday’s loss, Henry made it clear he still wants to be a difference-maker. He said anything he needs to do to help the offense go, that’s what he’s going to do.

He ran 17 times for 57 yards that night. That’s a back who still wants the ball. The question is whether Baltimore still wants to give it to him.

So here’s the verdict. He’s not washed. He’s a 32-year-old with a lot of miles, who still scores touchdowns. And what’s left is the perfect short-term rental.

Now, here’s the New England side. And this is where it gets exciting.

The Patriots are 5 and 3. Back in February, they played in the Super Bowl, where Seattle beat them 29 to 13. This team knows what it takes to get there. And it wants to get back.

But the backfield has been the question mark all season.

Rhamondre Stevenson has 367 rushing yards on 96 carries. That’s 3.8 yards per carry.

TreVeyon Henderson has 203 yards on 52 carries. That’s 3.9 yards per carry. And he’s been on the injury report lately with an illness. He missed the Miami game and didn’t travel with the team. And he’s been out of practice this week too.

And Henry? He has 689 yards on 175 carries. That’s also 3.9 yards per carry.

So here’s the honest part. On yards per carry alone, Henry isn’t really better than what the Patriots already have.

But here’s the difference. The end zone.

Henry has eight rushing touchdowns this season. Stevenson and Henderson combined have just three.

Eight to three. That’s the gap.

And remember what Mike Vrabel said after the Bears loss? The difference in that game was the red zone. They didn’t convert their red zone chance when it mattered. A goal-line back like Derrick Henry changes that completely.

And here’s the connection that makes this even more perfect. Henry isn’t walking into a stranger’s building.

He and Mike Vrabel were together with the Tennessee Titans. That’s where Henry became the King. That’s where he won a rushing title. Vrabel knows exactly what he can do and exactly how to use him.

AtoZ’s Austin Stanley put it simply. The Patriots have a need at running back, and Vrabel loves Henry.

And AtoZ’s Ryan Roberts was blunt about the current backfield. About Stevenson, he wrote that he’s neither efficient nor ball-secure, and that he puts the ball on the turf far too often. And about Henderson, he wrote that the problem is the Patriots just don’t trust him.

Now, I want to be fair to Stevenson. He’s been a great teammate, and Vrabel has praised his pass protection and the way he plays without the football. And he just had his best day in a while last Sunday in Miami. 82 yards on 19 carries, 4.3 yards per carry, in that 24 to 19 comeback win.

But the bigger picture is clear. This team could use a reliable finisher.

And think about what Henry would add to Drake Maye’s offense.

In the loss to Chicago, Maye went 24 of 31 for 321 yards and two touchdowns. In Miami, he went 16 of 20 for 199 yards, two passing touchdowns, and a rushing touchdown, with zero interceptions. He’s put up at least three total touchdowns in five straight games. He looks like himself again.

A quarterback playing like that, with a running game at 3.8 yards per carry. That’s the gap Henry would fill.

Imagine the play-action. Defenses would have to load the box to stop Derrick Henry. And the moment they do, Maye throws over the top. That’s how you make an offense almost impossible to defend.

And then there’s the schedule. Because this is where every piece matters.

After Tuesday’s deadline, the Patriots have nine games left. It starts Sunday against Green Bay. Then the Detroit Lions in Munich, Germany. Then the Chargers. Then the Buffalo Bills on December 6th. Then Minnesota. Kansas City. The Jets. Denver. And Miami to close it out.

Nine games. And every one of them counts in the AFC East race.

AtoZ said the AFC East still runs through Josh Allen and the Bills. And in their words, Henry would be another weapon in that fight.

And the Patriots have shown both sides of what this team can be. They beat the Raiders 45 to 7 at home. They won in Buffalo, 29 to 26. And they lost 35 to 6 in Jacksonville back in September. Adding a weapon like Henry helps make sure the good version shows up more often.

That’s the window where one more piece can matter in a huge way.

Now, let’s compare Henry to the other back everybody wants. Jonathan Taylor.

Taylor is 27 years old. Bleacher Report says the Colts are open to trading him. But they immediately turned down an offer of a 2027 second-round pick plus another pick.

AtoZ’s Freeze said Taylor’s market looks like a second-round pick, maybe a second plus a little extra.

Henry, on the other hand, is a late second to a late third in AtoZ’s simulator. And all three of their writers lean toward a third-round pick.

So that’s the difference. For second-round money and more, you get a 27-year-old running back. For a third, you get a 32-year-old with eight rushing touchdowns this season alone.

And the Colts still haven’t moved Taylor. So if teams can’t get him, Henry is the back who’s actually available, for a lot less.

Now, let’s talk about the competition. The New York Giants.

The Giants are 4, 2, and 1. And they’ve been aggressive about running backs over the last couple of weeks. That’s because their running back, Cam Skattebo, dislocated his right ankle in the Week 8 loss to the Eagles, and he had surgery.

And Henry knows the Giants’ head coach very well. John Harbaugh, who coached him in Baltimore in 2024 and 2025.

But AtoZ isn’t sold on New York. They wrote that the real question is whether the Giants are actually a contender, and they don’t think they are. They said the Giants shouldn’t let their record fool them into pushing all their chips in this year.

And Harbaugh himself admitted the cupboard is thin. He said the Giants aren’t loaded with 12 or 13 draft picks.

So for the Giants, Henry would be the move they can afford. For New England, he’d be a luxury piece for a real contender. And that’s the difference AtoZ is pointing to. The Patriots are the stronger fit.

Now, here’s the catch. And I want to be completely honest with you, because that’s how we do things on this channel.

AtoZ is the only outlet that has tied the Patriots to Derrick Henry so far. And they’re citing their own sources. Nobody else has matched this report yet.

ESPN’s Jordan Raanan wrote on Wednesday that the idea of Henry being available in a trade never seemed realistic to him. Although, to be fair, that was before Thursday night’s loss to Jacksonville.

And Raanan reported that the Giants’ known target is actually Jonathan Taylor, with Henry as the cheaper backup plan.

So this is not a done deal. It’s a report from one outlet. But it’s worth watching very closely, because of the Vrabel connection, the cheap price, and the clear need.

And notice the timing. Sunday’s game against Green Bay comes before the deadline. So if this happens, Henry’s first game as a Patriot could be in Munich, Germany, on November 15th, against the Detroit Lions. Imagine that. King Henry’s Patriots debut on an international stage.
