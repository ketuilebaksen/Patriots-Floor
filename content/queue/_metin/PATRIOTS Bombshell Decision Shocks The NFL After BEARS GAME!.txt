So, the big question. Why is a player as good as Quenton Nelson even available?

Because the Indianapolis Colts are a mess.

On Monday, they fired general manager Chris Ballard, the day after losing 26 to 20 to a Titans team that hadn’t won a single game all season.

And here’s the part that really matters. Last November, Ballard traded the Colts’ 2026 and 2027 first-round picks to the Jets for Sauce Gardner. And since that trade, the Colts have gone 3 and 11.

Think about what that means. The Colts are losing this year. But they don’t have their own first-round pick. So losing doesn’t even help them in the draft. What helps them now is picks. And the Patriots can offer picks.

Dave Helman of The Athletic said that if he were the Colts’ GM, his first order of business would be trading Nelson and DeForest Buckner to make up for those missing first-rounders.

And ESPN reports that Nelson is expected to generate interest from around the league as the Colts keep struggling. Which means the Patriots won’t be the only team calling. The Giants, the Packers, and the Buccaneers have all been floated as possible fits.

And the contract situation makes this even more interesting. Nelson’s four-year, 80-million-dollar extension from 2022 runs out after this season. So if the Colts keep him and he walks in free agency, they could lose him for nothing but a compensatory pick in 2028.

Now, to be fair, Nelson says he isn’t thinking about any of this. He told the Indianapolis Star that he leaves that up to God, and he’s just focusing on being his best.

And NFL.com reports that the Colts’ de facto interim GM, Ed Dodds, hasn’t hinted that he wants to move Nelson. But of course, that could change with the right offer.

And Albert Breer has already put the Patriots’ name on it. On Patriots Pregame Live, he said to keep an eye on the Patriots, because they have injury issues at guard right now. And he called Nelson a multi-time All-Pro, and maybe even a future Hall of Famer.

So how good is Quenton Nelson right now, at 30 years old? Let me tell you. He’s not just good. He might be playing the best football of his career.

Nelson has made the Pro Bowl in all eight of his NFL seasons. Every single one. He’s only the second offensive lineman in league history to do that. He has six All-Pro selections. And he was first-team All-Pro in each of his first three seasons.

And this year? Through six games, PFF has Nelson as the highest-graded guard in the entire NFL, with an 89.2 overall grade. First out of 77 qualified guards.

On 248 pass-blocking snaps, he’s allowed six pressures and zero sacks. Zero.

Look at what he did against Pittsburgh in Week 5. The Colts lost the game, but Nelson had the highest PFF grade of anybody on the field. And he allowed just one pressure on 47 pass-blocking snaps.

And here’s the thing the Patriots desperately need. He doesn’t miss games. He started all 17 games in 2024. And all 17 games in 2025. On a team that’s used 14 different offensive line combinations, durability like that is gold.

Now think about where Nelson plays. Left guard. And that’s exactly the spot Alijah Vera-Tucker just left open. Right next to Will Campbell at left tackle.

You’re not asking Nelson to learn a new position. You’re not asking him to switch sides. You’re asking him to do exactly what he’s done for the past eight years, in front of a 24-year-old quarterback, for the rest of the season.

Picture it. Will Campbell and Quenton Nelson on the left side. That’s a wall. And Drake Maye finally gets the clean pocket he needs to step up and throw the deep ball, the way he did against the Raiders, with that 99.9 deep passing grade.

And the run game? The one that got 44 yards on 13 carries in Chicago? Imagine Rhamondre Stevenson or Cory Ker running behind Quenton Nelson. That changes everything.
