Now let’s get to Drake Maye. Because that’s what all of this comes back to.

Here’s what we know right now. Adam Schefter reported that the Patriots are concerned Maye has a fractured ankle. Ian Rapoport reported that it’s a significant lower leg injury that will keep him out for an extended period of time.

But neither of those is an official diagnosis. And Mike Vrabel said it himself. It’s a lower leg injury, and he doesn’t know the exact details yet. The team kept Maye in Germany out of caution, and Vrabel said he’d be back shortly. Maye’s tests are now being done back in New England. So we’ll see what the official diagnosis is.

What Vrabel did say is that he wouldn’t imagine Maye being available when the team comes out of the bye.

And that means a streak is probably about to end. Before this injury, Drake Maye had started 43 straight games. That’s the third-longest active streak in the NFL, behind only Josh Allen at 146 and Jared Goff at 82. If Maye sits against the Chargers on November 29, which looks like the most likely scenario, the streak ends at 43.

And let’s look at what Maye was doing before the injury. He was averaging 8.4 yards per attempt, with a 73.3 QBR. He had also thrown 11 interceptions, which has been a real issue. But he was still the engine of this offense.

Against the Packers on November 8, he threw for 299 yards and three touchdowns, even in a 45 to 27 loss where he also threw interceptions. And in Munich, he was only on the field for one drive. He went 3 for 4 for 48 yards before he got hurt.

And there’s a big question hiding inside that decision. For a 5 and 5 team that just lost its franchise quarterback, is sticking with DeVito a sign of trust? Or is it a sign of something else?

And right now, Patriots fans have started pushing one name in particular. Charlie Becker.

Becker has been popping up all over Patriots social media. But let’s be clear. No media outlet has officially tied him to the Patriots. One analyst has said that, looking at the draft, Becker would be an awesome fit. So this is a fit, not a report. But it’s worth talking about, because Becker is really good.

Becker is a 6-foot-4 wide receiver at Indiana. In 2024, his only touch was a three-yard rushing touchdown. But in 2025, with Fernando Mendoza as his quarterback, he caught 34 passes for nearly 680 yards and four touchdowns in 16 games. That’s 20 yards a catch, the seventh-best mark in the FBS.

And this year has been even better. Through 10 games, he has 53 catches for 950 yards and 10 touchdowns. And he’s doing it without the Heisman Trophy winner throwing to him. He’s already passed everything he did in 2025.

He’s averaging 95 yards a game. He’s had at least three catches in every single game this season. He’s scored in six of his 10 games, and he’s gone over 100 yards in four of them.

And he shows up in the big games. Against Ohio State, he led the team with nine catches for 165 yards. In one of Indiana’s overtime games, against Minnesota, he caught eight passes for 149 yards. Against Western Kentucky, he had a 79-yard touchdown, his longest catch of the season. Last year, his longest was 53 yards.

Ten touchdowns on 53 catches means he scores on about one of every five catches. Against Rutgers, he had eight catches for 107 yards and two touchdowns. Against North Texas, four catches for 64 yards and two more touchdowns. And against Howard, he scored three touchdowns on three catches.

But let’s be fair. There are weak spots too.

In the more physical games, his numbers dropped. Three catches for 52 yards against Nebraska. Three catches for 32 yards against Michigan. Three catches for 75 yards against USC.

Pro Football Focus says he’s caught 23 of 35 contested targets, and he hasn’t dropped a single pass in his college career. That’s impressive. But PFF also says his route running is still developing.

PFF projects him as a top-15 pick, and says he could be the second wide receiver taken in this class. And PFF lists the Chargers, the Titans, and the Buccaneers as his best fits. The Patriots aren’t on that list. But with Maye out, depending on where the Patriots end up in the draft order, they could come into the picture.

Indiana is 9 and 1 right now, coming off its national championship. And in mock drafts, Becker is all over the place. Washington. Dallas. Kansas City. He’s going anywhere from the top 10 to the late 20s.

So where would the Patriots be picking? Right now, the Patriots’ own pick is projected right around the 14 range. That’s a placeholder, and it’s going to move a lot. And importantly, the Patriots still own their 2027 first-round pick. In the A.J. Brown trade, they sent their 2028 first-round pick, not their 2027 pick, along with a fifth-round pick.

And the need is real. Romeo Doubs has been injured this year. A.J. Brown has been injured. DeMario Douglas is going to be a free agent. Mack Hollins is going to be a free agent. There are a lot of moving parts coming in that wide receiver room.

Now, spending a top pick on a receiver is a lot when you also have problems on the offensive line and on defense. But if the Patriots end up in that range, I think Becker is worth thinking about.
