Announcement number one. A.J. Brown is going to Germany.

This was the first question Vrabel got. Was Brown expected at practice? And was the plan still for him to travel?

And Vrabel said yes. He said they’d see what Brown could do. And he said that almost everybody was going to Germany, except for a few players. And he included Brown in the traveling group.

Then a reporter followed up. Does going to Germany mean Brown will play?

And Vrabel made it very clear that it doesn’t. He said there will be players who go to Germany who won’t play. A lot of players, in fact, who travel but won’t be active. And he said he didn’t know yet who those players would be.

So what happened after that? Brown practiced throughout the week. And now, on game day, he’s listed as questionable. That’s a lot better than most people expected at the start of the week, when some fans thought he might be headed back to injured reserve.

And here’s why it matters so much. Brown has played in only three games this season, with nine catches for 101 yards. He’s been fighting an ankle injury since Week 1, and according to Adam Schefter, compensating for that ankle is what flared up his knee. He missed the Miami game and the Green Bay game.

But the Lions’ secondary has been banged up this week. Cornerback D.J. Reed didn’t practice earlier in the week, and safeties Kerby Joseph and Brian Branch were limited. If Brown is healthy enough to run his routes with speed, there could be real matchups to win.

And with the bye week right after this game, the Patriots face a choice. Play him today, or give him about three full weeks of rest before the Chargers.

Announcement number two. No regrets about the deadline.

The trade deadline came and went, and the Patriots didn’t add a single player. No Jonathan Taylor. No Derrick Henry. No Josh Downs.

A reporter asked Vrabel if he was disappointed.

And his answer was short and simple. He said he’s excited to get ready for the Lions. That’s all he can do right now. That’s it.

He said that had been the focus before the deadline too. And he said it was good to get the team in on Tuesday afternoon, to get a jump start on the week, to understand the schedule, how they were traveling, and what the plan was. And he said the Lions are another good football team, so that’s where the focus is.

No complaining about the front office. No talk about who they could have gotten. Just the next opponent.

And now, today, that decision gets its first real test. This roster, as it stands, has to go to Germany and beat a 6 and 2 team. If it does, standing pat looks smart. If it doesn’t, the questions about the deadline are only going to get louder.

Announcement number three. The Lions are the real deal.

Vrabel gave a full scouting report on Detroit, and he had a ton of respect for them.

He said the Lions know how to win. He said they’ve built a program he knows they’re proud of. And he called Dan Campbell a good football coach.

He said Jared Goff is playing at a high level, and has been for the last three years. Great skill players. Speed. Physical. He said their defense has played really well over the last three weeks. And he said they stay aggressive on fourth down and on special teams.

And then he dropped the stat that matters most. The Lions don’t turn the ball over on offense. At all.

He said that’s because they simply haven’t put the ball in harm’s way. But they still create explosive plays.

And he pointed to Jahmyr Gibbs, who he said leads the league in touches.

He also talked about the Lions’ offensive coordinator, saying the run game and the scheme still mirror what Detroit has always done, with a few new wrinkles in the passing game. And he said the coordinator gives Goff a lot of freedom at the line of scrimmage to get the offense into the right play.

So here’s what that means for today. This is a team that doesn’t beat itself. If the Patriots want to win, they can’t beat themselves either. And that’s been the problem. Drake Maye admitted after the Packers game that the team still hasn’t had a single clean, turnover-free game this season. Against a team that doesn’t turn the ball over, every giveaway could be the difference.

Announcement number four. It’s all of us.

This was one of the most important moments of the press conference.

A reporter asked about defensive game planning. Is there enough cohesion among the coaches? And do players have any say in the game plans?

And Vrabel said cohesion is exactly what the staff wants. He said that’s what they’ve done since he became a head coach. Get everyone’s ideas, figure out what works, and then present the game plan to the players.

And then he made his point. He said he knew where the question was going. Every time the media questions a player’s ability to do his job, the answer becomes, it’s got to be the coaching.

And Vrabel said it’s all of us.

He said the coaches talk to the players, and the players give feedback. But in the end, every player has a job to do. Block your man. Throw the ball to the open guy. Run where they’re not. Cover. Rush. Tackle.

And he said the coaches have to teach the players the skills to do that, and give them the confidence to go out and do it every week.

Patriots Nation, after giving up 45 points to Green Bay, that’s exactly the right message. No finger-pointing. No blaming one coordinator or one player. Everybody owns it together.

And you can see that message spreading through the locker room. Milton Williams just put a 1,000-dollar fine on any defender who jumps offsides or commits encroachment, after the team had seven of those penalties in the last two games. The money goes to the training staff. That’s a player taking ownership. That’s “it’s all of us” in action.

Announcement number five. Getting back to 53.

A reporter asked about the open spot on the roster.

And Vrabel said the team would probably get to 53 players. He mentioned Greg Van Roten returning. And he said fullback Reggie Gilliam would be back practicing too, which he called a good thing. But he said it was only Wednesday, the first practice of the week, and it would be important to see how those players did.

So where does that stand on game day? Van Roten is listed as questionable. And so is Walter Rouse, who entered the concussion protocol earlier in the week.

And that’s a big deal, because the interior of the offensive line is already short. Ben Brown, the center, is out and didn’t travel. Mike Onwenu is out for the season. Alijah Vera-Tucker is on injured reserve.

So if Van Roten can go, he gives Drake Maye a veteran presence at guard. He started for two straight seasons with the Giants before coming to New England. And against a Lions defense with 27 sacks, the third-most in the league, experience on the interior matters a lot.

Gilliam could help too. A healthy fullback can lead the way in the run game and help in pass protection. And with TreVeyon Henderson out, and Rhamondre Stevenson questionable with his knee, the Patriots might lean on heavier formations with Cory Ker in the backfield.

Announcement number six. The IR spots are running out.

A reporter asked Vrabel if there was a chance Rhamondre Stevenson lands on injured reserve.

And Vrabel said there’s a chance anything could happen, but he wasn’t going to speculate. And then he pushed back a little. He said, let’s get past the injuries, and focus on the Lions and what the team needs to do to win.

But then he added the part that every Patriots fan should understand. He said if you pay attention, the Patriots don’t have many spots left to bring players back from injured reserve.

Here’s why that matters. The NFL only gives each team eight designations per season to bring players back from IR. And according to Evan Lazar, the Patriots have already used five: A.J. Brown, Marcus Bryant, Mack Hollins, Greg Van Roten, and Reggie Gilliam.

If Romeo Doubs and Alijah Vera-Tucker use the next two, that’s seven. One left for the rest of the season.

So putting Stevenson on IR isn’t a simple decision. It could cost the team one of its last remaining return spots. That’s a big reason the Patriots are being so careful with him.

And the good news? Stevenson didn’t need surgery on his knee. He was limited in practice on Thursday, took part in warm-ups on Friday, and now he’s listed as questionable for today. A lot better than the multiple weeks people feared at the start of the week.

And there’s one more part of the press conference that’s going to matter today. Quick snaps and discipline.

A reporter asked what the defense can do when offenses use quick snaps against them.

And Vrabel kept it simple. He said when the center has his hand on the ball, the defense should probably be ready. And when the offense uses a hard count, the defense should probably wait until the ball is snapped.

He said offenses protect with seven blockers and snap it quickly, with three receivers in the route. And they do that against defenses that bring pressure on third down or move players around. He said the Patriots have done okay with it, but there are times they could be a step quicker getting lined up. And he said third down overall hasn’t been a problem.

That ties directly to Milton Williams’s new fine. Seven offsides and encroachment penalties in two games. Hard counts and quick snaps are exactly how offenses draw those penalties. And Jared Goff, with all that freedom at the line of scrimmage, is going to try it. The defense has to stay disciplined.

And Vrabel said practice was critical for exactly this reason. He said after the walkthrough on Tuesday, the team needed to clean up the first and second down plan, and get some speed work in. He said it would be hard to replicate Gibbs’s speed in practice, because the Patriots don’t really have anybody who runs the way Gibbs does. So the focus was on communication, getting speed in, and understanding the first, second, and third down game plan before they traveled.

With Carlton Davis out, the secondary needs him to be the star he’s been for most of this season. Kindle Vildor could be called up to help, the same Vildor who forced a fumble and earned a game ball against the Raiders.
