Let’s start with why this win matters so much. Because a 24 to 19 win over a team that came in at 1 and 5 might not look like much on paper. But context is everything.

Think about where this team was a week and a half ago. They had just lost in Chicago, 30 to 24, on a short week. The three-game winning streak was over. Mike Vrabel was talking about missed tackles and red zone failures. Drake Maye was standing at the podium saying, “I let my team down.” And the injury list kept getting longer.

And then, the day before this game, two more big names got ruled out. A.J. Brown, with a knee issue that flared up during the week. And TreVeyon Henderson. That’s 27 targets from those two players alone that suddenly had to go somewhere else.

So this team walked into Hard Rock Stadium with one of the thinnest receiver groups in the league. Pop Douglas. Kyle Williams. Efton Chism III. And a practice squad call-up.

Remember the road record coming into this game? The Patriots were 1 and 3 away from home this season, with losses in Seattle, Jacksonville, and Chicago. Now, let’s be honest about something. A 24 to 19 final against a 1 and 5 team isn’t a blowout. It was a fight. And that’s okay. Division road games are almost always tight. Now let’s get to the surprise moves. Because they’re a big part of this story.

The first surprise came before kickoff. When Brown and Henderson were ruled out, the Patriots had to fill two spots. And they did it with two names most fans had barely heard of.

They signed running back Lan Larison to the 53-man roster. And they elevated wide receiver Ryan Miller from the practice squad for the game.

And here’s what surprised people. The Patriots didn’t have to sign Larison. He still had two practice squad elevations left. They could have just called him up for this one game, the same way they did with Miller. Instead, they gave him a full roster spot. And nobody had to be cut, because the team already had an open spot.

So why make that call? There are a few possible reasons. Practice squad players can be signed away by any other team at any time. By putting Larison on the 53-man roster, the Patriots protect him. Nobody can take him. That tells you the team likes him more than people realize.

And Larison has real production in his background. At UC Davis in 2024, he ran for 1,465 yards and 17 touchdowns. He was the Big Sky Offensive Player of the Year in 2023. Over 50 college games, he piled up 3,634 rushing yards, 41 rushing touchdowns, and 127 catches. The Patriots signed him as an undrafted rookie in 2025, but a foot injury in the preseason wiped out his whole rookie year. This season, he’d only been called up once, in Week 1, when he returned two kickoffs for 49 yards.

So this was a guy waiting for his chance. And now he has a real spot on the roster.

The second move was Ryan Miller. And this one had a storyline you couldn’t make up.

Miller was on the Dolphins until October 13th, when Miami waived him. The Patriots signed him to their practice squad on October 19th. And on Sunday, he went back to Hard Rock Stadium to face the team that cut him less than a month earlier.

Miller has bounced around the league. Undrafted out of Furman in 2023, where he actually started as a tight end before moving to receiver. Then Tampa Bay. Then the Giants. Then Miami. And now New England. Four teams in two years.

And here’s the crazy part. Back in Week 2, Miller caught a 77-yard touchdown from Malik Willis, the same Dolphins quarterback he faced on Sunday.

But the main reason the Patriots elevated Miller was special teams. He has 421 special teams snaps in his career. And with two players out and one open spot, the Patriots wanted somebody who already knew exactly what to do on kickoffs.

And that mattered in this matchup. Because the last time these two teams met in Miami, special teams decided the game. In September 2025, Malik Washington returned a punt 74 yards for a touchdown to put Miami ahead, and then Antonio Gibson answered with a 90-yard kickoff return for the Patriots on the very next play. Vrabel specifically warned about Washington this week.

And Miller also brought something else. Inside knowledge. Receivers coach Todd Downing said Miller was still learning the verbiage of the play calls. But he’d seen Miami’s defense up close in practice. He knew their tendencies. In a game decided by five points, every little edge counts.

And the third move happened before all of this. And it might be the biggest one, because it’s still in play.

The Patriots converted half of Hunter Henry’s base salary into a signing bonus. That created about 3.3 million dollars in cap space, bringing New England to around 5.2 million total.

Henry didn’t take a pay cut. It’s a restructure. It spreads the cost over future years, giving the team breathing room right now.

And the timing tells you everything. The trade deadline is November 10th. Eight days from now.

So now the question is, what does this team do with that money?

Because after this win, the Patriots are 5 and 3. They’re in the thick of the AFC playoff race. And the front office has to decide whether this roster is good enough as it is, or whether it needs one more push.

And honestly? This win makes the case for buying even stronger. This team just won a road division game without its top receiver, without one of its top running backs, and with backups all over the offensive line. Imagine what it could do with a little more help.

Let’s think about where that cap space could go.

The offensive line. This has been the biggest problem all season. Mike Onwenu is out for the season. Alijah Vera-Tucker is on IR. Greg Van Roten is on IR. Ben Brown has been dealing with a knee injury. Through six games, the Patriots had used 14 different offensive line combinations.

Quenton Nelson, the highest-graded guard in the NFL through six games, with zero sacks allowed, is the dream. His remaining salary at the deadline would be around 8 million dollars. With 5.2 million in space now, the Patriots are a lot closer than they were. One more small move, and it could work.

And Andrew Wylie, the two-time Super Bowl champion that ESPN’s Bill Barnwell suggested for just a late-round pick swap, now fits comfortably.

But there’s another surprise that could be coming. And it’s not an addition. It’s a possible exit.

According to Albert Breer, the Patriots are reportedly open to trading Christian Barmore.

That one caught a lot of fans off guard. Because Barmore has been one of the most talented interior defensive linemen this team has had. Back in 2023, he put up 8.5 sacks, 16 quarterback hits, and 13 tackles for loss. And alongside Milton Williams, he helped anchor a strong defensive line on the 2025 team that went to the Super Bowl.

His playing time has gone down this season, and his production hasn’t been at that same level. And when he was asked about the rumors, Barmore said he’s doing everything the coach asks, and that questions about his future should go to the coaching staff.

Nothing has been confirmed. No deal has been reported. But the fact that his name is out there, eight days before the deadline, means it’s worth watching.

And after this win, the question gets even more interesting. Does a team that’s 5 and 3 and fighting for a playoff spot really want to trade away a proven interior pass rusher? Or does it use Barmore as a trade chip to bring in help somewhere else?

Personally, I’d only move him if the return is real. A pick that helps the future, or a player who fills a bigger need. I don’t want to see this team weaken the defensive line just to make a move. Especially with the young edge rushers like Elijah Ponder and Gabe Jacas developing. Interior pressure helps them win on the outside.
