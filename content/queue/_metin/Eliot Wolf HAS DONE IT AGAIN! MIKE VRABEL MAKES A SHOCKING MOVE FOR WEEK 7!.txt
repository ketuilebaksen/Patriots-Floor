Let’s start with the part of the press conference that should make every Patriots fan proud. The undrafted players.

A reporter asked Vrabel what it means to a program when undrafted players like Ponder, Woods, and Chism rise up and have the kind of success they’ve had.

And Vrabel’s answer was simple. He said it doesn’t really matter what you did before you got here. It just matters what you do when you get here.

That’s the whole philosophy right there.

Vrabel explained that the team tries to identify guys who can help, because not everybody gets drafted. There are only so many draft picks available. So they try to find players who can contribute. And if those guys do, they get more opportunities. And then Vrabel gave all the credit to the players.

But Patriots Nation, let’s give some credit to the people who found them too. Because this is exactly where Eliot Wolf’s front office has hit a home run.

Look at Elijah Ponder. A kid from Cal Poly. 32 teams passed on him. And now Five and a half sacks this season. At least half a sack in five straight games. Seven tackles for loss. Ten quarterback hits. A forced fumble and two fumble recoveries. He’s on pace for a season that would make a first-round pick jealous.

Look at Efton Chism III. Undrafted, and now earning more and more opportunities in a receiver room that badly needs him with Romeo Doubs and Mack Hollins on injured reserve.

And Charles Woods, another undrafted player making his mark.

That’s how you build a contender without spending top draft capital on every position. You find the guys everybody else missed. Then you coach them up. And then you give them a real shot.

Now let’s get to the shocking move. Caleb Lomu.

A reporter asked Vrabel if he expects Lomu to get some reps inside this week. Not at tackle. Inside, on the interior of the line.

And Vrabel said Lomu will have to be ready to go at multiple positions. He said they were even getting him ready during the Jets game to potentially move inside in different scenarios. And wherever he ends up on Thursday, he’ll have to be ready for a couple of spots.

Think about that, Patriots Nation. This is a rookie who was drafted with an eye on eventually replacing Morgan Moses at right tackle. And in the last two weeks, he’s played left tackle against the Raiders, right tackle against the Jets, and now he might be lining up inside against the Bears.

And he’s been great everywhere. Against the Raiders, he stepped in for Will Campbell and allowed just one pressure on 29 pass-blocking snaps, while the team held Maxx Crosby without a sack. Against the Jets, he played 59 snaps at right tackle without allowing a single pressure, including 16 one-on-one pass protection matchups with David Bailey.

That’s not a rookie surviving. That’s a rookie thriving. And now the coaching staff trusts him so much they’re willing to move him anywhere on the line.

And here’s why they might need to. Vrabel was asked whether Ben Brown or Alijah Vera-Tucker would be available this week. His answer Probably not.

So the interior is getting thin. Vera-Tucker, the left guard Vrabel has praised for playing every single snap this season, is dealing with a hamstring. Ben Brown has a knee issue. That’s two interior linemen likely out.

But there’s some good news too. A reporter brought up that Morgan Moses was listed as limited on the estimated injury report. And asked if Moses has a chance to play. Vrabel said, I think he does.

So if Moses can go at right tackle, that frees Lomu up to slide wherever the line needs him most. Maybe inside. Maybe at tackle. Wherever the hole is. That’s the kind of flexibility a coaching staff dreams about.

Vrabel also praised the backups who stepped up against the Jets. He was asked how Matt Prior and Walter Rouse performed when the line got squeezed by injuries.

And Vrabel said it was fun to watch those guys take advantage of the opportunity. He said they helped. And he pointed to the huge drive at the end of the game, the one where the Patriots went down the field and took time off the clock. That was the Cory Ker drive, eight straight touches, 42 of the drive’s 51 yards, more than 6 minutes off the clock. Vrabel said they’d have loved to finish with a first down at the end, but going up two scores was somewhere they were happy to be.

That drive doesn’t happen without the backup linemen opening holes. Next man up, once again.

Now let’s talk about the Bears, because this is a tough matchup. And Vrabel didn’t sugarcoat any of it.

A reporter pointed out that the Bears have the top rushing offense in the league right now. And that the Patriots struggled a bit against the run against the Jets. So how much of an emphasis is stopping the run going to be

Vrabel said it’ll be a huge key. He said the Bears have really good talent. The offensive line is excellent. They’re well coached. It’s a good scheme. The backs run hard. And he pointed out something a lot of people miss. When you can run the football, the receivers have to be a big factor in that, and the Bears’ receivers are. They block. They force the issue.

So the Patriots are going to have to do a great job against the run. But Vrabel also said there’s a lot more to what the Bears do than just running the football.

And that brings us to their play-action. A reporter asked what makes Chicago’s play-action concepts so hard to defend.

Vrabel explained that all play-action concepts are hard to defend, depending on how well somebody’s running the football. He said it’s usually two- or three-man route concepts. But it’s all about the ability to show and sell the run, and actually block the run, which the Bears do well. And once you do that, defenders start coming up to stop the run, and that creates chances for chunk plays. Overs, shots down the field, level routes.

So here’s the challenge for this Patriots defense. Stop the run, but don’t overcommit. Because the moment you bite too hard, the Bears will throw it right over your head.

Then Vrabel was asked about the Bears’ head coach, Ben Johnson, and quarterback Caleb Williams, and what’s made them so effective together.

Vrabel pointed to how few sacks Williams has taken. The ball coming out quick. The use of motion, which helps Williams figure out where he wants to go with the football. And then Vrabel said something every Patriots fan should pay attention to. A running game is a quarterback’s best friend, especially a young quarterback’s. And the Bears are committed to it.

Vrabel said Ben Johnson has had success everywhere he’s been, whether in Detroit or now in Chicago. And he’s sure Johnson enjoys developing a young quarterback. He said Williams’ sack numbers are down and he’s taking care of the football. And he called it a huge challenge.

And honestly, that sounds a lot like what the Patriots are trying to do with Drake Maye. Protect the young quarterback. Run the ball. Get it out quick. It’s a battle of two young quarterbacks who are both growing up fast.

Now here’s the quote of the day. Maybe the quote of the season.

A reporter suggested that Caleb Williams’ mobility seemed somewhat limited in the Bears’ game against the Falcons, coming off an injury. So does Vrabel prepare for a less mobile Williams Or does he expect him to be himself

And Vrabel said, and I love this, I’m not buying that lie. I hope the players don’t either. And then he added, he’s as fast as he needs to be.

Patriots Nation, that’s Mike Vrabel in a nutshell. He’s not falling for any of it. He’s not letting his defense relax for one second. If Williams looks slow on film, Vrabel assumes it’s either temporary or a trap. And he’s preparing his players to chase down the full version of Caleb Williams.

That’s exactly what you want from your head coach before a road game on a short week. No excuses. No assumptions. Prepare for the best version of your opponent.

And Vrabel was asked about Williams’ decision-making. He called it good. He said it’s decisive. Sometimes Williams gets the ball out of his hand quickly. And when he decides to turn and extend plays, whether he’s scrambling to throw or scrambling to run, there’s been a decisiveness to it. And if nothing’s there, Vrabel said, it’s tough. He’s extending, he’s spinning, he’s breaking tackles, he’s escaping the pocket.

So this defense has to stay disciplined. Contain the edges. Don’t let Williams escape. And Elijah Ponder, with all that pressure he’s been creating, is going to have to finish plays, not just reach the quarterback.

And Vrabel also talked about the Bears’ defense, because the Patriots offense has a tough test too.

He was asked about Dennis Allen, the Bears’ defensive coordinator. And Vrabel said Allen has the ability to game plan and change things up. He said there were some things each week that you thought might repeat, and they didn’t. And he said that’s a testament to the coaching, and to players who understand how they need to adjust.

And then he said third down is probably where Allen has made his mark, going all the way back to his time in New Orleans. A very sound defensive approach.

And when asked why the Bears are so effective on third down, Vrabel explained it. They’re creating third-and-longer situations by winning on first and second down. They disguise well. They blitz. And their off-the-ball players, linebackers and defensive backs, contribute heavily to the pressure. Whether they’re rushing four or bringing an off-the-ball guy, Vrabel said you saw the impact a player named Gordon had for them last week.

So what does that mean for the Patriots Win on first and second down. Stay out of third-and-long. And that’s where the running game matters so much. Cory Ker. Rhamondre Stevenson. Getting positive yards on early downs so Drake Maye isn’t staring at third-and-9 against a defense built to punish it.

And speaking of Maye, a reporter asked Vrabel what he thought about Maye’s decision-making after reviewing the Jets film. And Vrabel kept it short. He said he hopes Maye makes great decisions on Thursday night. That’s the most important thing.

Short. Simple. Focused on the next game. Pure Vrabel.

And Maye has been making great decisions lately. Two straight games without a turnover, after seven turnovers in the first four weeks. That’s the version of Maye this team needs against a Dennis Allen defense that lives on third down.

Now let’s go back to how Vrabel is handling the short week, because this is a real challenge.

He was asked about the mental side of a Thursday night road game. And he explained the approach. You come in after the game and treat the next day like a Wednesday, and get rolling. And he tries to explain to the players that by Thursday night, they’ll feel better physically. So you can’t let how you feel physically affect your mental preparation. He said that’s something they’ve focused on in short weeks over the years.

And there was a funny moment too. A reporter asked how much the team practiced against Bears looks the Friday before. And Vrabel said none. Their focus was completely on the Jets. He said it’s hard in this league to win a game each week, so you focus on one game at a time. He said he never tries to look ahead.

That’s the discipline. One game. Every week. And it’s worked. Three straight wins.

But the short week also brings a secret weapon. Familiarity. Vrabel was asked how the team gets ready for a non-conference opponent with so little time.

He said the staff looks at a group of teams in the offseason. And he said some coaches were able to get ahead. He mentioned Shane getting ahead defensively, Charles helping, and the quality control coaches. He said it’s always nice on a short week to have people familiar with the system.

And a reporter pointed out that Shane, in one of his last games as a defensive coordinator in New York last year, faced this exact Bears offense. Vrabel said it helped to have him go back, look at that game, break it down, and come up with some ideas.

And there’s one more inside source. Kevin Byard. Byard has experience with the Bears. A reporter asked how much the team can draw from him this week.

Vrabel said Byard may be able to share some things, personnel-wise, things he thinks or things he saw. But he said you have to be careful. The Bears do a lot. Byard isn’t going to know when they’re going to line up in a Wildcat, or when Caleb Williams is going to decide to scramble and extend. So there are things Byard can help with. But in the end, what happens in the game will be decided by how the Patriots react and play.

That’s smart coaching. Use every edge you’ve got. But don’t rely on it.

Now let’s talk about the players Vrabel praised on his own team.

First, Christian Elliss. A reporter pointed out that Elliss has become an every-down starter, after being a role player and mostly a special teamer when he first arrived.

And Vrabel had glowing things to say. He said Elliss cares deeply about the team. A great teammate. Somebody everyone enjoys seeing at work every day. He studies. He practices. He’s always trying to improve, and he keeps getting better. And he makes a huge impact on the defense. And then Vrabel said this. Elliss is like a lot of guys, somebody you would notice if he wasn’t here.

That’s one of the highest compliments a coach can give. Not every player is a star. But some players hold everything together. And you only realize how important they are when they’re gone.

And against the top rushing offense in the league, a linebacker who studies and makes the right calls is going to be absolutely critical.

Then Vrabel was asked about Hunter Henry, and how important he is with all the injuries at receiver.

Vrabel said Henry has been a consistent presence since he arrived. A veteran leader. He’s performed and produced every single year of his career. And he said quarterbacks like guys who are dependable.

And Henry has been exactly that. Against the Jets, he caught four of five targets for 48 yards and scored the Patriots’ last touchdown. And in each of the last three weeks, he’s topped his best receiving game from the first three weeks. With Doubs and Hollins out, Henry is going to be one of Maye’s most important targets in Chicago.

And Vrabel also talked about Eli, a young player who’s been growing since he arrived in April. Vrabel said Eli was trying to figure out how to block people at this level. And he said Eli blocks better now than what he’d shown in previous exposure. He said he’s growing and gaining confidence every day, and called him an exciting young player who’s fun to work with.

That’s the theme again. Young players. Development. Confidence.

And then there was Romeo Doubs. Vrabel was asked how much it hurts to lose him, and whether he expects Doubs to come back this season.

Vrabel said the expectation is that Doubs will work hard and get treatment. But he said it wouldn’t be fair to anybody to say exactly when he’ll be back. He said Doubs was continuing to build confidence, and he made a huge play in the game against the Jets, that explosive play, the touchdown. And it’s just unfortunate. Vrabel said the team will have to figure things out without him for a little while, and he’s not sure how long that will be.

So no timeline. But the team just signed Ryan Miller to the practice squad for depth. And now A.J. Brown, Pop Douglas, Kyle Williams, Efton Chism III, and Hunter Henry have to carry this receiving corps.
