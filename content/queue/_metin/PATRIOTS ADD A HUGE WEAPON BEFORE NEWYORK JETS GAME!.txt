Because a key receiver might be coming back, a rookie is about to face a real test protecting the quarterback’s blind side, and the running back room is suddenly running out of bodies.

Let’s start with the biggest news. The weapon. A.J. Brown.

Brown is back at practice after the required four weeks on injured reserve for the high ankle sprain he suffered in the opener. He was limited on Wednesday and Thursday. And Patriots Nation, just seeing him out there in pads again is a big deal.

Think about what this offense did without him. 536 total yards against the Raiders. Maye throwing for 374 yards and four touchdowns. DeMario Douglas, Hunter Henry, and Kyle Williams all finding the end zone. And they did all of that with their top receiver on the sideline.

Now imagine adding a player with Brown’s track record on top of that.

Josh McDaniels welcomed him back and talked about the fresh energy he could bring to the receiver room. And McDaniels mentioned something that should have every Patriots fan paying attention. Last year, the Jets doubled Stefon Diggs at times. Two defenders on one target.

And think about what that means. When a defense sends two guys at one receiver, somebody else is left one-on-one. And a quarterback with Maye’s arm is going to find the better matchup. If New York decides Brown is the guy they have to double, then Douglas, Williams, and Henry are suddenly working with more space. That’s how one receiver changes the entire passing picture for everybody else.

That’s what a true number-one target does. He doesn’t just catch passes. He bends the defense toward him.

Now, I have to be honest with you, because that’s what we do on this channel. Brown left his Sunday status up in the air. He said it’s Mike Vrabel’s decision. And the word that should guide that decision is readiness.

I get it. Trust me, I get it. You want your top target back the second he puts his cleats on. Production from the lead receivers has been uneven without him. And one Brown catch could swing a tight division game.

But there’s a big gap between returning to practice and handling a game, snap after snap, against a division opponent. This isn’t as simple as active or inactive. If Brown can accelerate, stop, fight for the ball, and run repeated routes without major restrictions, Maye gets a threat that stops the defense from crowding the easy throwing lanes. That’s a game changer.

But if Brown still needs his workload managed, McDaniels has to adjust, and the other receivers will carry most of the load anyway. And here’s the thing. A name on the lineup card doesn’t change coverage. Opponents react to what a player can actually do on the field, not to an announcement before kickoff.

So here’s what I want. I want Brown making the Jets choose a problem. I don’t want the Jets seeing a limited role after a few series and adjusting like he’s just a decoy. If he plays, I want him to play like A.J. Brown.

So watch the signals this weekend. Practice participation. The final injury report. The activation decision. If Brown is limited, Maye needs the offense’s design to create separation. If he returns in good shape, New England can punish every bit of extra attention the Jets throw at him.

Now let’s talk about the backfield, because this is where things got worse this week.

TreVeyon Henderson suffered a high ankle sprain against the Raiders, and he’s expected to miss several weeks. That’s a big loss. He’d shown real flashes, four carries for 22 yards and a 16-yard catch before he got hurt. And now he’s out for a while.

And Rhamondre Stevenson is dealing with a recent knee issue of his own. He played through it against Las Vegas and still put up 64 yards and a touchdown on 13 carries. But a running back managing a knee is a running back you have to be careful with.

When the backfield loses its margin for error, the offense needs more than just somebody to take handoffs. It needs enough players to practice, share the workload, and keep one absence from shrinking the whole game plan. Even a backup’s availability matters when a coaching staff is trying to build a full game week around that position.

And that’s where the first move comes in.

Terrell Jennings was in Foxborough on Friday, October 16th, for a workout with the Patriots. According to ESPN’s Mike Reiss, it was an evaluation. Let me be clear on that. No signing has been announced. But this small move tells you exactly what New England is trying to manage before facing the Jets.

Jennings knows the building. He spent time with the organization in 2024 and 2025. He joined this year’s offseason and preseason work, then landed on injured reserve in August. An injury settlement later ended that stint.

And the workout makes a lot of practical sense. The staff can see how he moves right now and compare it to the work they’ve already seen from him. They already have a frame of reference. Friday just gives them a current look.

And knowing the system matters a lot during game week. Especially when there’s little time to explain assignments from scratch.

Now, I’m not going to sell you a Friday workout as a ready-made Sunday solution. That’s not how this works. Vrabel needs to know what job Jennings could actually handle, and whether he’s healthy enough to handle it.

And I can already hear some of you. “If Jennings was already here and became available, why would he suddenly be the answer now?” Fair question. Totally fair.

Here’s the answer. Henderson’s injury changed the need. The backfield didn’t look like this back in August. That doesn’t suddenly make Jennings a better player. But it might make his familiarity more valuable, when the team has very little time to make a decision.

Think about the alternative. A veteran from outside might look better on paper. But if he has to learn the offense from scratch, he might give you less immediate help. The Patriots could burn precious practice time teaching him the language of the offense. If Jennings is healthy and passes the workout, he starts without that delay. Familiarity helps. It doesn’t promise production. But in the middle of a game week, it counts for something.

So what would his actual job be? If Stevenson can play and Henderson can’t, the team needs to divide carries, get through practice, and keep options for passing downs. This workout doesn’t prove Jennings would be the third-down back or handle a big workload.

So watch for three things. An agreement. The roster spot it uses. And who’s actually available on game day. A signing would show New England wants coverage. His spot and his eventual snaps would tell us much more. Is he trusted with real offensive work? Or is he there to give the room enough bodies to function? That distinction matters, because it tells us whether the backfield problem has been solved or just managed for another week.

And here’s what bothers me as a fan. It’s how easily a 45 to 7 win can make everything feel settled. It isn’t.

Maye produced against Las Vegas. But the next game might not give him the same conditions. With fewer reliable runs, the Jets could squeeze the passing windows and force him to create too much on his own. We’ve seen that version of Maye. Through Week 3, his PFF passing grade was the lowest among 26 qualified quarterbacks. Nobody wants to go back there.

Jennings might help with depth. But the question about offensive variety is still there. And that’s exactly why the next name is so interesting.

But Patriots Nation, this is where things get really interesting. While New England works out a familiar backfield option, a far more expensive idea has surfaced.

Alvin Kamara.

Athlon Sports named him as someone New Orleans could consider trading if the Saints decide to sell before the deadline. And that gives Patriots fans a lot to talk about.

But let me be clear. No contact between the teams has been reported. This is a market possibility, not a rumor of a deal. Actual interest would need credible reporting. I’m not going to pretend otherwise.

So why does Kamara even fit this discussion? It’s pretty clear. Henderson is expected to miss weeks. Stevenson has his own health question. And this offense has found real speed with Maye in command.

An experienced pass-catching back like Kamara could offer a short outlet against pressure. He could create yards in space. He could share the work with whoever’s available. And more than just adding depth, McDaniels would gain a role that forces defenses to respect both the run and the pass without knowing the answer from the formation. Line Kamara up in the backfield, and the defense doesn’t know if it’s a handoff, a screen, or a route out of the backfield. That’s a nightmare for a defensive coordinator.

Picture it. Maye under pressure, the blitz coming, and he flips it to Kamara in space. That’s the kind of play that turns a sack into a first down.

But a fit on a whiteboard still has to survive the cost. And there are real costs here.

A pick spent on a 31-year-old veteran is a pick that can’t fill another need. Kamara would have to learn the system mid-season. And Henderson should eventually come back. So what’s Kamara’s role when everyone’s healthy? If that answer can’t justify the price and the snaps taken from other players, his reputation can’t rescue the deal.

And New Orleans doesn’t have to sell, either. Yes, the Saints opened 1 and 4. Yes, they invested heavily in Travis Etienne Jr. And Kamara’s restructured deal doesn’t commit him beyond this season. Those factors could make the front office listen to offers.

But Etienne is on injured reserve, which cuts into the Saints’ own backfield depth. And Kamara has said he wants to finish his career in New Orleans. A team can plan for the future and still decide not to trade a player like that right now.

So where do I land? I wouldn’t rule out making the call. Maye threw for 374 yards and four touchdowns against the Raiders. A team seeing that kind of growth should be investigating every opportunity to surround him with weapons. But investigating also means being ready to walk away when the price is bigger than the value of the role.

I want aggressive questions and a disciplined offer. That’s how good front offices work.

So let’s be clear about where things actually stand. Jennings coming in for a workout is a reported evaluation of depth. Kamara is a market possibility. One doesn’t cancel out the other. New England can look for immediate coverage and still keep checking whether a trade would actually improve this offense.

For now, the immediate plan also depends on a less glamorous piece of the offense. Protection on the left side.

Caleb Lomu took over at left tackle after Will Campbell got hurt against the Raiders. The rookie was a first-round pick brought in to give this line flexibility. He worked on the right side during preparation, and he looked like the guy who might eventually take over for Morgan Moses.

Now he has a completely different assignment. Protect Drake Maye’s blind side while Campbell is out.

And Lomu looked good against Las Vegas. Really good. But the Jets game brings a longer test, with a defense that knows from the very first snap who’s lining up there. They’ve had a whole week to study him. They’ll design pressures to attack him.

Here’s how I see it. A promising debut doesn’t have to turn into a vote on who the starter should be. Campbell had answered a lot of the doubts from his playoff and Super Bowl performances with his play early this season. Another injury to the same knee raises concern about his availability. It doesn’t wipe away what he showed through those first five games.

Lomu should be judged by the snaps he’s about to play. Not by some position battle people imagined before he had a real sample as a starter. If he wins his matchups, great. Maye is safer, and the staff has more options when Campbell comes back. That doesn’t force an instant change in the pecking order.

And the left tackle spot changes the whole calculation we just made about Brown and the backfield. A receiver who draws attention can create opportunities, but Maye needs enough time to get to them. A versatile back can give him an outlet against a blitz, but that back can’t replace dependable blocking on every down.

And if New England has to send steady help to the left side, that help has to come from somewhere else. Maybe less freedom to release a tight end into a route. Fewer receivers going out. Fewer ways to shape the play. I’m not saying the Patriots will have to do that. I’m saying that’s the cost the staff has to avoid if Lomu struggles. This offense needs its options to stay open.

But there’s another possibility, and it’s worth getting excited about.

If Lomu holds up on his own in pass protection, and keeps the run game from stalling on his side, New England gets a lot more than a temporary fill-in. It gets proof that a draft investment is already giving them real coverage at one of the hardest and most expensive positions in football to fill.

For a team trying to fight its way back to the top of the AFC, that’s roster building with a direct impact on Sunday. It’s not a flashy trade. It’s the ability to keep the playbook open when a starter goes down. And honestly? That might be the most valuable weapon of all.

The good news wouldn’t be some forced controversy at left tackle. It would be finding out the line can take a hit without making Maye pay for it.

Now, an informed fan can push back here. One sample against the Raiders isn’t enough, and the Jets’ pass rush can attack the rookie’s inexperience. I agree with that completely.

So I’m not going to judge Lomu by sacks alone. Watch whether New England keeps sending help his way. Watch whether Maye can work through his reads. And watch whether the runs have a lane on the left side. When Campbell is ready, performance, health, and fit on both sides should shape the decision.

Until then, the priority isn’t crowning a new starter. It’s getting through this matchup without shrinking the offense to hide the line.

So here’s what I’m demanding from Foxborough this week. Maturity. Address the lack of depth in the backfield. See physical proof before bringing A.J. Brown back into a full role. And investigate expensive opportunities like Kamara without falling in love with the name.

The Raiders win showed this offense has real power. Sunday will show us the structure underneath it.

We still need to know who’s available, what Brown’s condition really is, and how Lomu responds to a pass rush that’s been planned specifically for him. Watch the final injury report. Watch the activation decisions. And watch the first few drives on Sunday, because they’ll tell us everything about how confident this staff really is.

If the Patriots get Brown back healthy, keep Maye protected, and find enough answers in the backfield, this offense could be a real problem for the Jets, and for everybody else in the AFC East.
