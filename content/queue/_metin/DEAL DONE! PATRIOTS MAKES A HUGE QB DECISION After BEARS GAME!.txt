The Bears are favored by about three points at home. DraftKings had them at minus 148 on the moneyline, with the Patriots at plus 124. In other words, the market thinks Chicago wins this game more often than not.

Now let’s get to the last-minute news. And for the Patriots, it starts up front.

Mike Vrabel ruled out both starting guards. Alijah Vera-Tucker with a hamstring, and Ben Brown with a knee.

So Walter Rouse will make the first start of his NFL career at left guard. And Matt Prior starts at right guard. And here’s a wild number. That’s the fourth different starting right guard in seven games this season for the Patriots. Four. In seven games.

These are the same two guys who helped close out the Jets on that eight-run Cory Ker drive. They earned this chance. But now they have to do it from the very first snap, against a defense that’s had all week to study them.

The big question is right tackle. Morgan Moses is questionable with a foot injury. When Vrabel was asked if Moses had a chance to play, he said, “I think he does.”

And if Moses can’t go, it’s first-round rookie Caleb Lomu. And Lomu was quietly fantastic on Sunday. On 30 pass-blocking snaps against the Jets, he gave up zero sacks, zero hits, and zero pressures. Zero across the board. If he has to start tonight, I’m not panicking.

In the secondary, Carlton Davis, Karan Prunty, and Channing Canada are all out. That leaves Christian Gonzalez, Marcus Jones, Charles Woods, and Kindle Vildor.

And watch the inactives closely. The Patriots signed some offensive linemen this week who could get called up from the practice squad. And tackle Marcus Bryant could come off injured reserve. With the line this banged up, every body counts.

He’s off the injury report. And after Sunday, he wants this one. Bad.

Okay. Now for the advanced numbers. And this is where it gets really interesting.

When the Patriots have the ball, the Bears’ defense is two completely different teams.

On third down, they’re a wall. Opponents convert just 25.8 percent of their third downs against Chicago. That’s the second best in the NFL. And they sack the quarterback on 9 percent of dropbacks, also second best.

Remember what Vrabel said this week about Bears defensive coordinator Dennis Allen. Third down is where he’s made his mark. They disguise. They blitz. Their off-the-ball players bring pressure. That’s the strength.

But on first and second down? The Bears rank 29th in the NFL in EPA per play.

Quick explanation. EPA is expected points added. Basically, how much every play moves the needle on the scoreboard. And on early downs, the Bears give up a lot of needle.

And here’s the biggest crack of all. Throws that travel 20 yards or more in the air. Opponents have completed 13 of 23 of those against Chicago this season. And by EPA per deep throw, that’s the worst deep pass defense in the NFL.

Now look at the Patriots offense. They have the sixth-highest explosive play rate in the NFL. 10.8 percent of their plays go for a pass of 20-plus yards or a run of 10-plus.

And Drake Maye has completed 13 of his 26 throws of 20-plus air yards. Over his last three games, he leads the entire NFL in passer rating, at 119.3, and in EPA per dropback.

Remember the Raiders game? Four of six on deep throws for 149 yards and two touchdowns, with a 99.9 deep passing grade. That’s the Maye who’s showing up tonight.

So the math is simple. The Bears want to drag you into third-and-long, and then come after you, with your two new guards. The Patriots want to hit the big one before third down ever shows up.

And that’s the huge quarterback decision for this game. Does New England unleash Drake Maye early? Take the shots on first and second down, when Chicago is at its weakest? Or play it safe and risk ending up in exactly the situations the Bears want?

Maye knows it too. He said this week that you’ve got to be good on first and second down, because the Bears are really good at getting off the field.

I say let him cook. Early and often.

Now let’s flip it. Because the Bears’ offense plays a completely different sport.

They lead the NFL in rushing, at 160.7 yards a game. They lead the NFL in time of possession, at 36 minutes and 1 second a game. Their offense runs 73.2 plays a game. And their defense? It’s only on the field for 50.2 plays a game. NFL.com says that’s the fewest defensive snaps per game since at least 2000.

Think about that. The Bears’ defense is so fresh because the offense keeps the ball forever.

Vrabel put it simply this week. He called it a physical football team. He said offensively, they extend drives and they run the football.

But here’s the good news for New England. The Patriots are one of only two teams that haven’t allowed a single run of 20 yards this season. Not one. And they’ve given up just 91.8 rushing yards a game, the eighth fewest in the NFL.

So this is a real strength-on-strength battle. The best rushing offense in football against one of the stingiest run defenses.

And with Monangai out, Chicago’s run game goes through D’Andre Swift. He has 398 yards and six touchdowns on 94 carries.

And then there’s Caleb Williams. So let’s settle this one properly. Maye against Williams. The numbers that matter.

Completion percentage? Williams leads the NFL at 72.5. Maye is at 63.9.

Completion percentage over expected? Williams again, plus 9.8 to Maye’s plus 5.2.

Yards per attempt? Maye has the edge, 8.0 to 7.8.

Touchdown passes over the last three games? Maye has 10. The most in football.

And interceptions? Maye has seven. Williams has just one.

So here’s the whole game in one sentence. Williams is the safer passer. Maye is the bigger play.

The Bears are built to grind you. The Patriots are built to beat you over the top.

And here’s a stat that tells you a lot about Williams. He doesn’t throw deep. His average throw travels just 6.1 yards. That’s 31st out of 33 qualified quarterbacks. And he’s dead last in how often he attempts deep passes.

So the Bears are going to try to dink and dunk, run the ball, and keep Maye on the sideline. And Vrabel already warned his team not to fall for any talk about Williams being slowed down by injury. He said, “I’m not buying that lie. I hope the players don’t either. He’s as fast as he needs to be.”

So the Patriots defense has to stay disciplined. Contain him. Don’t let him extend plays.

So here are the keys. Four things the Patriots have to do to win tonight.

Key number one. Take the shot early. On first and second down, when the Bears are 29th in EPA, and before the blitz shows up. That’s where Maye’s deep ball lives. Against the worst deep pass defense in the NFL, this is the night to let it fly.

Key number two. Protect the middle. Two guards who’ve never started next to each other, against a defense that sacks quarterbacks at the second-highest rate in the league. Quick throws. Max protection on the deep shots. And get Maye out of the pocket when it breaks down. The good news? Montez Sweat is out. That helps.

Key number three. Win first downs on defense. If Swift is getting four yards on first down every time, the Bears hold the ball for 40 minutes, like they did against Atlanta last week. And Maye is stuck on the sideline watching. This defense has to make Chicago one-dimensional. And with a run defense that hasn’t allowed a 20-yard run all season, they can do it.

And key number four. The big one. Stop turning the ball over.

The Patriots have lost the turnover battle in all six games this season. Every single one. They’re 30th in the NFL in turnover margin, at minus 6. And Vrabel is fed up. After the Jets game, he said they turned the ball over at a historic rate, and called it ridiculous. An embarrassment.

And guess who’s taken the ball away more than anyone since the start of last season? The Bears. 42 takeaways.

That’s the whole thing, Patriots Nation. If the Patriots win the turnover battle for the first time all year, I think they win this game.

And there’s real hope here. Drake Maye has gone two straight games without a turnover. He’s fixed his part. Now the backs and receivers have to protect the ball just as well.

The Patriots come out victorious, 31 to 28.

Yes, the backup guards are a concern. But they held up against New York. And this defense knows how to stop the run. Expect a lot of pressure from Mike Vrabel’s defensive front, with Elijah Ponder and his five and a half sacks leading the charge.

A shootout in Chicago. And the Patriots win their fourth straight.
