Let’s start with the biggest move. Early this morning, the Patriots released punter Bryce Baringer.

Now, why is this being called a ruthless move? And why, when you look deeper, might it actually be the right thing to do, for both the team and for Baringer?

Let’s go to ESPN’s Mike Reiss, who covered this in detail.

Reiss pointed out that Baringer was a critical, steadying, professional presence for kicker Andy Borregales and long snapper Julian Ashby when they were both rookies in 2025. Think about that. Two young specialists coming into the league, and Baringer was the veteran who helped them settle in. That’s the kind of locker room value that never shows up in a box score.

But then came the tough break. Baringer suffered an injury in the preseason. And that injury led to the Patriots adding Mitch Wishnowski.

And now, Reiss explained, the Patriots are facing a numbers crunch with the injured reserve return limit. And that’s where this so-called ruthless move actually starts to make sense.

Let’s look at who Baringer is. He’s been a three-year starter for this team. Last year, he played in all 17 games for New England. And he was in the final year of his contract, set to become an unrestricted free agent after this season, on a deal worth about 1.2 million dollars this year.

So why did New England move on?

His preseason injury put the Patriots in a tough spot. He was going to be out for at least four games. And you can’t go through an NFL season without a punter. So they signed Mitch Wishnowski. And Wishnowski has held the job ever since, and he’s been really solid.

Let’s look at the timeline. On September 1st, Baringer was placed on injured reserve. The next day, September 2nd, Wishnowski joined the practice squad. He was called up for the next three games. But practice squad players can only be elevated three times before you have to sign them. So on September 22nd, Wishnowski was signed to the 53-man roster. And now, about a month later, Baringer has been released.

And Wishnowski has earned it. He’s averaging 47.1 yards per punt. He’s already over 1,000 punt yards. And the big thing for me is he’s been getting the ball inside the 20. That’s exactly what you want from a punter. Pin the opponent deep and make them drive the length of the field.

Now here’s the part that really explains this move. And it’s a part a lot of fans don’t know about.

In the NFL, there’s a limit on how many players you can bring back from injured reserve during a season. No matter how many players you put on IR, you only get a certain number of return designations. Eight per season.

And here’s the problem for the Patriots. They have somewhere around 20 players on injured reserve right now. A lot of them are players the team really wants back. And according to the reporting, New England has already used three of its return slots. Which means only five remain.

Five. For a team with this many injuries.

Let’s think about who’s on that list and who the Patriots would want back.

Romeo Doubs. He went on IR after hurting his shoulder against the Jets. Before the injury, he led the team in receiving yards with 261. Vrabel said there’s no timeline, but the expectation is he’ll work hard and get back.

Mack Hollins. On IR with a calf injury. He had 209 receiving yards and brought a physical presence in both routes and blocking.

Alijah Vera-Tucker. The left guard went on IR right before the Bears game with a hamstring injury, and he’s out for at least four games. Vrabel has called him part of the long-term vision for the left side of the line.

Greg Van Roten. The backup guard, on IR with a thigh injury.

And there’s Harold Landry, who Vrabel said is working extremely hard every day and will return when he’s ready.

So think about the math. Five slots. And you’ve got a starting receiver, another key receiver, a starting guard, a backup guard, and a veteran edge rusher all hoping to come back. And that’s not even counting anybody else who gets hurt between now and January.

Every one of those return slots is precious. And using one on a punter, when you already have a punter doing a great job, just doesn’t make sense.

That’s the real reason behind this move. It’s not about Baringer’s talent. It’s about protecting those return slots for the players this team will desperately need down the stretch.

And here’s the part that might surprise you. This release could actually be a good thing for Baringer.

Think about his situation. If the Patriots kept him, he’d most likely stay stuck on injured reserve for the rest of the season. Yes, he’d keep getting paid. But at the end of the year, he’d be a punter heading into free agency who didn’t punt at all this season. That’s not a great spot to be in when you’re trying to land your next contract.

Now, he’s free. He can go find a fresh opportunity. Maybe with a team that needs a punter right now. Maybe somewhere he can show what he can do and earn a longer-term deal.

So yes, it stinks. The only reason he’s being released is because the Patriots don’t want to use an IR return slot on a punter. But it also gives him a real chance to go prove himself somewhere else.

And Reiss said something that I think every Patriots fan agrees with. Baringer is a good player, and he’s always been generous in the locker room and with the media. Everyone around the team hopes he lands with a contender and does well.

It’s just unfortunate. It’s not the way this team was set up this year.

And honestly, it puts in perspective how tough the NFL is. Baringer did everything right. He played every single game for years. And then he got injured at the wrong time, in a contract year. And suddenly, he’s in a tough spot.

And that’s not just true for punters. It’s true for every player in this league. Get hurt in the wrong season, and your career can take a completely different path. Look at Will Campbell last year, playing through a torn ligament in the playoffs. Look at the four guards who went down this season. This league is brutal on bodies.

Now, Baringer’s release wasn’t the only move the Patriots made today. They also brought back a familiar name. Outside linebacker Jose Ramirez.

Ramirez has been in and out of the Patriots organization. At one point, he was even on the 53-man roster for a week.

According to Mike Reiss, Ramirez is re-signing with the Patriots practice squad. And Reiss pointed out that the team keeps juggling its 17-man practice squad to account for injuries and depth.

Ramirez has helped in games on special teams before. And he had to be let go earlier because of the offensive line needs. And we know how bad those needs have gotten. With four guards out at one point, the team had to grab every offensive lineman it could find, and that cost players at other spots their place on the roster.

Ramirez has played in two games for the Patriots, with one special teams tackle.

And he brings some real value. First, outside linebacker depth is always useful, especially on a team where the edge rotation includes Elijah Ponder, Gabe Jacas, and Draymond Jones, and Landry still recovering.

Second, he knows the Patriots’ system and operation. That matters a lot when you bring a player back in the middle of the season.

And third, he has special teams experience. And that’s huge for a practice squad player. You have to be flexible. You have to play scout team for different positions. You have to be ready to be called up and contribute on special teams at a moment’s notice. Ramirez is exactly that.

And special teams matter to this coaching staff. Remember, the Patriots’ rookies have played the second-most special teams snaps in the entire league. And Vrabel has talked about players like Brenden Schooler, saying special teams means a lot to him, that he communicates, understands the scheme, and brings energy. Ramirez fits that profile.

Now, let’s step back and look at the bigger picture. Because these two moves aren’t just about a punter and a practice squad linebacker. They tell us something important about this roster.

This team is in an injury crisis. Around 20 players on injured reserve. Only five return slots left. Through six games, 14 different offensive line combinations. Carlton Davis III missing three straight games with a neck injury. Marcus Jones being pushed to outside cornerback, where he’s struggled.

And that’s exactly why the trade deadline on November 10th matters so much.

Think about it. If you only have five ways to bring players back from IR, the other way to get healthy bodies onto the field is the trade market.

And the Patriots have been linked to some big names. Quenton Nelson, the highest-graded guard in the NFL, who would fill Vera-Tucker’s exact spot at left guard. DeForest Buckner, the defensive tackle, with Albert Breer naming New England as a possible fit. Jonathan Taylor, the running back with seven rushing touchdowns. And Andrew Wylie, the two-time Super Bowl champion offensive lineman, who ESPN’s Bill Barnwell suggested for just a late-round pick swap.

None of those deals are done. No talks have been reported. But every one of them would add a healthy, proven player without using up an IR return slot.

So these small moves today could be setting the stage for bigger ones. Protecting the return slots. Juggling the practice squad. Making room. That’s what a front office does when it knows it has to get through a long season with a banged-up roster.
