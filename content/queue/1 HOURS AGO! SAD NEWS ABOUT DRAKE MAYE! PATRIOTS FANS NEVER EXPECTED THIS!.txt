Let’s start with Drake Maye. Because this is the story.

A reporter told Vrabel that since his radio appearance that morning, there had been multiple reports that Maye had possibly suffered a fractured ankle, and could be out for the rest of the season.

And Vrabel said he wasn’t going to make any comments about Maye’s status until Maye gets back to New England.

He explained that Maye stayed behind in Germany with a few members of the team’s staff, and they were on their way back. And once Maye is back, and the team’s people can visit with him again and talk with him again, Vrabel said they’ll have more to offer.

But then he added the line that every Patriots fan is going to remember. He said he wouldn’t imagine Maye being available when the team comes out of the bye.

So let’s be very clear about what we know, and what we don’t.

What we know: Maye left the game in Munich with an ankle injury. Adam Schefter reported that the Patriots feared a fracture, and that Maye stayed in Germany for more tests, including an evaluation of whether surgery is needed. And now Vrabel says he doesn’t expect Maye to be ready for the first game after the bye, against the Chargers on November 29.

What we don’t know: the exact diagnosis. Whether he needs surgery. And how long he’ll be out. The reports about the rest of the season are reports. The team hasn’t confirmed them. Vrabel made it clear he’ll say more once Maye is back home and the medical staff has seen him.

But Patriots Nation, let’s not sugarcoat it. Even in the best case, the Patriots are going to have to play without their franchise quarterback for a while.

And think about what this team loses. Last season, Maye led the Patriots all the way to the Super Bowl. This season, he won AFC Offensive Player of the Month for October. He had five straight games with at least three total touchdowns. He’s 24 years old. He’s the heart of this team.

So what happens at quarterback now?

With Maye out, Tommy DeVito is the most likely player to take over. DeVito came in against Detroit after Maye went down, and it was a struggle. Vrabel said earlier this week that DeVito’s performance wasn’t good enough. But he also said he trusts him, and expects him to improve over the bye week. And he said rookie Behren Morton has been progressing in the system.

And remember, Vrabel also said he doesn’t see a need to bring in outside help at quarterback right now, though the staff will talk it over and evaluate. That was before the reports about a possible season-ending injury. So if those reports turn out to be true, that conversation could change. Names like Jimmy Garoppolo, who knows Josh McDaniels from New England and Las Vegas, have come up. But there’s no deal in motion. Nothing has been reported beyond that.

Now let’s get to the trust question. Because this is a big one.

After the game, linebacker Christian Elliss made some comments about trust. About players maybe having trust issues, and having to put that to the side.

So a reporter asked Vrabel directly. Does he sense any lack of trust, either in the play calls, or in the players trusting each other on the field?

And Vrabel said no.

He explained that after losses, there’s frustration. He said everybody is searching for the right thing to say. And the more you search, the more it gets picked apart.

He said this team is used to being in a position to give itself a chance to win. And when you’re not in that position, it gets frustrating. It gets disappointing. There are a lot of emotions. And he said the players put a lot into this.

Then he explained what he expects from every player. Understand the concept of the call. Execute it to the best of your ability. Put your stamp on the team’s identity on that particular play. And if the other team makes a play, they make a play.

He said some of what happened in Munich was things the Patriots did to themselves. And sometimes, when the other team made a really nice play, a contested catch or a broken tackle, the Patriots let that play decide what happened on the next one.

But he also pointed out the positives. He said there were good opportunities to show the coordinated moments on defense. Third-down stops. Interceptions. Competing in the red zone. And he specifically mentioned Elliss getting his interception there.

And then he said maybe Christian could better clarify what his comments meant. But Vrabel said he knows the players want to be here, and they want to be part of the solution. They’re just frustrated.

And then he summed up the whole problem. He said too many times, it was one guy here, one guy there. One block. One catch. One throw. Or one mistake on defense. And that’s what happens against good teams.

Patriots Nation, I think that’s a fair answer. Elliss isn’t a player who has quit. He was out there making an interception. He’s a player who is frustrated because he cares.

Now, the next question was even tougher.

A reporter pointed out that the Patriots have now been blown out by 30 or more points three times this season. And according to that reporter, that’s the most since the 1990 Patriots team that went 1 and 15.

That’s a brutal stat. And the reporter asked Vrabel why this team isn’t in a position to win more.

And Vrabel gave a direct answer. He said the team turns the ball over. It gives up explosive plays. It didn’t run the ball efficiently enough in those games to stay balanced. It needs to create more plays on special teams, the kind of explosive plays that create field position and momentum. And he said the team has to coach better and play better.

No excuses. Turnovers. Big plays allowed. No balance. Special teams. And coaching. He included himself.

And that lines up with everything we’ve seen. The Jacksonville game, 35 to 6. The Green Bay game, 45 to 27, the most points the Patriots have ever allowed at Gillette Stadium, with four turnovers. And now Detroit, 35 to 10.

Then came the question about the locker room.

A reporter asked Vrabel what, in his opinion, would count as a locker room problem.

And Vrabel said he wouldn’t even want to know what a problem looks like, because he’s never had one. He said his players want to do well. They want to win. They want to find answers. They’re frustrated.

And then he said something really interesting. He said conflict is important for growth, in a positive way. Trying to find ways to be part of the solution, and certainly not part of the problem. And he said the team has those kinds of players.

And then he talked about the bye week. He said they’re not starting over. There’s no time to start over. But they definitely need some kind of reset with the bye. And he pointed out that at 5 and 5, a lot of teams go in different directions at this point in the season.

Patriots Nation, that’s the key. This is the fork in the road. Some 5 and 5 teams make a run. Some fall apart. Which one will this team be?

Now, here’s the part that surprised a lot of people. A reporter asked if there’s a disconnect between Vrabel’s messages, his non-negotiables, and what’s actually happening on the field.

And Vrabel said this. The Lions played harder than the Patriots did.

But he said that’s not the only reason Detroit won. He said the Lions certainly outplayed the Patriots. They outcoached the Patriots. And they played harder.

Think about that, Patriots Nation. A head coach admitting the other team outcoached his team, outplayed his team, and played harder. That’s about as honest as it gets.

And he said the coaches will have to keep finding the right messages, and the right actions and behaviors. Show the players the positive ones and the success they lead to. And show them the ones that don’t.

Then a reporter asked Vrabel to speak directly to the fans. What’s his leadership plan for the rest of the season?

And Vrabel said he believes in saying less and doing more. He said he isn’t going to sit there and try to sell a message.

He said that in the year and a half he’s been here, the fans have recognized what it looks like when the Patriots give themselves a chance to win. Put yourself in position. Don’t stall out on drives. Don’t make mistakes. Don’t give up big plays. Get stops on third down. All the things they know lead to winning, especially against good teams.

And then he made a really good point. He said the Patriots have played a lot of good teams. And he reminded everyone that this is the same football team that went into Buffalo and beat a Bills team that, at the time, only had one loss.

Patriots Nation, he’s right. In Week 4, everybody counted this team out. And it went into Buffalo and won 29 to 26. That team is still in that locker room. The question is whether it can find that level again, now without Drake Maye.

Now let’s talk about the field conditions. Because this became a story after the game.

Milton Williams said after the game that he believed the field conditions in Munich played a factor in Drake Maye’s injury. And there was a question about whether another player’s injury was connected to the field too.

So a reporter asked Vrabel. Did he feel the field conditions affected a lot of players?

And Vrabel said he doesn’t think Maye’s injury was caused by the turf. He said they’ve seen other players who have celebrated, or left their feet, and come down and had issues. And he said the field was the same for both teams. That’s just what the field was.

So no excuses from Vrabel. Even with his own defensive leader pointing at the field, Vrabel wasn’t going to blame it.

And then Vrabel got asked about effort. Since he said the Lions played harder, why does he think the effort was lacking?

And Vrabel said it comes back to mental strength. He said when the other team makes some plays, the Patriots have to be mentally strong enough to handle it. He said the Lions are talented, and they’re going to make plays. When they do, the Patriots’ response is critical. He said you can’t let one play affect the next one.

He also said there were a bunch of plays where his players were going full speed to the tackle, or finishing longer than the guy with the ball. But he said until the offense gets plays started and gets to the second level, it’s not going to matter. And he mentioned a play early in the game, when Hunter broke a tackle along the sideline, and players were transitioning and doing the right things.

And then he addressed the elephant in the room. Was it the momentum of losing the quarterback, losing Drake? Vrabel said he isn’t going to sit there and make excuses for that. He said he’s just going to find ways to make sure those things stay consistent, and that everybody believes in how important they are.

Patriots Nation, that’s leadership. He could have said, “We lost our quarterback on the first series, what do you expect?” And honestly, a lot of people would have accepted that. But he didn’t. He wants more.

Then a reporter asked if it’s been difficult for Vrabel to get consistent effort out of this team this season.

And Vrabel reframed it. He said the team hasn’t had consistent performances. And a lot of things lead to performance. Turnovers. Split-second decisions. Critical mistakes. Missed tackles. Drops. And effort. All of that leads to being inconsistent.

He said they need consistent actions. He said the preparation has been great, and he thought the team had a good week of practice before Munich. But he said the only thing that matters is how they perform and how they play on Sunday.

So they’ll keep preparing, and they’ll have to find ways to play better and play more consistently.

And finally, the toughest question of all. A reporter asked if this team is mentally fragile.

He’s right about that. After the 35 to 6 loss in Jacksonville, the Patriots won three straight games. After the loss to the Bears, they beat the Dolphins. This team has responded before.
