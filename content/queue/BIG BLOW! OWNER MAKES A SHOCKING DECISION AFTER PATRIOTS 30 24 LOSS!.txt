Here’s the deal.

The Patriots get running back Jonathan Taylor and guard Quenton Nelson.

The Colts get running back TreVeyon Henderson, a 2027 second-round pick, and a 2028 fifth-round pick.

Now, let’s be clear. This is a proposal. DraftKings put it out there as a “who says no” type of trade. Nobody has reported any talks between the teams.

But the fit is worth talking about. Because both of these players could realistically be moved before the deadline.

The Colts fired their general manager, Chris Ballard. They’re 2 and 4. ESPN’s Stephen Holder reported that multiple teams have trade interest in Taylor. And Jeremy Fowler reported that rival teams believe the Colts will be open for business before the trade deadline.

And remember, last November, the Colts traded away their 2027 first-round pick to the Jets for Sauce Gardner. So they don’t have a first next year. If their season keeps going sideways, picks are what they really need.

So if the Colts do sell, look at what’s on the table. Jonathan Taylor. Quenton Nelson. DeForest Buckner. Three of the biggest contracts on their roster.

And Taylor knows the noise is loud. On Thursday, he said he hasn’t given it any thought. He said it would be a discredit and a disservice to his teammates and the whole organization to think about what could, should, or may happen.

Meanwhile, the Patriots are 4 and 3, a game and a half behind Buffalo in the AFC East, with a quarterback still on his rookie deal.

So why would the Patriots want this? Because a move like this is about one thing. Winning right now.

Let’s start with why the Patriots would want both players.

Patriots running backs are averaging 3.9 yards a carry this season. That’s 17th in the league. And against the Bears, they ran just 13 times for 44 yards. Mike Vrabel himself admitted after the game that they didn’t run the ball well enough.

And on the offensive line, Drake Maye was sacked twice and hit six times in Chicago. And the Patriots have had four guards out at the same time.

So both needs are real. And this trade would hit both at once.

Let’s talk about Jonathan Taylor. He’s 27 years old. A three-time Pro Bowler. He has more than 8,000 career rushing yards and 76 rushing touchdowns.

And this isn’t the first time his name has been in trade talks. Back in August 2023, the Colts gave him permission to seek a trade. He ended up staying and signing a three-year, 42-million-dollar deal.

Last season, he ran for 1,585 yards. He led the NFL in carries with 323, in rushing touchdowns with 18, and in rushing first downs with 84.

This year, through six games, he has 122 carries for 492 yards and seven rushing touchdowns. That’s tied for the most in the NFL.

Now, let me be honest with you. He’s averaging four yards a carry this season. The Patriots’ backs are at 3.9. So he’s not exactly running away from everybody.

But he has seven rushing touchdowns. And the Patriots’ running backs have combined for just three.

That’s the difference. Taylor finishes drives. And remember what Vrabel said after the Bears loss? The difference in the game was the red zone. They didn’t convert their red zone chance when it mattered. A back like Taylor changes that.

And then there’s Quenton Nelson. If you’ve seen our video on him, you know the numbers. Through six games, PFF had him as the highest-graded guard in the NFL, at 89.2. And he hasn’t allowed a single sack this year.

And he plays left guard. Exactly the spot Alijah Vera-Tucker just left when he went on injured reserve.

And here’s a bonus. Nelson and Taylor have been working together in Indianapolis since 2020. So this isn’t just a trade for a running back and a guard. You’d be getting a guy who’s been opening holes for that exact running back for years. They already know each other’s timing. That’s chemistry you can’t build overnight.

So the question becomes, is what the Patriots would give up actually worth it?

Let’s start with TreVeyon Henderson.

The Patriots took him with the 38th pick in the 2025 draft. And as a rookie, he was solid. 911 yards and nine touchdowns, at 5.1 yards per carry.

This year, though, it’s been a different story. 52 carries for 203 yards and one touchdown. And if you take away his 39-yard touchdown against Pittsburgh, he’s averaging just 3.2 yards per carry.

And the big worry is his vision. Against the Bears, he played just 13 snaps. And the Boston Globe pointed out it was the second straight week he didn’t have a role in the fourth quarter of a close game.

Now, Vrabel explained after the Bears game that they had specific packages for Henderson, and that he’d help them. But the usage tells its own story.

Put him next to Taylor this season. Taylor has 492 yards to Henderson’s 203. Seven touchdowns to Henderson’s one.

But here’s the stat that hurts. Henderson is still on his rookie deal through 2028. His cap hit is just 2.5 million dollars this season, 3 million next year, and 3.5 million in 2028. That’s incredibly cheap for a young running back with real talent.

Then there are the picks. A 2027 second and a 2028 fifth.

And remember, the Patriots already sent their 2028 first-round pick to Philadelphia for A.J. Brown back in June. So this would be the second time in five months they’ve pushed future picks in for help right now. And that tells you how this front office sees the window it’s in.

And now, the money. This is the part where it becomes a decision for the very top of the organization.

Taylor signed a two-year, 44-million-dollar extension in August. His cap hit is 16.2 million dollars this season. Then 21 million in 2027. And 23 million in 2028.

Now, at the deadline, a new team only takes on what’s left of this year’s salary. ESPN’s Dan Graziano put Taylor’s at about 5.3 million dollars. SB Nation put Nelson’s at around 8 million.

And according to Spotrac, the Patriots have only about 4.5 million dollars in cap space.

So the Patriots would have to clear a lot of room this year. And then they’d have to decide if they really want a running back at 21 million dollars next year, instead of Henderson at 3 million.

Think about that. That’s not a coaching decision. That’s a decision about how this organization spends its money during the window when Drake Maye is still cheap. And that’s the kind of call that ownership has to sign off on.

There is one escape hatch. Pro Football Rumors reported that Taylor’s 15-million-dollar base salary for 2027 doesn’t become fully guaranteed until February. So in theory, you could get out of the deal after the season.

But then you’d have given up Henderson and two picks for just one playoff run.

So who says no first?

Honestly, if you ask me, I think the Colts probably say no first.

One report says the Colts are looking for a first-round pick and more for Taylor. And this week, SB Nation’s Colts writer valued Nelson alone at a 2027 third-round pick.

So let’s do the math. Taylor alone: a first-round pick and more. Nelson alone: a third. And this deal offers both of them for a second, a fifth, and Henderson. That doesn’t come close to what the Colts reportedly want.

Let’s put it in perspective with another trade. When the 49ers traded for Christian McCaffrey at the 2022 deadline, they gave Carolina a second, a third, and a fourth in 2023, plus a fifth in 2024. All for one running back.

And the Patriots are a good team right now. So that second-round pick would probably land late in the round. Basically, it’d be close to a third. And for a Colts team that already gave away next year’s first, that’s not the kind of pick that fixes their problems.

Now, to be fair, there’s a case for the Colts. Taylor was a second-round pick too, 41st overall in 2020. Henderson went 38th in 2025. If the Colts still believe in Henderson, they’d get a cheap young back through 2028, and they’d get out of Taylor’s 21- and 23-million-dollar cap hits over the next two seasons.

So would the Colts take it as is? I don’t see it. But never say never.

And the money makes the Patriots stop and think too. Because it’s not about whether the talent is real. It is. It’s about paying a running back 21 and 23 million dollars for his age 28 and 29 seasons, while Drake Maye is still on his rookie deal. Those are the years where you probably want that money going almost anywhere else. The offensive line. The secondary. Long-term protection for your quarterback.

So would I do this full deal? Probably not.

To be honest, I really want to believe in Henderson. I think there’s some kind of disconnect right now between him and the coaching staff. In college, he was a great pass blocker. That hasn’t shown up the same way in the NFL. And his vision, there’s got to be something there that the staff can figure out.

Is he going to be as good as Jonathan Taylor? No. But you’re paying him a tiny fraction of the money. And at the end of the day, it would probably take a lot more than this offer to actually get Taylor.

So I’m looking at Nelson and saying that’s the best bet. And remember, there are cheaper options too, like Andrew Wylie, the two-time Super Bowl champ from Washington, that Bill Barnwell suggested for just a late-round pick swap.

The Patriots have options. They just have to choose the right one.
