Patriots Nation, 135 million dollars.

That’s what the Patriots gave Christian Gonzalez back in September. A four-year, 135-million-dollar contract. The message was crystal clear. This is our guy. This is the cornerback we’re building this defense around.

And then the last two weeks happened.

Against Green Bay, according to NFL Next Gen Stats, Gonzalez gave up 10 catches for 187 yards and a touchdown in coverage. And against Detroit in Munich, Amon-Ra St. Brown beat him for a 56-yard touchdown in the first quarter.

And now, on top of losing Drake Maye, the NFL officially pulled the Patriots out of Sunday Night Football. That’s the sad reality of where this team is right now.

So everybody in New England is asking the same question. Is Christian Gonzalez the problem with this defense? Or is he the guy being set up to fail?

In the last two games, the Patriots gave up 80 points. 45 to Green Bay. 35 to Detroit.

And remember, this is a Mike Vrabel defense. For years, the one thing you could count on with a Vrabel team was that the defense would keep you in games. Even when the offense struggled, the defense gave you a chance.

Right now, that’s just not happening.

And early this season, it was. Before the Bears game, this defense hadn’t allowed a single run of 20 yards or more all year. It was one of the best run defenses in the league. So what changed?

But let’s get back to Gonzalez. Because I don’t think this is as simple as “he’s overpaid.”

First, look at what he’s being asked to do.

In this defense, Gonzalez is usually the guy matched up with the other team’s best receiver. A lot of man coverage. A lot of time on an island. And a pass rush that hasn’t been getting home consistently.

When Green Bay can hold the ball as long as it wants, and Detroit can run play action all day, even elite cornerbacks give up big plays. No cornerback in this league can cover forever.

And it’s worse right now, because the cornerback across from him is hurt too. Carlton Davis III was ruled out before Munich with a knee injury. That means offenses can focus even more of their attention on attacking Gonzalez, and they know the help behind him is thinner.

And let’s not forget why the Patriots paid him in the first place. When he’s right, Christian Gonzalez is one of the best young cornerbacks in football. Through his first six games this season, he had allowed just 196 yards total. Teams don’t hand out a contract like that by accident. And that talent didn’t disappear in two weeks.

Second, look at what happened after that touchdown in Munich.

Gonzalez didn’t fold. He came back and picked off Jared Goff in the first half. It was his first interception of the season.

And after the game, Mike Vrabel talked about a defensive back who slipped on a play, with nobody else there to help get the ball carrier on the ground. And Vrabel said he liked that the player responded. He challenged guys. And he went and got an interception.

And then Vrabel said the line that matters most. He said it isn’t about one guy. It never will be, and it never has been.

Now, sure, that’s a coach protecting his player. But it’s also true. When the guy next to you doesn’t make the tackle, your bad play turns into a touchdown. A slip on one play shouldn’t be six points. It becomes six points when there’s nobody else there.

According to one report, former Patriots cornerback Asante Samuel went public, saying Vrabel should reach out to him to help sort out Gonzalez’s struggles.

Now, whether that conversation ever happens, nobody knows. But when a guy who won Super Bowls in that Patriots secondary is saying it out loud, you know people are paying attention.

And honestly, it shows how much people care about Gonzalez. Nobody offers to help a player they’ve given up on.

So here’s my answer to the question from the start.

Christian Gonzalez is not the problem. He’s the symptom.

He’s the most visible player on a defense where too many guys are a step late. And when you’re the one with the biggest contract and the toughest assignment, you’re the one everybody sees on the replay.

The safety who’s late to the spot doesn’t show up on the highlight. The pass rusher who doesn’t get home doesn’t show up either. The linebacker who misses the tackle doesn’t get the headline. But the cornerback standing next to the receiver who just scored? That’s the guy everybody remembers.

And look, I get the frustration. When you pay a cornerback 135 million dollars and he gets beat for a long touchdown on an international stage, people are going to talk.

But here’s my problem with the bust take.

Two bad games don’t erase who a player is. Gonzalez earned that contract with years of being one of the best young cornerbacks in the league. He’s been the guy who shadows the other team’s top receiver week after week.

And in the same game everyone’s using against him, he got his first interception of the year. Busts don’t respond like that. Busts hide. Gonzalez went and made a play.

The real question isn’t whether Christian Gonzalez can play. It’s whether this defense around him can stop putting him in impossible situations.

And this is where the bigger picture comes in. Because Gonzalez is just one piece of what’s gone wrong.

Think about everything this team has dealt with.

Drake Maye, who had one of the most explosive deep passing games in the league, is now likely out for an extended period of time, possibly the rest of the season. Vrabel already said he doesn’t expect Maye to be available coming out of the bye.

The running game, which you’d normally lean on without your quarterback, has been beaten up too. Rhamondre Stevenson has been dealing with a knee injury. And TreVeyon Henderson just landed on injured reserve.

A.J. Brown left the Munich game with a hamstring issue. Romeo Doubs has been on IR. The offensive line has been missing multiple starters.

And on the other side of that one play in Munich, Aidan Hutchinson’s season is now officially over. Dan Campbell confirmed it’s a torn ACL, with surgery expected in about two weeks. Hutchinson got hurt celebrating the sack that knocked Maye out of the game.

Two stars. One snap. And two teams changed overnight.

That’s sad for everybody. Nobody wants to see either one of those players go down.

So here’s what I’d do on defense.

First, give Gonzalez help. More safety support over the top. More disguised coverages, so offenses can’t just line up their best receiver on him and attack him all day. When the quarterback doesn’t know where the help is coming from, he can’t pick on one guy.

Second, get the pass rush home. Every extra second a quarterback has turns into a bad day for the secondary. Milton Williams, Christian Barmore, Harold Landry, K’Lavon Chaisson. These guys have to win their matchups. Because the best pass coverage in the world is a quarterback on the ground.

And third, tackling. Vrabel said it himself after Munich. Line up, tackle them, hit them, knock the ball loose. It sounds basic. But that’s exactly what’s been missing. When Gonzalez slipped in Munich, there was nobody there to finish the play. That can’t happen.
