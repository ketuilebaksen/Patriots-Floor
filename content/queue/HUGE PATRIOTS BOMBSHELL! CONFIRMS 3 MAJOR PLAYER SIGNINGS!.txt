To understand how much more dangerous these Patriots have become, we have to go back to the biggest criticism of last year’s team.

They hadn’t beaten anybody.

Sure, New England reached the Super Bowl. But throughout that run, people kept questioning the quality of the opponents. And when Drake Maye struggled in the playoffs, those doubts only got louder.

And then the Patriots lost Super Bowl 60 to Seattle, 29 to 13. Maye was sacked six times in that game. And to a lot of people, it seemed to confirm their suspicions. These Patriots were frauds. A team that got lucky.

So how could New England silence those critics?

A remarkably underrated draft class was one part of it. Caleb Lomu. Gabe Jacas. Eli Raridon. All three have made real contributions as rookies.

But the Patriots had something much bigger planned. In June, they traded with Philadelphia and landed A.J. Brown, sending a 2028 first-round pick to the Eagles.

That was supposed to be the moment. The moment everyone realized the Patriots were going to be even more dangerous this year.

But it didn’t go the way anyone planned.

On paper, A.J. Brown was a massive upgrade. But on the field, he hasn’t been able to have the same impact yet. Two of Maye’s top four targets from his breakout season were gone. And A.J. Brown ended up on injured reserve after just four targets in the opener against Seattle.

He’s played in only three games this season, with nine catches for 101 yards. And he’s been dealing with knee and ankle issues ever since.

And that meant Drake Maye started the 2026 season right where he left off in the Super Bowl.

And it was ugly.

In the opening game, he threw one touchdown and three interceptions, including a gutting final interception in the end zone that sealed the loss. And then, in the next two games, he threw three more interceptions and no touchdowns.

One touchdown and six interceptions in three games. Through Week 3, his PFF passing grade was the lowest among 26 qualified quarterbacks. Dead last.

Even Mike Vrabel, the players’ coach, kept it real. When he was asked about his concern level with Drake, Vrabel said his concern level was very high, for the whole football team.

That was startling. A quarterback who looked like an MVP through 17 games last season suddenly looked like the rattled, turnover-prone version of himself from the playoffs.

And longtime Patriots reporter Tom Curran had an explanation. He compared Maye to Drew Bledsoe, saying both have gunslinger mentalities. Guys who believe they can fit any throw, make any play, extend any scramble. And sometimes, a gunslinger doesn’t like being reined in.

That partly explained the dip. But there was something else going on too. Maye wasn’t playing with the same confidence. The same comfort. All those deep shots and tight-window throws he made a living on in 2025 were nowhere to be seen.

And it all bottomed out in Jacksonville. A 35 to 6 humiliation. No signs of life.

The doubters were louder than ever. And then came Buffalo.

Week 4. Buffalo. Against a very familiar opponent, in one of the hardest places to play in the NFL. And that’s where Drake Maye finally got his swagger back.

Three touchdowns. Just one interception. And the dagger inside the final two minutes, handing the Bills their first loss of the season. Patriots 29, Bills 26.

And when Vrabel was asked what changed, it really came down to one word. Decisiveness.

Vrabel said Maye was decisive. Whether that meant scrambling and taking off when he saw a running lane, or using his legs to extend plays. Vrabel said Maye looked out in front of things. If it was there, he let it go. And if it wasn’t, he found somewhere else to go with the football, and put the ball in really good spots down the field.

That was exactly what was missing in the first three weeks. And it was the reason he seemed to be spiraling. His indecision got worse with every turnover. But once he started playing freely again, we saw the best of the North Carolina gunslinger.

And Josh McDaniels noticed the growth too. He said the game is never won or lost in the first or second quarter. And he said Maye now understands that every mistake shrinks the margin for error, and that it sharpens his focus. McDaniels praised how Maye handles himself on the sideline, and said it takes a mature young player to keep leading others after making an error. And Maye knows how to do that.

And here’s why that Buffalo win was so important. It wasn’t just a glimpse of the old Drake Maye. It was the turning point of the season. It flipped the AFC East race on its head.

Since then, the entire vibe of this offense has changed.

Against the Raiders, Maye exploded for 374 yards and four passing touchdowns, plus a rushing touchdown, with no turnovers, completing more than three quarters of his passes. A 45 to 7 blowout. PFF gave him a career-high grade, and a 99.9 grade on deep throws, a mark only Jordan Love and Patrick Mahomes had hit this season.

Then a 26 to 19 win over the Jets.

Then a loss in Chicago, 30 to 24. But even in that loss, Maye threw for 321 yards and two touchdowns, and the Patriots went toe-to-toe with one of the NFC’s favorites at Soldier Field, on a short week.

And then Miami. Down 10 points at halftime, in the rain. And the Patriots came back to win 24 to 19, with Maye going 16 of 20 for 199 yards, two passing touchdowns, a rushing touchdown, and zero interceptions.

He’s put up at least three total touchdowns in five straight games. The Patriots are 5 and 3. And they’re undefeated in the division, 3 and 0.

This team found a way. Even with its biggest offseason acquisition hampered by injury.

And that brings us to the three confirmed signings. Because they follow the exact same blueprint. Players who were overlooked, undervalued, or released, who keep showing up for this team.

Signing number one. Lan Larison.

The day before the Miami game, with A.J. Brown and TreVeyon Henderson both ruled out, the Patriots signed Larison from the practice squad to the 53-man roster.

And here’s what made it surprising. Larison still had two practice squad elevations left. The Patriots could have just called him up for one game. Instead, they gave him a full roster spot, without cutting anyone, because they had an open spot.

That tells you they believe in him. And it protects him from other teams who could have signed him away.

And his story is incredible. At UC Davis in 2024, he ran for 1,465 yards and 17 touchdowns on 284 carries. He was the Big Sky Offensive Player of the Year in 2023. Over 50 college games, he piled up 3,634 rushing yards, 41 rushing touchdowns, and 127 catches.

The Patriots signed him as an undrafted rookie in 2025. Then he hurt his foot in the preseason and spent his entire rookie year on injured reserve. No games. This August, he was cut at final cuts, and brought back to the practice squad the very next day.

A young player who did everything right, worked in the shadows, and now gets his chance. That’s the kind of story that inspires a locker room. It tells every practice squad player that opportunity is real in Foxborough.

And with Henderson out with an illness that might be more involved than first thought, Larison could have a real role going forward.

Signing number two. Mike Brown.

This week, the Patriots signed safety Mike Brown from the practice squad to the 53-man roster.

Brown had already been elevated earlier this season, so he’s been in and out of game-day roles. And now he gets a permanent spot. That’s a reward for staying ready.

And it matters. This secondary has been tested all season. Carlton Davis III missed four games with a neck injury. Christian Gonzalez missed time with a shoulder injury. Channing Canada, Karan Prunty, and Charles Woods have all missed time. And now Marcus Jones, who leads the team with 39 tackles and handles punt returns, is dealing with a foot injury.

Every healthy defensive back counts on this team. And Mike Brown just earned his place.

Signing number three. Kindle Vildor.

And this one might be the sneakiest good move of the season.

The Patriots released Vildor on November 3rd, when Marcus Bryant came off injured reserve. And Vildor had interest from other teams’ practice squads. He had choices.

But he chose to stay in New England. And the Patriots re-signed him to their practice squad.

Think about that. Other teams wanted him. And he picked the place where he already knows the playbook, the defense, and the coaches.

And remember what he did the last time this team needed him. He was signed to the 53-man roster on October 10th, and the next day against the Raiders, he played 44 defensive snaps and earned a game ball. He punched the ball out of Brock Bowers’s hands on a fourth down, and Craig Woodson recovered it. He also broke up a pass on third down.

Vrabel called him a veteran who’s played a lot of football, and said he handled it well when he was thrust into the lineup with Gonzalez and Davis both out. And defensive coordinator Zak Kuhr called him the ultimate pro, saying he didn’t bat an eye.

With Marcus Jones dealing with a foot injury, Vildor could be the corner to watch today against the Packers. The Patriots can elevate him from the practice squad for the game, just like they did before the Buffalo game.

And against Jordan Love, a quarterback with 2,246 passing yards and a Packers offense that Vrabel says has more 20-yard pass plays than anyone in the league, the Patriots need every reliable cornerback they can get.

So what do these three signings tell us?

They tell us this front office never stops working. Larison, Mike Brown, and Vildor aren’t headline grabbers. But they’re exactly the kind of moves that keep a team winning in November and December, when injuries start taking over the league.

And they fit the identity of this team. Undrafted players like Chism, Elijah Ponder, Cory Ker, and Charles Woods. Veterans who choose to be here. Young players who stay ready. Vrabel said it himself this season. It doesn’t matter what you did before you got here. It just matters what you do when you get here.

And this might be just the start. The trade deadline is Tuesday, November 10th at 4 p.m. Eastern. The Patriots have about 6.1 million dollars in cap space after restructuring Hunter Henry’s contract. They’re reportedly one of four teams in the wide receiver market, with Josh Downs, Maye’s college teammate, on the table. AtoZ Sports has reported interest in Derrick Henry, who played for Vrabel in Tennessee. And Albert Breer mentioned a possible reunion with K’Lavon Chaisson.

None of those are confirmed. But with a 5 and 3 record and a perfect division record, this team has every reason to keep pushing.

And here’s the scary part for the rest of the league. The Patriots have done all of this without a healthy A.J. Brown. Romeo Doubs is still on injured reserve. Mack Hollins and Greg Van Roten just returned to practice from injured reserve. Harold Landry is still working his way back.

So imagine this team when those players come back. A healthy Brown. Doubs and Hollins back in the lineup. Landry joining a pass rush that already has 22 sacks, seventh in the league.

That’s a team that could be even more dangerous than the one that reached the Super Bowl.
