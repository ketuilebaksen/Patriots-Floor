And it’s not just one play. Look at this number.

When you look at yards lost to drops this season, the Patriots sit at around 260 yards through the air. The next closest team, Tampa Bay, is somewhere around 180.

That’s about 80 more yards lost to drops than the next-worst team in the entire league.

And that number hurts way more than just the offense. Because every drop that kills a drive sends the defense right back onto the field. Tired legs. More snaps. More chances for the other team to score. It’s a chain reaction.

And honestly, I’m usually the guy who says, throw a better ball, don’t just blame the receivers. But 80 extra yards lost compared to the next-worst team? There’s no excuse for that.

Now think about the Packers game. Green Bay put up 45 points. The defense was on the field way too long. And every time the offense went three-and-out after a drop, or turned the ball over, that defense had to go right back out there.

That’s exactly why A.J. Brown matters so much. A receiver who catches the ball. Who fights for it. Who never stops running.

And yet, when Brown has played this season, he’s looked like a shell of himself.

Remember the opener against Seattle? He had three catches in about a quarter and a half before he got hurt. Then he went on injured reserve.

Since coming back? 20 targets. Nine catches. 101 yards. No touchdowns. Career lows across the board.

He’s been fighting an ankle injury. And according to Adam Schefter, compensating for that ankle is what caused his knee to flare up. So he missed the last two games. And most of us figured we wouldn’t see him again until after the bye.

And this is where I have to give you my honest opinion. And I want to be clear, this is my read, not a report.

I think the Patriots rushed him back too fast the first time. They were panicking about the lack of bodies at receiver. They brought him back before that ankle was fully right, and the knee paid the price.

That’s the risk when a team gets desperate. You make a decision for this week, and you end up paying for it for the next month.

Remember his first game back against the Jets? Three catches on 10 targets for 27 yards, with drops. He owned it afterward, saying no excuses, ankle or not, he should make those plays. But maybe his body just wasn’t ready.

So when the team got ready to leave for Germany, the big question was simple. Would Brown even get on the plane?

On Monday, Vrabel said the plan was for him to travel, but no final decision had been made. And not many people were holding their breath.

Then, right before the flight, reporters looked over at the practice field and saw something almost nobody expected this soon.

A.J. Brown was practicing.

Not standing on the sideline. Not riding the bike. Reporter Sophie Weller saw him put on the cleats, grab the jersey and the helmet, and go to work, just hours before the team left for Germany.

And Vrabel confirmed it. The plan is for Brown to travel.

Now, Vrabel made a point of saying that traveling doesn’t guarantee anyone plays. He said there will be a lot of guys who go to Germany who won’t be active.

But think about it with me. If the Patriots wanted to shut Brown down, they had the perfect excuse. Skip Munich. Take the bye week. That’s about three weeks of rest for that knee and that ankle.

Instead, he’s practicing. To me, that tells you they see a real chance he suits up on Sunday.

And with Ben Brown, the center, not making the trip, Drake Maye is going to need every reliable target he can get in that stadium.

But here’s the real decision. And it’s a tough one.

If Brown isn’t truly ready, you don’t play him. Not before the bye, and not after it. You need him in December and January. Not just for one Sunday in November.

The Patriots already made the mistake of bringing him back too early once. If they do it again and the knee gets worse, they could lose him for the most important stretch of the season. And that would be a disaster.

But if he is ready? Then you play him. Because this Lions defense is banged up in the secondary, with cornerback D.J. Reed not practicing and safeties Kerby Joseph and Brian Branch limited earlier in the week. There could be matchups for Brown to win.

That’s the balance Vrabel and the medical staff have to find.

But even if Brown gets healthy, this team still has a quiet problem. And it comes from an NFL rule.

The NFL only gives each team eight designations to bring players back from injured reserve during a season. Eight. That’s it.

And as Evan Lazar pointed out, the Patriots have already used five of them. A.J. Brown. Marcus Bryant. Mack Hollins. Greg Van Roten. And now Reggie Gilliam, who’s back practicing.

Now assume Romeo Doubs and Alijah Vera-Tucker are next. And they should be, because this offensive line has missed Vera-Tucker badly. That would be seven.

One left. For the rest of the season.

That’s why I’d be surprised to see Rhamondre Stevenson go on injured reserve, even though Vrabel didn’t rule it out. He said anything could happen, but he wasn’t going to speculate. And he did say that if you pay attention, the Patriots don’t have many spots left to bring players back.

And honestly, I’m not sure I would have spent a spot on Gilliam. He’s battled a knee issue all year, and his run blocking hasn’t been what we expected when he came over from Buffalo.

Because here’s the problem. If you run out of return spots, the next injured player can’t come back from IR. So the team either has to keep him on the 53-man roster while he’s hurt, which means a roster spot sitting unused, or lose him for the season.

That’s why every injury decision is so complicated right now. And it’s one more reason the front office’s choice to stand pat at the deadline puts so much pressure on the players coming back.

So where does all of this leave us before Munich?

Look at who missed practice. Ben Brown. Brenden Schooler. Carlton Davis. Rhamondre Stevenson. And TreVeyon Henderson.

And Walter Rouse is a question mark too, after entering the concussion protocol. If he can’t go, Greg Van Roten probably slides back in at left guard.

It’s not a perfect roster. You and I both know that. Injuries have chased this team all year long. And the front office chose to ride it out instead of adding help at the deadline.

That decision only looks right if the players coming back actually stay healthy.
