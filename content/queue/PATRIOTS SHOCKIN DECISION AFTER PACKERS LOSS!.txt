PATRIOTS FACE A SHOCKING DECISION AFTER PACKERS LOSS!

Patriots Nation, the Patriots lost to the Green Bay Packers. The record drops to 5 and 4. And now, with the trade deadline just one day away, this front office is facing one of the biggest decisions of the entire season.

Because this wasn’t supposed to go this way. Going into Sunday, it looked like the Patriots had caught a break. Two big Packers absences were confirmed. Micah Parsons, ruled out with a knee injury. And their starting center, out with an ankle injury. Any Patriots fan felt a wave of relief when that news came out.

But then you looked at what was missing on our side. And that relief started to fade.

A.J. Brown, out. TreVeyon Henderson, out while recovering from a frightening health situation. Hunter Henry, questionable. Morgan Moses and Greg Van Roten, questionable. Romeo Doubs, still on injured reserve.

The test in Foxborough was never just about taking advantage of who Green Bay didn’t have. It was about who New England could still put on the field. And how far this front office should go to fill the gaps.

So today on Patriots Floor, we’re breaking down what this loss means, the questions it raises, and the decision Eliot Wolf and Mike Vrabel have to make before 4 p.m. Eastern tomorrow.

Let’s start with the big picture. Because one loss doesn’t define a season. But this one comes at a very important moment.

The Patriots are now 5 and 4. Still undefeated in the AFC East, at 3 and 0. Still in the playoff conversation. But Buffalo is pulling ahead in the overall standings. And the margin for error is getting smaller.

And look at the schedule coming up. The Detroit Lions in Munich, Germany, on November 15th. Then the Chargers. Then the Buffalo Bills on December 6th. Then Minnesota, Kansas City, the Jets, Denver, and Miami.

Let’s talk about what the Packers absences were supposed to mean. Because it tells you a lot about what this team needs.

Without Micah Parsons, the plan for protecting Drake Maye was supposed to change. When Parsons plays, an offense has to account for him on every snap. Can a tackle handle him alone? How often does a tight end or running back stay in to help?

Without him, the Patriots had more freedom to send those players out into routes. And that mattered a lot this week. Because with A.J. Brown out and Hunter Henry questionable, Maye didn’t have every answer this roster was supposed to give him. One more target running a route can be the difference between a clean read and holding the ball too long.

But an injury report doesn’t hand you a win. Green Bay can still bring pressure. Missed blocking assignments still happen. Third-and-long still happens. And with Moses and Van Roten both questionable, the Patriots’ own offensive line needed attention too.

On the other side, the Packers’ center being out forced Green Bay to adjust on the interior. The center identifies fronts, makes the blocking calls, and organizes the response to pressure up the middle. That was an opportunity for the Patriots’ defense to disrupt Jordan Love’s timing.

And this defense had just shown it can do that. Against Miami, the Patriots recorded five sacks. Elijah Ponder, Gabe Jacas, Milton Williams, Christian Barmore. This front can get after the quarterback.

But there was always a serious counterpoint. Green Bay can punish a defense that gets too excited about rushing the passer. Give Love an easy read, and the absence of a center may not matter much. Vrabel said this week that nobody has more 20-yard pass plays than the Packers. He called Love wildly efficient.

So the loss tells us something important. Catching a break on the injury report isn’t enough. You still have to turn it into actual football. And with this many pieces missing, that gets really hard.

And that brings us to the receivers. Because this is where the missing pieces hurt the most.

A well-protected quarterback still needs reliable people to catch the ball. And right now, Drake Maye’s options are thin.

A.J. Brown has played in just three games this season, with nine catches for 101 yards, and he’s dealing with knee and ankle issues. Romeo Doubs, who led the team in receiving yards before he got hurt, is on injured reserve. Mack Hollins was still working his way back from injured reserve. And Hunter Henry, who leads the team with 29 catches, was dealing with a foot injury after leaving the Miami game early.

That leaves DeMario Douglas, Kyle Williams, Efton Chism III, Eli Raridon, and a lot of hope.

And this week, an old roster decision came back into focus. Kayshon Boutte.

Boutte is with the Houston Texans now. But this week, he showed his support for Drake May after Maye was named AFC Offensive Player of the Month. He also shared a special Patriots jersey he kept, and said he was disappointed he never got to wear it on the field.

I get why that hits home in Foxborough.

The Patriots traded Boutte to Houston before the season. And in Houston, his role got smaller. A player who might have kept building chemistry with Maye ended up fighting for fewer chances somewhere else.

Now, you have to judge the Patriots’ decision in its original context. Back then, they had more healthy receivers. They had just traded for A.J. Brown. Keeping every receiver doesn’t guarantee anyone targets, and a crowded room can block other players from developing.

But depth gets tested when injuries arrive. And they arrived.

On third down, with Brown out, one more route from somebody Maye trusts could change how a defense covers. We don’t know whether Boutte would have made the catch. But his exit removed a familiar option.

And let’s be clear about one thing. Boutte’s social media posts show affection for New England and his former quarterback. They don’t show any conversation with Eliot Wolf, or any decision about his next contract.

A possible reunion belongs to next offseason, when Boutte could reach free agency. And DeMario Douglas and Mack Hollins have contract decisions coming too. If there’s a roster spot and interest on both sides, the front office can reassess the fit then.

But right now, the more urgent question is what this team does tomorrow.

And speaking of Drake Maye. Let’s take a second to appreciate what he’s done. He was just named AFC Offensive Player of the Month. He has nearly 2,000 passing yards through eight games coming into the Packers game. He put up at least three total touchdowns in five straight games. After throwing six interceptions in his first three games, he became one of the best quarterbacks in the league.

That’s exactly why this decision matters so much. When you have a quarterback playing at this level, you don’t waste the season because you ran out of healthy weapons.

Now, there’s one absence that calls for a very different kind of pause before we talk about adding anyone.

TreVeyon Henderson said he spent several days in the hospital after experiencing neurological symptoms, and the cause is still unknown. He said the test results were reassuring, but the symptoms affected his strength, his coordination, and some of his physical functions. He missed the Packers game.

I know this channel lives and breathes football. But when a 24-year-old player says his health and his long-term well-being come first, our first reaction has to be about the person. I hope he gets answers. And I hope he makes a full recovery.

Mike Vrabel said the team is focused on Henderson and his wife, Kenzie. And when he was asked about injured reserve, Vrabel didn’t give a timeline. That’s not an invitation to guess at a diagnosis. That’s simply where the information stops. And we should respect that.

On the field, the effect is clear. Rhamondre Stevenson remains the lead back. Cory Ker takes on more work behind him. And Lan Larison, who was signed to the 53-man roster before the Miami game, rounds out the group. Vrabel has praised Ker’s energy and his kick return work.

But replacing a back means more than just handing someone else the ball. The backup has to protect the quarterback. Recognize the front. Catch passes when needed. Handle special teams. One missed assignment can hurt a lot more than one short run helps.

And here’s the tougher question. It’s not how many running backs are on the roster. It’s how much of the offensive plan is still available with the guys who can play.

Henderson showed real explosive ability as a rookie, topping 1,100 total yards with 10 touchdowns. Ker can bring energy and earn tough yards. But asking him to duplicate everything Henderson offered would be unfair. And if the offense becomes predictable whenever Stevenson rests, defenses have fewer threats to respect, and Maye has to carry even more of the load through the air.

And that brings us to the decision. The one that could shape the rest of the season.

Derrick Henry.

AtoZ Sports reported that the Patriots and Giants are both interested in a possible trade with Baltimore for Henry. And the connection is clear. Mike Vrabel coached Henry for six seasons in Tennessee. The Ravens are struggling at 4 and 5. And the deadline is tomorrow.

I won’t hide my fan reaction. Picture Derrick Henry in the same backfield as Drake Maye. That grabs you. Henry makes a defense think about contact and angles, and four quarters of punishment.

He has 689 rushing yards and eight touchdowns through nine games. He just passed LaDainian Tomlinson for seventh on the all-time rushing list. And his remaining salary for this season is only about 600,000 dollars.

But Eliot Wolf’s question isn’t whether the name excites fans. It’s what job Henry would do here, and at what cost.

His fit deserves a real look. Henry could give the Patriots physical runs in short yardage. He could make linebackers respect the box. And that would open up reads for Maye when the defense creeps closer to the line. And Vrabel knows exactly how to use him.

Still, a running back can’t create space by himself if the offensive line gives him none. And Stevenson is already producing as the starter. If Henry arrived, they’d split touches and roles. Ker would lose chances he just earned. And when Henderson is healthy, the room would get even more crowded.

A famous addition only works if his specific role helps more than the disruption hurts.

And Baltimore has its own decision to make. A 4 and 5 record puts the Ravens under pressure. But it doesn’t force them to sell a player who can still help them compete. And after the loss to Jacksonville, Henry said he hasn’t asked to be traded, and that he wants to help turn things around in Baltimore.

AtoZ reported New England’s interest. But interest isn’t an offer. It isn’t advanced talks. It isn’t an accepted price. AtoZ’s own estimate puts Henry somewhere between a late second-round pick and a third-rounder, with a third being their preference. That’s a long way from assuming he’ll go for a fourth or fifth.

And the contract is attractive in the short term, with very little money left this season. But a light cap hit today doesn’t erase the cost of losing a draft pick.

Now, fans calling for aggression have a real case. Maye is playing at an MVP level. The deadline is tomorrow. And Henry could help with the tough runs a team needs in January. And honestly, after a loss like this, nobody in Foxborough should celebrate doing nothing just to protect picks.

But here’s where I get careful. My hesitation starts when Henderson’s absence becomes an automatic reason to pay a high price. We have no idea how long he’ll be out. The offense already has Stevenson, and the staff needs to keep evaluating Ker and Larison. And the offensive line is competing for those same resources.

If Henry’s price drops, and the staff sees a separate job for him, I want the front office to explore it. If it costs a premium pick just to duplicate what Stevenson already does, excitement isn’t enough.

Smash that like button if you’d make a smart move, not a desperate one.

And there are other options too. The Patriots are reportedly one of four teams in the wide receiver market, with Josh Downs, Drake Maye’s college teammate, available from Indianapolis. And with this receiver room as thin as it is, a receiver might matter even more than a running back right now.

Albert Breer also floated a possible reunion with K’Lavon Chaisson, the pass rusher who had seven and a half sacks for New England last season, plus three more in the playoffs.

And the offensive line could still use help, with names like Quenton Nelson and Andrew Wylie linked to New England.

So the shocking decision isn’t just yes or no on Derrick Henry. It’s where the Patriots put their limited resources. A running back? A receiver? A pass rusher? An offensive lineman? Or nothing at all?

With about 6.1 million dollars in cap space, and picks in rounds one through four and six in 2027, plus a fifth from Chicago, the Patriots have the tools. The question is whether they use them.

So let’s put it all together.

The loss: the Patriots fall to the Packers and drop to 5 and 4, despite Green Bay missing Micah Parsons and its starting center.

The missing pieces: A.J. Brown out, Romeo Doubs on injured reserve, Hunter Henry and Morgan Moses questionable, and a receiver room stretched thin.

The old decision: Kayshon Boutte, traded to Houston before the season, sharing his support for Drake Maye and the Patriots jersey he never got to wear.

The human story: TreVeyon Henderson, recovering from unexplained neurological symptoms. His health comes first, and his recovery will move on its own timeline.

And the decision: Derrick Henry, Josh Downs, K’Lavon Chaisson, or standing pat, with the deadline tomorrow at 4 p.m. Eastern.

The Packers game is over. The film will show us where things went wrong. But the trade deadline is where the front office shows us what it believes about this season.
