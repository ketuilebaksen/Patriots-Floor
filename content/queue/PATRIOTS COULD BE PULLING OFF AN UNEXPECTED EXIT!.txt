Let’s start with the Hunter Henry news. Because this move could be a lot more important than it looks.

The Patriots converted half of Henry’s base salary into a signing bonus. That created about 3.3 million dollars in additional cap space. And it brings New England’s total available cap space to around 5.2 million dollars.

And look, the timing is what makes this so interesting. We’re getting very close to the November 10th trade deadline.

Now, let me be clear about what this is. Henry didn’t take a pay cut. He’s still getting his money. What happened is a restructure of his contract. It gives New England more financial flexibility right now, while spreading part of that cost over the coming years.

And honestly, it’s hard to look at this move and not think about the Patriots bringing someone in.

Think about the trade targets we’ve been talking about. Quenton Nelson, the highest-graded guard in the NFL. Before this move, the Patriots had about 4.5 million dollars in cap space, and Nelson’s remaining salary at the deadline would be around 8 million. That was the big problem. Now that gap just got smaller.

Andrew Wylie, the two-time Super Bowl champion lineman from Washington, whose salary already fit. Now it fits even more comfortably.

And there’s DeForest Buckner, Josh Downs, Riq Woolen. All names that have been linked to New England in some way.

Now, I’m not saying a deal is already in the works. I’m not saying the Patriots have a specific player in mind. But when a front office creates cap space at this point in the season, it’s only natural for fans to start wondering what reinforcements might be coming.

And remember something else. Having more cap space doesn’t automatically mean the Patriots will spend it. It just gives the front office more options. They could use it to chase a player at a position that needs help. Make room for a trade. Or simply give themselves more financial breathing room for the rest of the season.

What matters is what they decide to do with that opportunity.

And here’s how I feel about it. If this team has a chance to improve the roster before the deadline, I’d much rather see them take advantage of it, than sit back and watch other teams get stronger.

But while that’s exciting, another situation has started raising real concerns among Patriots fans.

According to Albert Breer, the Patriots are reportedly open to trading Christian Barmore.

And this is where I really want to know what the front office is thinking. Because we’re not talking about just any player here.

Barmore has already shown what he’s capable of. Back in 2023, he recorded 8.5 sacks, 16 quarterback hits, and 13 tackles for loss. That’s elite production from an interior defensive lineman. And alongside Milton Williams, he helped form a really strong defensive line in 2025, on the way to the Super Bowl.

Now, it’s true that his playing time has dropped this season. And he hasn’t been able to produce at that same level. But is that really enough to start thinking about a trade?

And think back to the start of this season. Before the Raiders game, Barmore was questionable, but he practiced fully on the Friday after two limited days. And at that point, we talked about how much his return could matter. With four cornerbacks out, interior pressure from Barmore could cut down the time the opposing quarterback had to find open receivers. He was seen as a key piece.

So what changed?

I understand the coaching staff might have a different vision for his role now. And I understand that a strong offer could change the front office’s plans. But as a fan, I wouldn’t want to see New England simply move on from a player with that kind of potential without a clear plan to replace him.

Think about it for a second. The Patriots have a defensive front that’s been making life difficult for quarterbacks. Elijah Ponder, with five and a half sacks. Rookie Gabe Jacas, with five pressures against the Bears. Draymond Jones, the veteran. Milton Williams inside. If Barmore leaves, the team gives up a player who’s already proven he can make an impact as a pass rusher. That’s not something you can just ignore.

Even if his role has changed this season, his past production still matters when you evaluate what he could bring to this defense.

The big question is whether the Patriots believe they get more value by keeping him, or by moving him before the deadline. And that’s a decision the front office has to get right.

And there’s another part of this situation that concerns me even more. The secondary.

The Patriots have Christian Gonzalez, one of the defense’s biggest talents. And Carlton Davis III, who was brought in to form a really strong cornerback duo with him. When both of those guys are on the field, this secondary can give opposing offenses a lot of problems.

The issue is availability. Davis has now missed four games with a neck injury. And Gonzalez has also dealt with physical setbacks, missing games earlier this season with a shoulder injury.

And when the starters aren’t available, the backups have to step up. But Channing Canada, Karan Prunty, and Charles Woods have also missed time with injuries over the past few weeks.

That makes things even harder. Because there’s no point in having a great starting cornerback duo if the defense can’t hold that level when one of them goes down.

We saw exactly what happens in Chicago. With Davis out, Marcus Jones was pushed to the outside, where he’s struggled. According to PFF, he’s already played 93 snaps outside this season, compared to 60 in all of last season. His coverage grades against the Raiders and Bears were 48.8 and 37.4. And he was credited with six missed tackles against the Bears. Caleb Williams found the open spaces and took advantage.

A team can look completely different when its best players are healthy, compared to when the backups have to take on bigger roles. Opposing offenses are always looking for weak spots. And losing depth at cornerback makes an already tough situation even tougher.

That’s why I don’t think this is a position New England can afford to overlook.

Now, here’s the good news. Davis is expected to return for the Week 8 matchup against Miami. And that’s huge. It means Marcus Jones can move back into the slot, where he’s at his best.

But I still think the Patriots need to take a serious look at cornerback depth. It’s a long season. Injuries happen. And nobody wants to reach the biggest games of the year relying on players who haven’t shown consistency yet.

And that’s exactly where a familiar name comes into the picture. Alex Austin.

The cornerback was released by the Dallas Cowboys without appearing in a single game this season. And he showed interest in coming back to New England, after responding to a fan who asked him to return.

Austin already knows the Patriots’ system. And he had some productive moments during his three seasons with the team.

Of course, he wouldn’t be a signing that fixes every problem in the secondary. Let’s be realistic. But when you think about roster depth, he could be a really interesting option.

And honestly, if there’s a chance to bring back a player who already knows the system and could help in a rotation, I think it’s at least worth considering.

One thing I really like about this possibility is that Austin wouldn’t be walking into a completely unfamiliar situation. He’s already spent time in this organization. He knows the people. He knows the defense. That doesn’t guarantee he’ll come in and make an immediate difference. But it makes the transition a lot easier compared to bringing in someone who’s never been part of this team.

And remember the IR situation. The Patriots only have five IR return slots left, with around 20 players on injured reserve. That’s why they released punter Bryce Baringer. When you can’t count on getting injured players back, signing healthy depth from the outside becomes even more valuable.

And that’s the kind of move I think the Patriots should at least explore. Not every addition has to be a big trade or a big-name signing. Sometimes adding one more option to the roster makes a difference when injuries start hitting the depth chart. If Austin can provide useful depth, it could be a smart move that doesn’t require a major commitment.
