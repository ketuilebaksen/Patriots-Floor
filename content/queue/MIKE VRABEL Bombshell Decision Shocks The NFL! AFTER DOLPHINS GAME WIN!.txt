Now, let’s talk about K’Lavon Chaisson. Because his story is one of the best in the league. And to understand why he matters, you have to start at the beginning.

In 2020, the Jacksonville Jaguars took him 20th overall in the first round, out of LSU. And that pick actually came from the Rams, as part of the Jalen Ramsey trade. So the Jaguars had a lot riding on it.

And it went nowhere.

In his first four regular seasons in Jacksonville, Chaisson had just five sacks total. One in 2020. One in 2021. One in 2022. Two in 2023.

In 2022, he hurt his knee and went on injured reserve in October.

And then, in May of 2023, the Jaguars declined his fifth-year option. That option would have paid him a fully guaranteed 12.141 million dollars for 2024.

Think about that. The team that drafted him in the first round looked at 12 million dollars for one more year, and said, no thanks.

And it got worse before it got better. Carolina signed him in March of 2024, and then cut him on September 3rd. Seven days later, the Raiders signed him to their practice squad.

A first-round pick, cut in training camp, sitting on a practice squad. That was rock bottom.

But that’s when things started to turn. The Raiders made him active on October 5th. And he finished that season with five sacks. More than his last three seasons in Jacksonville combined.

And then, the Patriots called.

Smash that like button if you love a good comeback story.

In March of 2025, New England signed Chaisson to a one-year deal. A 3-million-dollar base, worth up to 5 million with incentives.

The plan was to use him as a situational pass rusher. A rotational piece. But he ended up playing 62.7 percent of the team’s defensive snaps. And Mike Vrabel said he showed up ready to learn, work, and earn a role.

And he did exactly that.

Then came Week 7 of last season against the Titans. Two sacks and a fumble return for a touchdown. He was named AFC Defensive Player of the Week. And after the game, Vrabel said he was hopeful that Chaisson’s best football was still in front of him.

He was right.

Chaisson finished the 2025 regular season with seven and a half sacks. A career high.

Look at the progression. Two sacks in 2023. Five in 2024. Seven and a half in 2025. Every single year, he got better.

And then came the playoffs. Chaisson added three more sacks in four playoff games. Two against the Chargers. One against the Texans.

And it wasn’t a fluke. By Week 7 of last season, he was already second on the team in sacks, behind only Harold Landry. In the playoff game against Houston, he played a season-high 53 snaps, with a sack and four quarterback hits.

And it wasn’t just the sacks. ESPN ranked him 15th among all edge rushers in pass rush win rate that October.

The Boston Globe wrote that he had outplayed his contract and would likely cash in. And the only question was at what cost.

And the answer came in free agency. Washington signed him to a one-year deal worth 11 million dollars, and up to 12 million.

Do the math. Up to 5 million from the Patriots. Then 11 million from Washington. More than double.

The Patriots didn’t lose a bad player. They lost a bargain that got too expensive.

So the big question now is, is he still that guy?

This season with Washington, Chaisson has three and a half sacks in seven games. That’s about one sack every two games.

And last year in New England, it was seven and a half sacks in 16 regular-season games. Also about half a sack per game.

Same rate. Different team. He’s still producing.

He also has 10 quarterback hits this season. And his three and a half sacks tie him with Patriots defensive tackle Christian Barmore.

And then came his game against the Eagles. A week after he had zero quarterback hits against the 49ers, he bounced back in a big way. One sack, three quarterback hits, and seven pressures. Commanders beat writers called it their best pass rush performance of the season, with a 14.7 percent pressure rate.

That’s three straight seasons as at least a quality rotational pass rusher, after four years of one-sack seasons.

So what changed? The usage. A coaching staff found the right role for him, and he kept it. In New England, Vrabel’s staff built a plan around his speed and effort. And Chaisson ran with it.

He’s playing well. And that’s the problem. Because it makes him even harder to get.

Now, here’s the question everybody asks. Do the Patriots even need another edge rusher?

And the honest answer is, the pass rush is not the problem. The Patriots have 22 sacks this season. That’s seventh in the league.

Elijah Ponder, the undrafted second-year player, has been on fire, with at least half a sack in five straight games heading into Miami. Rookie Gabe Jacas had five pressures in Chicago. Draymond Jones brings the veteran savvy.

But Barmore missed two games, and he has three and a half sacks. And his name has been in trade rumors too, with Breer reporting the Patriots are open to trading him.

So Chaisson wouldn’t be fixing a broken defense. He’d be a rental, to make a good defense even deeper. And that matches exactly what Breer said. Buyers. But not a team going all-in on every move.

And think about how a rotation works. Fresh legs late in games. Different looks for different quarterbacks. Depth for the next injury. A player who already knows Vrabel’s system and can step right in. That’s real value.

And here’s the catch. The Patriots have a history at the deadline. And last year, they didn’t buy. They sold.

Keion White went to the San Francisco 49ers. Kyle Dugger went to Pittsburgh. Both for 2026 sixth-round picks. And Breer said at the time that the Patriots didn’t want to spend high picks on short-term fixes.

But a late-round pick for a rental isn’t a high pick. So this kind of move actually fits the way this front office thinks.

Now, why would Washington even consider selling him?

Look at what they did last March. They signed Odafe Oweh to a four-year, 100-million-dollar deal. And they signed Chaisson for just one year. So Chaisson is the cheaper half of their pass rush.

And that pass rush has 16 sacks in seven games. That’s 21st in the league. So Washington signed two edge rushers and still can’t consistently get home. Selling the cheaper one isn’t a crazy thought.

Breer also pointed to other Commanders players on expiring deals. Daron Payne, at 27.95 million dollars. Dorance Armstrong, at 11.58 million. And Chaisson, the cheapest of the three, at 11.47 million.

For the money, Chaisson is probably the easiest one to move.

So what would it actually cost the Patriots?

Let’s start with the cap. Chaisson’s cap hit is 11.47 million dollars this year. And according to Spotrac, the Patriots now have about 6.1 million dollars in cap room. That’s up from the Hunter Henry restructure, which created about 3.3 million in space.

But a trade this late in the season doesn’t cost the full 11.47 million. A team only takes on what’s left of the season. Roughly half of it.

So the money is close. And that’s why the price in draft picks matters more.

And look at what rental pass rushers have cost recently. In 2024, Detroit paid a fifth and a sixth for Za’Darius Smith. Last year, Chicago sent a sixth for Joe Tryon-Shoyinka. And Baltimore got Draymond Jones for a conditional fifth.

A late-round pick. That’s the going price for a rental.

And the Patriots have those picks. They own their 2027 picks in rounds one through four, and in round six, plus a fifth from Chicago.

So the price isn’t the problem. The question is whether Washington wants to sell.

The Commanders are 2, 4, and 1. But they just beat the Eagles 27 to 10, and Chaisson had a sack in that game. And the NFC East is wide open. The Giants lead the division, and Washington is two games back.

Breer said Chaisson’s availability could depend on where Washington sits in the NFC East. And Washington has 25.5 million dollars in cap space, so nothing forces them to sell. They sell for picks, or they don’t.

So here’s where I land. There are three things to watch.

Number two, the Patriots. This Sunday, the Green Bay Packers come to Foxborough at 4:25. A win keeps New England firmly in buyer mode. And the momentum from Miami could carry right into that game.

And number three, the deadline itself. Tuesday, November 10th, at 4 p.m. Eastern. Whatever happens, it happens by then.

And here’s the best part. If it doesn’t happen, the Patriots lose nothing. They keep their picks. And Chaisson becomes a free agent again in March, since his deal is only for one year.
