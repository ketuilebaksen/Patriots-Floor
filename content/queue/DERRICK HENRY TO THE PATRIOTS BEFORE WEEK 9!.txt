To understand why this possibility has gotten so much attention, we have to start with TreVeyon Henderson. And unfortunately, the update he shared revealed something much more serious than a normal football injury.

Henderson explained that he went through an unexpected neurological condition, with symptoms that required him to be hospitalized for several days. He went through a series of tests, and the results came back positive and reassuring. But doctors still haven’t figured out what caused the symptoms.

He also revealed that the condition affected his strength, his coordination, and some of his physical functions. And now he’s working with specialists and physical therapists, focused on his recovery and, most importantly, his long-term health.

Henderson also asked for privacy and support during this time.

And honestly, before we even talk about what this means for the team, I want to say this clearly. I truly hope he makes a full recovery. We’re talking about a young player with a ton of talent, and the potential to build a great career in this league. No roster need should ever come before his health.

The most important thing right now is that Henderson gets all the support he needs to get better. And this fan base should give him exactly that.

But this situation does raise a football question. What happens to the running game while he’s out?

Henderson has been out since October 29th. He missed the Miami game, and he was ruled out for the Packers game too.

And even though his numbers this season have been modest, we can’t forget what he showed us in 2025. As a rookie, he finished among the league’s top first-year players, with 1,132 yards from scrimmage and 10 touchdowns in 17 games.

So we’re not talking about just another guy on the roster. Henderson represents a big part of New England’s offensive future. His speed, his explosiveness, and his ability to make plays in space are exactly the kind of traits that can change this offense.

And without him, even more responsibility falls on Rhamondre Stevenson.

And this is where we have to look at an issue that’s been showing up all season. The Patriots need more consistency in the running game.

Stevenson has 367 rushing yards and two touchdowns through eight games, averaging 3.8 yards per carry.

Now, that doesn’t mean he can’t handle the job. Stevenson has been a great teammate. Mike Vrabel has praised his pass protection and the way he plays without the football. And he just had one of his better games in Miami, with 82 yards on 19 carries in the comeback win.

But the numbers show the running game still isn’t making the kind of impact New England wants. Take out Drake Maye’s scrambles in the Chicago game, and the Patriots ran for just 44 yards on 13 carries. And across the whole backfield, the rushing touchdowns have been hard to come by.

And when an offense can’t establish a consistent threat on the ground, life gets a lot harder for the quarterback. Defenses can focus more attention on Drake Maye. They can bring pressure more aggressively. And they can make the passing game much tougher.

That’s exactly why the possibility of Derrick Henry deserves our attention.

According to a report from AtoZ Sports, the Patriots are among the teams that could make a deal with the Baltimore Ravens for Derrick Henry before the trade deadline. The New York Giants were also mentioned as a potential landing spot. But AtoZ said the Patriots have the stronger case.

And when you look at this possibility, there’s one detail that makes it even more interesting.

Mike Vrabel knows Derrick Henry better than almost anyone.

The two spent six seasons together with the Tennessee Titans. Vrabel knows how Henry likes to run. He understands his strengths. And he’s already built an offense around Henry’s ability to dominate on the ground. That’s where Henry became the King. That’s where he won a rushing title.

And New England already has other players with Tennessee ties. A.J. Brown. Kevin Byard. Harold Landry. All of them played with Henry in Tennessee. That familiarity helps explain why a reunion between Vrabel and Henry is getting so much buzz.

Imagine walking into a new locker room in the middle of the season, and already knowing the head coach, a star receiver, a veteran safety, and a pass rusher from your old team. The transition would be about as smooth as a midseason trade can get.

But I don’t want us to look at this as just a reunion between two guys who worked together before. The real argument is what Henry could bring on the field.

He’s still putting up impressive numbers. Henry has 689 rushing yards and eight touchdowns through nine games. At 32 years old, he still has the power, the speed, and the ability to turn an ordinary run into an explosive play.

And here’s the stat that matters most. Eight rushing touchdowns. That’s more than the Patriots’ running backs have combined. Stevenson has two. And remember what Mike Vrabel said after the Bears loss. The difference in that game was the red zone. They didn’t convert their chance when it mattered.

Derrick Henry is a red zone weapon. Near the goal line, there are very few players in NFL history who are harder to stop.

And look at his career. On Thursday night against Jacksonville, Henry passed LaDainian Tomlinson for seventh place on the NFL’s all-time rushing list. He has 13,650 career rushing yards. In 2024, he ran for almost 2,000 yards. Last year, over 1,500.

Now imagine that kind of player in the Patriots’ backfield. Henry pounding the ball between the tackles. Opposing defenses would have to respect the running game a lot more. And that creates better opportunities for Drake Maye.

Think about the play-action. Defenses would have to bring an extra defender into the box to stop Henry. And the moment they do, Maye fakes the handoff and throws over the top. Remember, against the Raiders, Maye posted a 99.9 PFF grade on deep throws. Give him a running game that forces defenses to commit, and those deep shots open up even more.

It would also make it harder for defenses to keep blitzing Maye. And with an offensive line that’s been banged up all season, with Mike Onwenu out for the year and Alijah Vera-Tucker on injured reserve, anything that slows down the pass rush is a big deal.

And here’s another important point. Henry wouldn’t have to carry the whole offense by himself. His presence could change how the Patriots spread out the workload. It would give this team more balance, and make it less dependent on Maye having to put together huge performances just to move the ball.

And Maye has been carrying a lot. He has nearly 2,000 passing yards through eight games. He’s put up at least three total touchdowns in five straight games. And he’s done it without a healthy A.J. Brown, without Romeo Doubs, and with Hunter Henry dealing with a foot injury.

Of course, Henry wouldn’t fix every problem on offense. But he’d give New England another weapon that very few defenses can afford to ignore.

And think about the schedule ahead. After the deadline, the Patriots travel to Munich, Germany, to face the Detroit Lions on November 15th. Then the Chargers. Then the Buffalo Bills on December 6th. Then Minnesota, Kansas City, the Jets, Denver, and Miami.

That’s a tough stretch. And in December and January, when the weather turns cold in Foxborough, teams that can run the ball win games. A back like Henry, built for physical, cold-weather football, could be exactly what this team needs for a playoff push.

Now, there’s one part of this potential deal that could decide whether it’s worth making. The price.

The report estimates Henry could cost a late second-round pick or a third-round pick, with analysts leaning toward a third-rounder.

And the money is very manageable. His remaining base salary for 2026 is about 600,000 dollars, because his bonuses have already been paid. And next year’s salary has no guaranteed money.

That’s huge for the Patriots. Most trade targets have been tough to fit under the cap. Henry fits easily.

At first glance, giving up a third-round pick for a running back with this kind of ability sounds like a great opportunity.

But we also have to think about where New England is right now.

The Patriots are still building this roster around a young quarterback. Draft picks are essential for bringing in young talent, adding depth, and making sure this team stays competitive for years. And remember, the Patriots already sent their 2028 first-round pick to Philadelphia for A.J. Brown back in June.

That’s why I don’t think bringing in Henry should be an automatic decision. The coaching staff has to decide whether the immediate impact is worth giving up a pick that could help this team for several seasons.

And there’s a big difference between trading for a player just because he’s available, and bringing someone in because he actually fills a need.

In Henry’s case, the fit makes sense. Henderson’s absence increases the need for depth in the backfield. Stevenson’s production on the ground still has room to improve. And Vrabel’s experience with Henry is a huge positive.

But that doesn’t mean the Patriots should pay any price. A third-round pick? I’d do it. Anything much more than that, and I’d start to think twice.

And we also have to look at the other side of this potential deal. Baltimore.

The Ravens are coming off a 33 to 10 loss to Jacksonville, and they’re now 4 and 5. They’ve lost four straight games, to Cleveland, Cincinnati, Buffalo, and Jacksonville. And their quarterback situation is a mess.

That’s fueled a lot of speculation about possible roster moves. But it doesn’t automatically mean the Ravens are willing to trade their starting running back.

And here’s the detail that could change everything. After the loss to Jacksonville, Henry said he hasn’t asked to be traded. And he said he intends to help turn things around in Baltimore.

That matters. Henry isn’t pushing his way out. He wants to help the Ravens fix this. So if a trade happens, it would have to be Baltimore’s decision, not his.

So let’s be clear. This is a reported possibility. Not a confirmed trade negotiation. AtoZ is the outlet that has tied the Patriots to Henry, and they’re citing their own sources. ESPN’s Jordan Raanan even wrote earlier in the week that the idea of Henry being available never seemed realistic to him, although that was before the Jacksonville loss.

The deadline is tomorrow at 4 p.m. Eastern. Until then, New England has to decide whether this is an opportunity worth chasing.

And there are other options too. The Patriots are reportedly one of four teams in the wide receiver market, alongside the Chiefs, the Steelers, and the 49ers, with Josh Downs, Drake Maye’s college teammate, on the table. Albert Breer has floated a possible reunion with pass rusher K’Lavon Chaisson. And there’s still Jonathan Taylor in Indianapolis, although the Colts reportedly turned down an offer of a second-round pick plus another pick.

So the front office has options. And with about 6.1 million dollars in cap space after restructuring Hunter Henry’s contract, they have some room to work.

But Derrick Henry might be the most interesting option of all. Because he’s cheap, he’s productive, and he already knows the head coach.

And when you put everything together, I think this conversation goes way beyond Derrick Henry.

Henderson’s situation has shown us how quickly a team’s plans can change. A player who was part of the team’s future now has to focus all of his energy on getting healthy. Stevenson still has an important role. But this offense needs more consistency on the ground. And Henry represents a chance to raise the level of that unit right away.

The one thing I don’t want to see is the Patriots making a rushed decision just because Henderson is out. His recovery needs to happen on his own timeline. And the team’s plans can’t depend on a return date that hasn’t even been set.

At the same time, if there’s a real chance to strengthen this offense and give Drake Maye more support, I think it’s only right for the front office to seriously consider it.

So let’s put it all together.

The concern: TreVeyon Henderson, out since October 29th, dealing with an unexplained neurological condition that required several days in the hospital. His health comes first, and we’re all wishing him a full recovery.

The need: a running game led by Rhamondre Stevenson, with 367 yards and two touchdowns through eight games, at 3.8 yards per carry.

The opportunity: Derrick Henry, with 689 yards and eight touchdowns in nine games, seventh on the all-time rushing list, and six seasons with Mike Vrabel in Tennessee.

The price: likely a third-round pick, with only about 600,000 dollars left on his salary this season.

And the catch: Henry says he hasn’t asked for a trade and wants to help turn things around in Baltimore. And the only reported link so far comes from AtoZ Sports.
